§1 Abstract

Between March 2022 and August 2025, a single adult subject (hereafter Ro) was photographed on eight separate occasions in a state of unscheduled daytime sleep. Observations were opportunistic rather than systematic: the investigator recorded events as they arose, which was often, and stopped recording only because eight seemed like enough.

No stimulus was applied. No subject was repositioned. In none of the eight observations did the subject wake, and in none of them did she appear to be trying to sleep. The survey therefore documents not rest but onset: the moment at which an ordinary afternoon activity is abandoned mid-motion and quietly not resumed.

In twenty-five seconds the investigator obtained three distinct camera positions and four exposures, requiring movement around two sides of the bed and one change of elevation. Across the same interval the subject produced no measurable change in posture, grip, or respiration. The series is included here because it is the clearest demonstration in the survey of the central finding: that the thing being documented is not fragile, and cannot be disturbed by documenting it.

§4 Findings

  1. Onset is confined to daylight hours.

    All eight recorded onsets fall between 12:44 and 16:55. Not one observation was made at night. Whatever this behaviour is, it is not sleep deprivation; it is a parallel schedule running underneath the ordinary one.

  2. Held objects are not released.

    In six of eight observations the subject fell asleep while holding something: a telephone (four) or a hardback book (two). In no case was the object dropped. Grip appears entirely unaffected by loss of consciousness. Of the two exceptions, ST-004 involved no object at all, and ST-001 involved a device that had been set down deliberately beforehand, which may be the single most premeditated act in the survey.

  3. Substrate is irrelevant.

    Recorded surfaces: bed (four), sofa (two), wicker chair (two). Two observations occurred outdoors, in direct sun, in shoes. The subject demonstrates no substrate preference and requires neither horizontality nor shade.

  4. The behaviour is stable across years.

    Observations span three years and four months with no detectable change in onset speed, posture variety, or grip retention. This is not a phase. It is a constitution.

  5. The subject cannot be woken by observation.

    Eight photographic sessions, one of them conducted from three angles at a range of under one metre. Wakings: zero.

§5 Methodology & Notes on the Record

Capture

All images were taken handheld on consumer mobile devices under available light. No flash was used at any point, for reasons that should be obvious. No subject was repositioned, arranged, prompted, or otherwise interfered with. Everything here is as it was found.

Preparation of the record

Prior to publication, every file was stripped of its embedded metadata (EXIF, GPS block, device make and model, firmware identifiers, subsecond timestamps and unique image IDs) and re-encoded from raw pixel data so that nothing could survive in the container. Orientation flags were resolved into the pixels themselves rather than left as instructions. Recorded GPS coordinates were null in all eight files; no location was ever written. The subject cannot be located by way of this document. She can only be admired.

The capture timestamps reproduced throughout §2 were transcribed by hand before stripping, on the grounds that they are the most interesting part of the data and it would have been cowardly to lose them.

Consent

The subject has reviewed this survey in full and consented to its publication. She wishes it noted, for the record, that she was resting her eyes.
