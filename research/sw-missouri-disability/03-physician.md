## 1. Program and resource cards

### 1.1 Evidence for DMH Division of DD eligibility (the clinical part)
- Run by: Missouri Department of Mental Health (DMH), Division of Developmental Disabilities (DD), through the Springfield Regional Office or Joplin Satellite Office.
- Serves (ages, disability types): all ages. People with an intellectual developmental disorder (IDD) or a developmental disability (DD) as defined in state law. This includes cerebral palsy, epilepsy, head injury, autism, a learning disability related to a brain dysfunction, or any other cognitive or physical impairment that meets the functional test.
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year):
  - Statute (RSMo 630.005, text effective 28 Aug 2018): a developmental disability must (a) be attributable to one of the listed conditions or any other mental or physical impairment, (b) be manifested before age 22, (c) be likely to continue indefinitely, (d) cause substantial functional limitations in 2 or more of 6 areas (self-care; receptive and expressive language; learning; self-direction; capacity for independent living or economic self-sufficiency; mobility), and (e) reflect a need for lifelong or extended, individually planned and coordinated services.
  - Intellectual disability in statute: significantly subaverage general intellectual functioning that originates before age 18 and is associated with a significant impairment in adaptive behavior.
  - Regulation (9 CSR 45-2.010, amended effective 30 Apr 2023): "Intellectual developmental disorder" means intellectual functioning "at or below two (2) standard deviations below the mean, including a margin for measurement error when appropriate, as measured by an individually administered, comprehensive, and psychometrically sound test of intelligence", with onset before age 18, and significant impairment in adaptive behavior "assessed by both clinical evaluation and culturally appropriate, psychometrically sound measures."
  - "Substantial" means at least 2 standard deviations below the mean on a standardized, norm referenced measure, taking the standard error of measure into account.
  - Functional test tool: DMH uses its own Missouri Adaptive Abilities Scale (MAAS) for everyone age 5 and older. Ages 5–17: MAAS score 1.5 SD below the mean in at least 2 areas, or 2 SD below the mean in 1 area. Adults 18 and older: the comprehensive evaluation, including MAAS results, must show deficits in 2 or more of the 6 areas.
  - The rule says DMH intends to use "functional assessment" and to "discontinue the practice of linking eligibility to a specific diagnosis."
  - If a person claims eligibility because of intellectual developmental disorder but MAAS does not show limits in 2 areas, the team "shall consider any additional assessments or other relevant information provided by the applicant." Adaptive behavior assessment must use one or more tools "currently defined by the American Association on Intellectual and Developmental Disabilities."
- Prerequisites (what the family must have first): a referral to the regional office (anyone can refer) and a signed application. DMH does not require MO HealthNet for eligibility itself.
- Documents and evidence needed:
  - DMH intake collects: legal name, date of birth, SSN, addresses, MO HealthNet status, the diagnosed IDD or DD, the "diagnosing physician and/or entity", special communication needs, and responsible person details. The packet includes an Authorization for Disclosure of health information (release of information). Guardianship or custody papers must come with the application if they exist.
  - The rule says the regional office accepts, and does not duplicate, assessment information from other private and public bodies, "including schools, if regional offices determine that information to be reliable and appropriate."
  - Clinically useful records (my list, based on the rule's evidence types): the most recent full intellectual evaluation report (with test name, full scale score, index scores, and examiner credentials); any adaptive behavior scale report; school evaluation reports and IEPs; diagnostic reports for autism, cerebral palsy, epilepsy, or brain injury; a history that shows onset before 22 (for DD) or before 18 (for IDD).
- How old a test may be: the rule sets **no maximum age** for a prior test. It says the team uses records it finds "reliable and appropriate." I could not find a DMH written rule on test age. See Open questions.
- Who may give the tests: the rule does not name a license for outside testers. It says the assessment team is "Professionals employed by the Division of Developmental Disabilities with specialized training and experience." DMH staff give the MAAS.
- How to apply in SW Missouri: referral online ([DDD Online Referral](https://redcapdd.azurewebsites.net/redcap/surveys/?s=L4T7L78HEKTL9NPP)), by phone, fax, email, mail, or in person to the Springfield Regional Office (417-895-7400) or Joplin Satellite Office (417-629-3020). DMH information line: 1-800-241-2741, Information.Specialist@dmh.mo.gov.
- Typical time or wait: by rule, intake contact within 1 business day of referral; comprehensive evaluation within 30 business days after a valid application "and sufficient supporting medical, psychological, and/or educational reports"; plus up to 30 more business days under a temporary action plan if collateral records have not arrived. Written eligibility notice within 3 business days of the decision. Waiver funding wait is a separate issue (agent 06 and 08).
- Cost to family: DMH does the eligibility evaluation and MAAS at no cost (no fee is named in the rule or on the DMH page). Private outside testing costs money; see card 1.3.
- Where families get stuck or denied (clinical side):
  - The 30 business day clock starts only after "sufficient supporting" reports arrive. Slow records from schools and clinics delay the decision.
  - Adults who left school years ago may have no copy of their last school psychological report. Schools may have destroyed old special education records.
  - Adults with a physical or "other" condition (not IDD) must show onset before 22 with records. Adults over 22 with no childhood records have a hard time.
  - People with autism and an average IQ must show the functional limits on the MAAS; the diagnosis alone does not decide eligibility.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: the rule points to 9 CSR 45-2.020. Detail belongs to agent 04 (legal) and agent 01.
- Screener questions: Does the person have a condition that started before age 22? Is it expected to last indefinitely? Does the person need a lot of help in at least 2 of: self-care, language, learning, self-direction, living on their own or earning a living, mobility? Is there a diagnosis of intellectual disability, autism, cerebral palsy, epilepsy, or brain injury? Is there a written test report or school evaluation?
- Screener rule: (condition began before age 22) AND (likely lifelong) AND (needs substantial help in 2 or more of the 6 areas) THEN "may qualify; refer to DMH regional office." A missing test report is NOT a reason to skip the referral; DMH does its own MAAS.
- Sources: [RSMo 630.005](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=630.005), checked 2026-10-26; [9 CSR 45-2 (current, 3/31/23 printing)](https://www.sos.mo.gov/cmsimages/adrules/csr/current/9csr/9c45-2.pdf), checked 2026-10-26; [DMH Individual, Family, Guardian Info](https://dmh.mo.gov/dev-disabilities/individual-family-guardian-info), checked 2026-10-26; [DMH Springfield Regional Office](https://dmh.mo.gov/dev-disabilities/regional-offices/springfield), checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High for the definitions, timelines, and office contacts. Low for any rule on how old a prior test may be (not found).

### 1.2 Evidence for SSI and SSDI (including Childhood Disability Benefits) disability decisions
- Run by: Social Security Administration (SSA). Medical decisions are made by the state Disability Determinations office for SSA. Benefit rules belong to agent 06.
- Serves: children and adults. This card covers the adult rules that apply at 18 and older.
- Eligibility rules (medical part only; listings in 20 CFR Part 404, Subpart P, Appendix 1, eCFR version current 2026-10-01):
  - 12.05 Intellectual disorder, A or B:
    - A: inability to take part in standardized IQ testing, AND dependence on others for personal needs (toileting, eating, dressing, bathing), AND evidence that the disorder began before age 22.
    - B: full scale (or comparable) IQ of 70 or below; OR full scale 71–75 with a verbal or performance IQ (or comparable part score) of 70 or below, on an individually administered standardized test; AND extreme limitation of 1, or marked limitation of 2, of the 4 areas of mental functioning (understand, remember, or apply information; interact with others; concentrate, persist, or maintain pace; adapt or manage oneself); AND evidence that the disorder began before age 22.
  - 12.10 Autism spectrum disorder: medical documentation of (1) qualitative deficits in verbal communication, nonverbal communication, and social interaction, and (2) significantly restricted, repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or activities; AND extreme limitation of 1, or marked limitation of 2, of the 4 areas of mental functioning.
  - 11.07 Cerebral palsy, A, B, or C: (A) disorganization of motor function in 2 extremities causing extreme limitation in standing up from a seated position, balance while standing or walking, or use of the upper extremities; or (B) marked limitation in physical functioning and in 1 area of mental functioning; or (C) "Significant interference in communication due to speech, hearing, or visual deficit."
  - 11.02 Epilepsy: needs "a detailed description of a typical seizure" and seizure frequency over a set number of consecutive months "despite adherence to prescribed treatment." Example: generalized tonic clonic seizures at least once a month for at least 3 consecutive months (11.02A); dyscognitive seizures at least once a week for at least 3 consecutive months (11.02B). 11.02C and D use lower frequencies plus a marked limitation.
  - 2.02 Vision: remaining vision in the better eye after best correction 20/200 or less. 2.03: visual field in the better eye no wider than 20 degrees, or other field measures. 2.04: visual efficiency 20 percent or less, or visual impairment value 1.00 or greater.
  - 2.10 Hearing (no cochlear implant): average air conduction threshold 90 dB or greater AND average bone conduction 60 dB or greater in the better ear; OR word recognition 40 percent or less in the better ear. 2.11 (cochlear implant): disabled for 1 year after first implant; after that, HINT word recognition 60 percent or less.
  - 2.09: loss of speech, "inability to produce by any means speech that can be heard, understood, or sustained."
  - 1.00 Musculoskeletal (several listings, for example 1.15, 1.16, 1.18): many require a "documented medical need" for a walker, bilateral canes or crutches, or a wheeled and seated mobility device. "Documented medical need" means evidence from a medical source that supports the need for the device "for a continuous period of at least 12 months." The evidence "must describe any limitation(s) in your upper or lower extremity functioning and the circumstances for which you need to use the assistive device." SSA does not require a prescription.
  - 3.00 Respiratory and 4.00 Cardiovascular: relevant for medically complex adults (for example, 3.04 cystic fibrosis, 3.14 respiratory failure). Evidence is test based (spirometry, blood gas, imaging). I did not extract full criteria; see Open questions.
- Who may give the evidence ("acceptable medical source", 20 CFR 416.902(a)): licensed physician (MD or DO); licensed psychologist at the independent practice level; licensed or certified school psychologist "for impairments of intellectual disability, learning disabilities, and borderline intellectual functioning only"; licensed optometrist (vision only); licensed podiatrist (foot or ankle only); qualified speech language pathologist (speech or language only); licensed audiologist (hearing loss, auditory processing, balance; claims filed on or after 27 Mar 2017); licensed APRN and licensed physician assistant within scope (claims filed on or after 27 Mar 2017).
- Testing rules that matter:
  - IQ testing for 12.05B must be given by a "qualified specialist": "currently licensed or certified at the independent level of practice in the State where the test was performed." If a psychological assistant gives the test, a qualified specialist must interpret and co-sign. The test must have a mean of 100 and SD of 15 and meet "contemporary psychometric standards."
  - SSA "do[es] not require the results of an individually administered standardized test of adaptive functioning" (12.00H3c).
  - Onset before 22: SSA does not need records made before 22. It accepts current testing plus history. Examples it lists: tests, special education records, an IEP "including your transition plan", school performance reports, medical records, employer reports, statements from a group home or sheltered workshop supervisor, and statements from people who knew the person (12.00H4).
  - Hearing: SSA "generally require[s] both an otologic examination and audiometric testing", with audiometry "within 2 months of the otologic examination." The otologic exam must be by a licensed physician or audiologist. Hearing aids are off during testing. Audiometry must be by, or under the supervision of, a licensed audiologist or otolaryngologist.
  - Vision: SSA needs an eye exam report with best corrected central acuity or visual fields, and documentation of the cause of the loss. For SSI statutory blindness, SSA needs only acuity or field measures and no duration (2.00A3).
  - Supports count: SSA looks at help from family, special education, supported work, and structured settings (12.00D). A person who does well only because of heavy support may still meet a listing.
- Prerequisites: an SSA application (agent 06). SSA can buy a consultative examination (CE) when records are not enough.
- Documents and evidence needed from clinicians (12.00C2 list): symptoms, history, exam findings, rating scales, measures of adaptive functioning, psychological testing, imaging and lab results, diagnosis, medications and effects, therapy and effects, side effects, clinical course, observations of function during exams, sensory or speech issues that affect testing, and the expected duration. SSA also asks for longitudinal records over months or years (12.00C5).
- What SSA ignores: statements that a person "is disabled", "unable to work", "meets a listing", or a residual functional capacity stated only as an exertional level (sedentary, light, and so on) are "inherently neither valuable nor persuasive" (20 CFR 416.920b(c)). SSA weighs a medical opinion mostly on "supportability" and "consistency" (20 CFR 416.920c).
- How to apply in SW Missouri: agent 06.
- Typical time or wait: agent 06.
- Cost to family: SSA pays for any consultative exam it orders. Records fees from clinics may apply.
- Where families get stuck or denied (clinical side):
  - Only old childhood IQ scores in the file, or a school report from a non licensed examiner. A licensed or certified school psychologist is an acceptable source for ID; other school staff are not.
  - Letters that say "disabled" or "cannot work" without functional detail.
  - Seizure logs missing, or no record of treatment adherence (11.02 needs both).
  - Hearing tests done with aids on, or no otologic exam within 2 months.
  - No record of the 12 month device need for a walker or wheelchair.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: agent 04 and agent 06.
- Screener questions: Does the person have a full scale IQ score of 75 or below on record? Does a doctor or psychologist have records of the condition over time? Does the person use a walker, two canes or crutches, or a wheelchair because of a lasting condition? Better eye 20/200 or worse with glasses? Profound hearing loss or a cochlear implant in the past year? Seizures at least monthly despite medicine?
- Screener rule: any of these is a flag to "gather medical evidence now", not a decision. SSA decides. Example: (IQ on record <= 75) AND (records of help with daily life) THEN "ask a licensed psychologist or treating clinician for a functional statement."
- Sources: [eCFR Appendix 1 to Subpart P of Part 404 (Listing of Impairments)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-III/part-404/subpart-P/appendix-Appendix%201%20to%20Subpart%20P%20of%20Part%20404), text via eCFR API version 2026-10-01, checked 2026-10-26; [20 CFR 416.902](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-III/part-416/subpart-A/section-416.902), [20 CFR 416.913](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-III/part-416/subpart-I/section-416.913), [20 CFR 416.920b](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-III/part-416/subpart-I/section-416.920b), [20 CFR 416.920c](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-III/part-416/subpart-I/section-416.920c), checked 2026-10-26. The SSA Blue Book web pages (ssa.gov) blocked automated access today; the eCFR text is the binding version of the same listings.
- Confidence: High for the listing text and source rules. Medium for 3.00 and 4.00 (not extracted).

### 1.3 Intellectual (IQ) and psychological testing for transition age youth and adults
- Run by: school psychologists (while the student is in school), licensed psychologists in private practice or clinics, university training clinics, and SSA consultative examiners (paid by SSA).
- Serves: anyone who needs a current test report for DMH DD eligibility, SSA, VR, college, or guardianship evidence (guardianship evidence: agent 04).
- Eligibility rules (who may test, by program):
  - SSA 12.05B: a "qualified specialist", licensed or certified at the independent level in the state where testing occurred. A licensed or certified school psychologist is an acceptable medical source for intellectual disability, learning disability, and borderline intellectual functioning (20 CFR 416.902(a)(2)(ii)).
  - DMH: "an individually administered, comprehensive, and psychometrically sound test of intelligence" (9 CSR 45-2.010(2)(N)). The rule names no license for outside testers.
  - MO HealthNet payment (Behavioral Health Services Manual, file dated 2026/05): psychologists, provisional licensed psychologists, qualified doctoral level psychology interns, and psychiatrists may bill testing. Testing is covered for children and adults. Limit: "a maximum of four (4) hours per participant, per performing provider, per rolling year." A behavioral health diagnosis "is not required for this service."
- Prerequisites: a referral or self referral; for MO HealthNet payment, a provider enrolled with MO HealthNet.
- No cost routes (by rule):
  - School reevaluation under IDEA "at least once every 3 years, unless the parent and the public agency agree that a reevaluation is unnecessary" (34 CFR 300.303(b)(2)).
  - A school does not have to evaluate before eligibility ends because of graduation with a regular diploma or aging out. It must give a summary of academic achievement and functional performance (Summary of Performance) instead (34 CFR 300.305(e)(2)–(3)).
  - Parents may ask for an independent educational evaluation at public expense when they disagree with the school evaluation (34 CFR 300.502(b)). A private evaluation the parent shares "must be considered" by the school if it meets agency criteria (34 CFR 300.502(c)). Legal detail: agent 04 and 07.
  - SSA may buy a consultative examination when records are not enough (12.00C5c).
  - DMH does its own MAAS adaptive scale at no cost (card 1.1).
- Who can provide it in SW Missouri (verified pages only):
  - Missouri State University, McQueary College Collaborative Care Clinic, Learning Diagnostic Services: serves "students in grades K-12 through college, as well as adults." Lists autism spectrum evaluations, ADHD evaluations, learning disabilities, "cognitive assessments", academic achievement, and other areas. Intellectual disability is not named. Services "are open to the community." Fees "vary by service." Clinic phone 417-836-5275, CollabCare@MissouriState.edu, Ann Kampeter Health Sciences Hall, 901 S. National Ave., Springfield, MO 65897. MO HealthNet acceptance: not stated.
  - Burrell Autism Center: lists "Multidisciplinary Diagnostic Assessments" for "Ages 1 through 8" (psychology, OT, speech, and a developmental pediatrician consult). "We accept Medicaid, private insurance and self-pay clients." 1300 E Bradford Pkwy, Building B, Springfield, MO 65804; 417-761-5330. No adult autism diagnostic service is listed.
  - Private licensed psychologists: not catalogued. See Open questions.
- Typical time or wait: a search snippet of an older MSU Learning Diagnostic Clinic page said testing takes "six-to-eight hours" in several sessions and the wait was "approximately 6-8 months." I could not open that page today. Treat as Low confidence.
- Cost to family: MO HealthNet pays an enrolled provider up to the 4 hour cap. Private pay amounts not verified.
- Where families get stuck:
  - The MO HealthNet 4 hour cap per provider per year may not cover a full intellectual test plus an adaptive measure plus a report. (Inference from the cap and the MSU time estimate; not a stated policy problem.)
  - No adult autism diagnostic clinic was found in Springfield in this search. Burrell lists ages 1 to 8 only.
  - The last school evaluation before exit is often a review of existing data, so the newest IQ score may be from early childhood.
  - Families do not know that a school psychologist report is acceptable to SSA for intellectual disability.
- Appeal rights: not applicable (agent 04 for IEE disputes).
- Screener questions: Is there a full IQ test report from the last 3 years? Who signed it (licensed or school psychologist)? Is the student still in school with an IEP? Does the person have MO HealthNet?
- Screener rule: (in school with IEP AND age >= 16) THEN "ask the IEP team for full cognitive and adaptive reevaluation before exit." (no current report AND has MO HealthNet) THEN "ask for testing by an enrolled psychologist; note the 4 hour per year cap."
- Sources: [MO HealthNet Behavioral Health Services Manual (docx, 2026/05)](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/05/Behavioral%20Health%20Services%20Manual.docx), checked 2026-10-26; [34 CFR 300.303](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/subtitle-B/chapter-III/part-300/subpart-D/subject-group-ECFR4ba3ecf4c40d6e3/section-300.303), [34 CFR 300.305](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/subtitle-B/chapter-III/part-300/subpart-D/subject-group-ECFR4ba3ecf4c40d6e3/section-300.305), [34 CFR 300.502](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/subtitle-B/chapter-III/part-300/subpart-E/section-300.502), eCFR API version 2026-10-01, checked 2026-10-26; [MSU Collaborative Care Clinic, Learning Diagnostic](https://mchhs.missouristate.edu/collabcare/learning.htm) and [FAQ](https://mchhs.missouristate.edu/collabcare/faq.htm), checked 2026-10-26; [Burrell Autism Services](https://www.burrellcenter.com/our-services/autism-services), checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High for rules and the listed pages. Low for wait times and costs.

### 1.4 Adaptive behavior evidence
- Run by: DMH (MAAS), psychologists, schools.
- Rules:
  - DMH: the Missouri Adaptive Abilities Scale (MAAS) is required for age 5 and older. If MAAS does not show limits but the person claims intellectual developmental disorder, the team must use "One (1) or more standardized testing tools currently defined by the American Association on Intellectual and Developmental Disabilities." The rule names the Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scales only for ages 0–4.
  - DD waivers (ICF/IID level of care): uses the federal "related condition" definition, which needs substantial functional limits in **3 or more** areas, confirmed by the MAAS (DD Waiver Manual, file dated 2026/09). DMH eligibility needs only 2. A person can be DMH eligible but not meet waiver level of care.
  - SSA: does not require a standardized adaptive test (12.00H3c). It uses all evidence, including family, school, and employer reports, and asks about "the kind, extent, and frequency of help" (12.00H3d).
- Where families get stuck: parents describe the person's best day; assessors need typical function and how much help is given. Strengths in one area do not cancel deficits in another (SSA 12.00H3d(i)).
- Screener rule: count the areas (self-care, language, learning, mobility, self-direction, independent living or economic self-sufficiency) where the person needs substantial help. 2 or more: refer to DMH. 3 or more: likely relevant to DD waiver level of care (DMH decides).
- Sources: [9 CSR 45-2](https://www.sos.mo.gov/cmsimages/adrules/csr/current/9csr/9c45-2.pdf); [MO HealthNet DD Waiver Manual (docx, 2026/09)](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/09/DD-Waiver-Manual.docx); [eCFR Listing 12.00H](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-III/part-404/subpart-P/appendix-Appendix%201%20to%20Subpart%20P%20of%20Part%20404); all checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High.

### 1.5 Hearing evidence and hearing aids (audiology and ENT)
- Run by: audiologists and ENT physicians; MO HealthNet Hearing Aid Program; SSA for disability evidence.
- Serves: Deaf and hard of hearing people of all ages.
- Eligibility rules (MO HealthNet Hearing Aid Provider Manual, file dated 2026/04):
  - All hearing aids and dispensing fees need prior authorization.
  - Pure tone average at 500, 1000, and 2000 Hz must be "30 decibels of Hearing Level (dB HL) or greater in the better ear."
  - Word recognition testing with "a minimum of 25 phonetically balanced word lists" is required at age 7 and older. The manual says that if "a participant's cognitive impairment precludes word recognition testing, this must be stated in Section G of the RHAE."
  - A "medical ear examination" by an MD or DO must happen before the prior authorization request.
  - One new hearing aid and related services "once every four (4) years." Repairs and post fitting adjustments: 3 per rolling calendar year.
  - Age 21 change: binaural (two) hearing aids "for participants age 21 and older is a covered service only in cases of blindness." Under 21, HCY adds binaural aids and hearing assistive technology (FM or Bluetooth systems).
  - SSA hearing evidence: card 1.2 (otologic exam plus audiometry within 2 months, aids off, by or under a licensed audiologist or otolaryngologist).
- Who can provide it in SW Missouri:
  - Mercy Clinic Audiology, 1229 E. Seminole Street, Suite 530, Springfield, MO 65804; 417-820-5071. Lists comprehensive hearing evaluations for children and adults, hearing aid fitting, a cochlear implant center for children and adults, and hearing and balance tests. Insurance not stated on the page.
  - CoxHealth Audiology, 1001 East Primrose Street, Floor 3, Springfield, MO 65807; 417-875-3600. Works with the ENT team and offers "cost-effective hearing aid options." A 2020 CoxHealth news item said cochlear implants were offered for adults only at that time (Medium; may be old). Insurance not stated.
  - MSU Collaborative Care Clinic Audiology: "Hearing evaluations" and "Hearing aid fitting and dispensing" for "all ages." Fees vary.
- Where families get stuck: one aid only for adults unless blind; 4 year replacement cycle; the medical ear exam is a separate visit; people who cannot do word recognition tests need the clinician to document why.
- Screener questions: Age? Hearing loss in both ears? Also blind or low vision? Has MO HealthNet?
- Screener rule: (age >= 21 AND MO HealthNet AND not blind) THEN "MO HealthNet covers one aid every 4 years; plan the binaural request before 21 if clinically indicated."
- Sources: [MO HealthNet Hearing Aid Provider Manual (docx, 2026/04)](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/04/Hearing-Aid-Provider-Manual.docx); [Mercy Clinic Audiology E. Seminole](https://mercy.net/practice/mercy-clinic-audiology-e-seminole); [CoxHealth Audiology](https://coxhealth.com/our-locations/coxhealth-audiology); [CoxHealth cochlear implant news (2020)](https://www.coxhealth.com/newsroom/coxhealth-begins-offering-cochlear-implants/); [MSU Audiology](https://mchhs.missouristate.edu/collabcare/audiology.htm); all checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High for manual rules; Medium for local services (pages do not state MO HealthNet acceptance).
- Overlap: Deaf services, interpreters, and VR are agents 04, 07, and 08.

### 1.6 Vision evidence, eye care, and low vision
- Run by: optometrists and ophthalmologists; MO HealthNet Optical Program; SSA.
- Rules (MO HealthNet Optical Provider Manual, file dated 2026/04):
  - Eye exam every 12 months for age 20 and under, or for people in the blind, pregnant women, or nursing facility (including ICF/IDD) categories. Age 21 and over: "every 24 months."
  - One pair of complete eyeglasses every 2 years. For age 21 and over, frames are not replaced in the 24 month period, and a new exam to replace lost or broken glasses is not covered in that period. A 0.50 diopter change allows new lenses.
  - "Contact lens(es) with corrective power for visual improvement are not covered for participants age 21 and over" (medical purpose contacts are a separate rule).
  - "Magnifiers" are listed as non-covered.
  - SSA vision evidence: card 1.2 (best corrected acuity or fields, plus the cause of loss). A licensed optometrist is an acceptable medical source for vision.
- Low vision exams and aids: MO HealthNet optical does not cover magnifiers. Low vision rehabilitation and aids go through Rehabilitation Services for the Blind (agent 07). Local low vision optometry: only directory leads found (Pierce Vision Specialists; Vision Rehabilitation Center of the Ozarks, registry record last updated 2011). Not verified. Low confidence.
- Where families get stuck: adults with IDD who break glasses wait up to 2 years for MO HealthNet replacement; no MO HealthNet route for magnifiers.
- Screener rule: (age >= 21 AND MO HealthNet AND not in blind category) THEN "exam every 24 months; glasses every 2 years; no replacement for breakage in that period."
- Sources: [MO HealthNet Optical Provider Manual (docx, 2026/04)](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/04/Optical-Manual_0.docx), checked 2026-10-26; [eCFR 2.00 Special Senses](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-III/part-404/subpart-P/appendix-Appendix%201%20to%20Subpart%20P%20of%20Part%20404), checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High for manual rules. Low for local low vision providers.

### 1.7 MO HealthNet durable medical equipment (DME): orders, CMN, prior authorization
- Run by: MO HealthNet Division (MHD). Managed care plans have their own processes (not reviewed here).
- Serves: all MO HealthNet participants. Some items are limited to age 20 and under (card 1.8).
- Rules (MO HealthNet DME Manual, dated 07/06/2026):
  - Items "must be ordered in writing by the participant's physician, advanced practice nurse, or nurse practitioner." "A written prescription is required."
  - Who may prescribe: physicians (MD, DO, podiatrists) and advanced practice nurses with a collaborative practice agreement that allows it. For pre-certification items, speech pathologists may prescribe augmentative communication devices (ACDs) only.
  - The ordering, prescribing, or referring (OPR) provider's NPI must be on the claim. "The OPR must be actively enrolled with MHD even if the provider does not accept MO HealthNet participants." If not, "the claim will be denied."
  - Certificate of Medical Necessity (CMN) must show: procedure codes, modifiers, item description (brand and model), accessories, "Reason why the equipment or items are needed", "Anticipated length of need", date prescribed, diagnosis, and prognosis. Approved CMN period: 6 months from the prescription date, 12 months for wheelchair codes K0001, K0002, K0003, K0006.
  - Prior authorization (PA) request must include diagnosis, prognosis, "Detailed explanation from the prescribing physician and/or therapist", length of need, "Projected outcome", and invoice. "Listing only a diagnosis code and description does not provide sufficient information." The prescriber must sign. PA expires 3 months after approval unless stated.
  - Custom or power wheelchair PA justification must cover 8 points: diagnoses and conditions; history of limitations; physical and cognitive ability to use the equipment; past interventions and devices; existing equipment and why it fails; "why a less costly mobility device is unable to meet participant needs"; justification of each component; ability to use the equipment safely. Any PT, OT, or wheelchair clinic evaluation must be included, and the chair must fit the home.
  - Since 1 Aug 2025, DME providers that supply complex rehab technology must employ a RESNA certified Assistive Technology Professional (ATP).
  - Face to face encounter: required for DME items that need one under Medicare (42 CFR 440.70(g)(1)).
  - Nursing facility residents: extra rules (physician face to face exam before any supplier contact; order within 90 days).
  - Bulletin dated 09/23/2026: new "Power Wheelchair Seat Elevation Medical Criteria." Not read in detail.
- Who can provide it in SW Missouri: any MO HealthNet enrolled DME supplier; prescriber must be MHD enrolled. Wheelchair seating evaluations by PT or OT (local seating clinics not verified).
- Typical time: not published. PA validity 3 months.
- Cost to family: none for covered items when rules are followed (participant non-liability rule in manuals).
- Where families get stuck: order signed by a clinician not enrolled with MHD; letters that give only a diagnosis; no record of why cheaper devices fail; replacement within limits needs a stated reason (lost, stolen with police report, or condition change).
- Appeal rights: agent 04.
- Screener rule: (needs equipment AND MO HealthNet) THEN "check that the prescribing clinician is enrolled with MO HealthNet; ask for a functional justification letter."
- Sources: [MO HealthNet DME Manual (docx, 07/06/2026)](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/08/Durable-Medical-Equipment-Manual-07-06-2026.docx); [MO HealthNet DME page](https://dss.mo.gov/mhd/dme); both checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High.

### 1.8 The age 21 cliff in MO HealthNet medical services (HCY/EPSDT ends)
- Run by: MO HealthNet Division. HCY is Missouri's EPSDT program for participants under 21.
- Rule: under HCY, "A medically necessary item or service that is normally non-covered ... may be covered for participants ages 20 and under" (DME Manual 2.20). At 21 this ends. Specific changes found in current manuals:

| Service | Under 21 (HCY) | Age 21 and older | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outpatient PT, OT, speech therapy | Covered when medically necessary | "not covered for MO HealthNet participants ages 21 and over, except for PT in a hospital or physician's office and to adaptive training" for orthotic, prosthetic, or artificial larynx | Therapy Manual 2.15 |
| Habilitative therapy, adult expansion group (ME code E2) | All medically necessary | 20 visits per rolling year, all providers combined | Home Health Manual |
| Private duty nursing | Covered under HCY with BSHCN prior authorization | Not a state plan benefit; only via the Medically Complex (formerly Fragile) Adult Waiver (card 1.10) | PDN Manual |
| High frequency chest wall oscillation vest | "only covered for participants ages 20 and under" | Exceptions process only | DME Manual |
| Cough stimulating device | "covered for participants ages 20 and under" | Exceptions process only | DME Manual |
| Enteral nutrition formula | Covered "for children under the age of 21" | Exceptions process lists "Enteral Formula and Supplies" | DME Manual 2.22; Exceptions Manual |
| Pulse oximeter probes (purchased oximeter) | Set allowance for ages 20 and under | Not stated | DME Manual |
| Binaural hearing aids, FM or Bluetooth systems | Covered under HCY | Binaural only "in cases of blindness" | Hearing Aid Manual |
| Eye exam | Every 12 months | Every 24 months | Optical Manual |
| Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) for autism | Covered under 21 | Not listed for adults | Behavioral Health Manual |
| Home health | "expanded home health services" under HCY | Skilled nurse plus aide visits capped at 100 per calendar year (13 CSR 70-90.010) | Home Health Manual; PDN Manual |

- DD waiver route: the DD Waiver Manual lists occupational therapy, physical therapy, and speech therapy as waiver services (OT "requires a prescription by a physician"). For a DD waiver participant, therapy can continue after 21 through the waiver. Waiver access and wait list: agents 06 and 08.
- Where families get stuck: no one tells them before the birthday; devices and nursing stop or must be re-justified through a narrow exception.
- Screener rule: (age 19 or 20 AND MO HealthNet AND uses any service in the table) THEN "plan the age 21 change now; see section 7."
- Sources: [Therapy Provider Manual (docx, 2026/03)](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/03/Therapy-Provider-Manual.docx); [Home Health Provider Manual (docx, 2026/05)](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/05/Home%20Health%20Provider%20Manual.docx); [Private Duty Nursing Provider Manual (docx, 2026/04)](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/04/Private-Duty-Nursing-Provider-Manual.docx); [DME Manual (docx, 07/06/2026)](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/08/Durable-Medical-Equipment-Manual-07-06-2026.docx); [Exceptions Manual (docx, 2026/04)](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/04/Exceptions-Manual.docx); [Hearing Aid Manual](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/04/Hearing-Aid-Provider-Manual.docx); [Optical Manual](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/04/Optical-Manual_0.docx); [Behavioral Health Services Manual](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/05/Behavioral%20Health%20Services%20Manual.docx); [DD Waiver Manual (docx, 2026/09)](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/09/DD-Waiver-Manual.docx); all checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High (current manuals read today). Managed care plan rules may differ; not checked.

### 1.9 MO HealthNet Exceptions process (the adult route for non-covered items)
- Run by: MO HealthNet Division; the MHD director has final authority.
- Rules (Exceptions Manual, file dated 2026/04):
  - Only "a physician, physician assistant, or advanced practice nurse" may submit. The request must be "initiated by the treating prescriber" (13 CSR 70-2.100).
  - The covered alternatives must be "tried and found to be inappropriate or ineffective" first.
  - Approval must come before the item is provided (exceptions: retroactive eligibility; emergency with request within 1 state working day).
  - The request must show at least one of: the item is "required to sustain the participant's life"; it "would substantially improve the quality of life for a terminally ill patient"; it replaces an item lost to a natural disaster; or it is "necessary to prevent a higher level of care." It must be cost effective.
  - Third party resources must be exhausted.
  - Not allowed as exceptions (examples): expanded HCY services for under 21 (use HCY instead), personal care and respite, "Psychological testing or counseling not otherwise covered by MHD", MO HealthNet waiver services, and "Services exceeding the limits of the covered MHD services."
  - Specific forms exist for, among others, cough stimulation device, enteral formula and supplies, high frequency chest wall oscillation device, incontinence supplies, and air fluidized or low air loss surfaces.
- Where families get stuck: the bar (life sustaining or prevents a higher level of care) is high; the request must come from the prescriber, not the supplier or family.
- Screener rule: (age >= 21 AND item is HCY only AND item is life sustaining or prevents hospital or nursing facility care) THEN "ask the treating prescriber to file an exception request before the birthday or before the item runs out."
- Sources: [Exceptions Manual (docx, 2026/04)](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/04/Exceptions-Manual.docx), checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High.

### 1.10 Private duty nursing at 21: Medically Complex Adult Waiver (formerly Medically Fragile Adult Waiver)
- Run by: DHSS Bureau of Special Health Care Needs (BSHCN) with MO HealthNet. Agent 08 reports the DHSS program page now uses the name "Medically Complex Adult Waiver (MCAW)". The MO HealthNet provider manual (file dated 2026/03) still says "Medically Fragile Adult Waiver (MFAW)." Same program.
- Serves: age 21 and older with serious and complex medical needs who are "no longer eligible for home care services" under HCY.
- Eligibility rules (provider manual):
  - MO HealthNet eligible with a federally matched ME code.
  - "Require medical care equivalent to the level of care received in an Intermediate Care Facility for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities (ICF-IID)." The manual says "ICF-IID LOC requires the presence of developmental disabilities as defined in 42 CFR 435.1010."
  - "Cannot receive services through another waiver."
  - "Have previously required PDN services through the Healthy Children and Youth (HCY)/ Early Periodic Screening Diagnosis and Treatment (EPSDT) Program in the State of Missouri, or had a documented need for PDN services prior to the age of 21."
  - Agent 08's source says fee for service MO HealthNet only (not managed care).
- Services: private duty nursing, waiver attendant care, specialized medical supplies (agent 08 lists more).
- Medical evidence needed: a BSHCN RN service coordinator assesses level of care. The provider agency submits a physician signed plan of care (elements in section 6 below). PDN maximum "448 units (112 hours) per week", with short term exceptions and more in "unique circumstances such as post-hospitalization for a participant newly-requiring a ventilator." Authorizations last up to 6 months.
- Where families get stuck: a young adult with a medically complex condition that is not a "related condition" under 42 CFR 435.1010 may not meet ICF-IID level of care (my reading of the manual text; DHSS decides). A youth who never had HCY PDN and has no record of PDN need before 21 cannot enter.
- Screener rule: (age >= 21 AND had HCY PDN or documented PDN need before 21 AND MO HealthNet with federal match AND not in another waiver) THEN "refer to BSHCN regional office for MCAW." Phone (from manual grievance text): BSHCN (800) 451-0669.
- Sources: [MO HealthNet MFAW Provider Manual (docx, 2026/03)](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/03/Medically-Fragile-Adult-Waiver-Provider-Manual.docx); [MO HealthNet waiver page](https://dss.mo.gov/mhd/waiver/medically-fragile-adult); [Private Duty Nursing Provider Manual](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/04/Private-Duty-Nursing-Provider-Manual.docx); all checked 2026-10-26. DHSS program page (health.mo.gov) blocked automated access today; see agent 08.
- Confidence: High for manual text; Medium for current program name and fee for service rule.
- Overlap: waiver mechanics are agent 08.

### 1.11 MO HealthNet home health for adults (the medical order part)
- Run by: MO HealthNet; Medicare certified, state licensed home health agencies.
- Rules (Home Health Provider Manual, file dated 2026/05):
  - Services must be "prescribed by either a MO HealthNet-enrolled physician, nurse practitioner, clinical nurse specialist, or physician assistant."
  - Written plan of care "reviewed by the ordering practitioner at least every 60 days"; certification period no longer than 60 days.
  - Face to face encounter (42 CFR 440.70) "no more than 90 days before or 30 days after the start of home health services"; telehealth allowed as state law permits.
  - Skilled nurse plus home health aide visits combined may "not exceed 100 visits per calendar year per 13 CSR 70-90.010."
  - "Intermittent" skilled need: usually at least once every 60 days.
- Where families get stuck: home health is short term and skilled; it does not replace daily personal care (DHSS, agent 08) or private duty nursing.
- Sources: [Home Health Provider Manual (docx, 2026/05)](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/05/Home%20Health%20Provider%20Manual.docx), checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High.

### 1.12 Level of care evidence (DD waivers and DHSS home care): the clinician's part
- ICF/IID level of care for DD waivers: the DMH support coordinator completes the "Evaluation of Need for ICF/IID LOC" form using the MAAS plus "educational, psychological, and medical records." Federal related condition rule: onset before 22, likely indefinite, limits in 3 or more areas. Re-evaluated "at least once every 365 days." Clinicians supply diagnoses, history, and current function. No physician form is named in the manual.
- DHSS nursing facility level of care (used for HCBS and state plan personal care): DHSS staff assess. Personal care needs nursing facility level of care "such that the participant would require admission to a hospital or a long-term care facility if personal care services were not provided." The rule (19 CSR 30-81.030, 3/31/22 printing) scores categories such as cognition, mobility, and eating, and asks whether there is "a physician ordered therapeutic diet" and tube feeding. The rule sets 18 points for long term care facility admission on or after 15 Jul 2021. Agent 08 reports DSDS may send a "Healthcare Professional Inquiry" or General Health Evaluation form to the doctor. Detail: agent 08.
- What helps from the clinician: a current problem list, medication list, treatments and their frequency, physician ordered diets, cognitive findings, and falls or hospital use. (Elements drawn from the rule's scoring categories; not a published DHSS checklist.)
- Sources: [DD Waiver Manual (docx, 2026/09)](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/09/DD-Waiver-Manual.docx); [Personal Care Provider Manual (docx, 08/10/2026)](https://dss.mo.gov/sites/mydss/files/media/file/2026/08/Personal-Care-Provider-Manual-Published-08-10-2026_0.docx); [19 CSR 30-81](https://www.sos.mo.gov/cmsimages/adrules/csr/current/19csr/19c30-81a.pdf); all checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High for DD waiver text; Medium for DHSS (rule printing is 2022; current HCBS practice is agent 08).

### 1.14 Evidence for school, college, and Vocational Rehabilitation (VR) accommodations
- School (IEP or Section 504): the school must evaluate. Outside reports "must be considered" if they meet agency criteria (34 CFR 300.502(c)). At exit, the school gives a Summary of Performance with recommendations (34 CFR 300.305(e)(3)). Detail: agent 07.
- VR (Missouri Vocational Rehabilitation, Rehabilitation Services for the Blind): eligibility must be based on "existing data, including counselor observations, education records, information provided by the individual or the individual's family ... and determinations made by officials of other agencies" (34 CFR 361.42(d)). A person "determined eligible for Social Security benefits under title II or title XVI" is "Presumed eligible" and "Considered an individual with a significant disability" (34 CFR 361.42(a)(3)). Detail: agent 07.
- College disability services: AHEAD guidance (2012, professional association) says a student's own narrative "may be sufficient", and outside documentation such as medical records, school psychologist reports, IEPs, and Summaries of Performance can support it. Colleges set their own rules. Medium.
- Sources: [34 CFR 361.42](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/subtitle-B/chapter-III/part-361/subpart-B/section-361.42), eCFR API version 2026-10-01, checked 2026-10-26; [34 CFR 300.305 and 300.502](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/subtitle-B/chapter-III/part-300), checked 2026-10-26; [AHEAD documentation guidance](https://www.ahead.org/professional-resources/accommodations/documentation), search result only, checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High for federal rules; Medium for AHEAD.

## 2. Order and dependencies

1. A diagnosis and records of onset come first. DMH needs evidence that the condition was manifested before 22 (intellectual disability: before 18). SSA 12.05 needs evidence that supports onset before 22. Gather school and medical records while they still exist.
2. DMH DD eligibility (2 or more areas, MAAS) comes before any DD waiver. ICF/IID level of care (3 or more areas, federal related condition) comes before DD waiver enrollment. MO HealthNet is also needed for a waiver (agent 06).
3. The last school reevaluation (age 16–20) should come before exit. After exit, the school owes only a Summary of Performance. A full cognitive and adaptive reevaluation by a certified school psychologist feeds SSA, DMH, VR, and college evidence.
4. HCY private duty nursing (or a documented PDN need) must exist before 21 to enter the Medically Complex Adult Waiver at 21. ICF-IID level of care is also required.
5. A MO HealthNet enrolled prescriber comes before any DME claim. The order and CMN or PA come before delivery. A Medicare type face to face encounter comes before certain DME and all home health.
6. A medical ear exam by an MD or DO comes before a hearing aid prior authorization.
7. A trial of covered alternatives comes before a MO HealthNet exception request, and the request must be approved before the item is provided.
8. An SSI or SSDI award makes VR eligibility presumed (34 CFR 361.42(a)(3)).
9. Decision making supports should be planned "at least by age 17" and documented in the medical record before transfer to adult care (AAP 2018). Legal options: agent 04.

## 3. Gaps and failure points

1. **Age 21 cliff in MO HealthNet.** Outpatient PT, OT, and speech therapy stop for adults except narrow cases; private duty nursing becomes waiver only; chest wall oscillation vests, cough assist devices, and enteral formula move to the exception process; binaural hearing aids stop unless blind; ABA is listed for under 21 only. Evidence: current MO HealthNet manuals (card 1.8).
2. **Waiver entry rules can exclude medically complex adults.** MCAW requires ICF-IID level of care, which the manual ties to developmental disability under 42 CFR 435.1010, and prior HCY PDN or a documented need before 21. Young adults whose need starts after 21, or whose condition is not a "related condition", have no clear adult PDN route (my reading; card 1.10).
3. **Definition mismatch.** DMH eligibility needs 2 areas of substantial limitation; DD waiver level of care needs 3. A person can be eligible for DMH and still not reach a waiver (card 1.4).
4. **Testing capacity and payment.** MO HealthNet pays for psychological testing for adults, but only 4 hours per provider per rolling year. The one university clinic found reported (older, unverified page) a 6–8 month wait and 6–8 hours of testing. The only autism diagnostic clinic found (Burrell) lists ages 1 to 8. No adult autism diagnostic service was found in Springfield.
5. **Stale or missing childhood evidence.** IDEA lets parent and school skip the 3 year reevaluation and requires no evaluation before graduation or aging out. Many young adults leave school with only early childhood IQ scores. Neither DMH nor current SSA listings state an expiry for an old score; SSA presumes a score accurate unless evidence suggests otherwise (12.00H2d).
6. **Unenrolled prescribers.** A DME claim is denied if the ordering clinician is not enrolled with MO HealthNet, "even if the provider does not accept MO HealthNet participants." A specialist who does not take Medicaid can block equipment.
7. **Letters that do not work.** SSA treats statements that a person "is disabled" or "unable to work" as "inherently neither valuable nor persuasive." MO HealthNet says a diagnosis alone is "not ... sufficient" for a PA. Clinicians often write exactly these letters.
8. **Adult clinicians.** National data: 40.7 percent of physicians were very confident they could give equal quality care to patients with disability; 56.5 percent strongly agreed they welcomed them; some physicians tried to discharge them (Iezzoni 2021; Lagu 2022). The AAP 2018 report cites the 2016 National Survey of Children's Health: "83% of youth with SHCN ... do not meet the national HCT performance measure."
9. **Eye and hearing limits for adults.** Exams every 24 months, glasses every 2 years with no replacement for breakage, no magnifiers, one hearing aid every 4 years. Adults with IDD who lose or break devices go without.

## 4. Recommendations from your field

1. **Ask for a full exit reevaluation.** At the last IEP reevaluation (age 16–20), ask for an individually administered intelligence test and a standardized adaptive measure by a certified school psychologist, and a written report. This report is acceptable to SSA for intellectual disability and usable by DMH and VR. Who: family, advocate; school districts can make it routine.
2. **Build a transition evidence packet by age 17.** Last psychological report, IEP and Summary of Performance, medical summary and emergency plan (Got Transition tools), seizure log, device history with dates, audiogram, eye exam, and a list of all HCY services in use. Who: family with the pediatric practice and case manager.
3. **Use function based letters.** Use the element lists in section 6. Describe limits, frequency of help, device need over 12 months, and cheaper options tried. Do not write "disabled" or "cannot work." Who: clinicians; health systems can adopt templates.
4. **Plan the 21st birthday at 19.** List every service in the card 1.8 table that the person uses. For each: refer to MCAW (if PDN), refer to DMH for DD waiver therapy, ask the prescriber about an exception request, and complete clinically needed device replacements while HCY applies. Who: family, pediatric clinician, DMH support coordinator, BSHCN.
5. **Check prescriber enrollment.** Before any DME order, confirm the prescriber is enrolled with MO HealthNet as an ordering provider. Who: family, DME supplier, health systems (enroll specialists as ordering providers).
6. **Adopt the Six Core Elements.** CoxHealth, Mercy, Jordan Valley, and Burrell can adopt Got Transition's Six Core Elements, name adult clinicians who accept young adults with IDD or medical complexity, and use warm handoffs. Who: providers.
7. **County SB40 boards:** fund adult psychological and adaptive testing for DMH and SSA evidence when MO HealthNet limits or waits block it, and fund health transition care coordination. Who: county boards.
8. **State agencies:** MO HealthNet could raise or waive the 4 hour testing cap for intellectual disability evaluations; cover adult PT, OT, and speech for people with lifelong disabilities; and cover chest wall oscillation, cough assist, and enteral nutrition for adults with neuromuscular or swallowing conditions as standard benefits. DMH could publish written guidance on which prior tests it accepts and how old they may be. DHSS could publish an adult PDN path for people who do not meet ICF-IID level of care. Who: state agencies; the legislature for funding.
9. **Missouri State University clinics:** publish fees, MO HealthNet status, and adult intellectual disability evaluation capacity. Who: provider.

## 6. What a clinician letter must contain

These are required or requested elements taken from the rule or manual named. They are not clinical content. A clinician fills them from the record.

### 6.1 DMH Division of DD eligibility (9 CSR 45-2.010; RSMo 630.005)
- Diagnosis or condition, and the diagnosing clinician or entity (DMH intake asks for "Diagnosing physician and/or entity").
- Evidence the impairment is "demonstrable by medically acceptable clinical and laboratory diagnostic techniques."
- Age at onset (before 22; intellectual disability before 18) and how it is known.
- Statement that the condition is likely to continue indefinitely.
- Functional limits in each of the 6 areas (self-care; receptive and expressive language; learning; mobility; self-direction; capacity for independent living or economic self-sufficiency).
- For intellectual developmental disorder: name of the individually administered intelligence test, scores, and the margin for measurement error; adaptive behavior measure used.
- Need for lifelong or extended, individually planned and coordinated services.
- Copies of test reports, IEPs, and medical records, with a signed DMH release.

### 6.2 SSI, SSDI, or Childhood Disability Benefits (20 CFR 416.913, 416.920b, 416.920c; Listings 12.00C and H, 11.00, 2.00, 1.00)
- Objective medical evidence: signs, laboratory findings, imaging, psychological test results.
- Diagnosis, history, symptoms, treatment, response, side effects, clinical course, prognosis, expected duration.
- Medical opinion in terms of specific abilities: physical (sitting, standing, walking, lifting, reaching, handling), mental (understanding, remembering, concentration, persistence, pace, following instructions, responding to supervision, coworkers, work pressure), senses (seeing, hearing), and environment. Explain the support for each limit (supportability) and how it fits the rest of the record (consistency).
- Help and supports the person receives: kind, extent, and frequency (12.00D, 12.00H3d).
- Intellectual disorder: test name, full scale and part scores, mean 100 and SD 15, examiner license at the independent level (or co-signature), validity statement, observations during testing, and onset evidence before 22.
- Epilepsy: "a detailed description of a typical seizure", frequency over consecutive months, and adherence to prescribed treatment.
- Walker, two canes or crutches, or wheelchair: the limitation in upper or lower extremity function, the circumstances of use, and need for at least 12 continuous months.
- Hearing: otologic exam (history, effect on the person, ear canal and tympanic membrane findings); audiometry within 2 months, aids off, otoscopic exam just before testing.
- Vision: best corrected central acuity or visual field measures, and the cause of the loss.
- Do not include: statements that the person is "disabled", "unable to work", "meets a listing", or a work level label (sedentary, light) without functional description. SSA gives these no analysis.

### 6.3 DME under MO HealthNet (DME Manual, 07/06/2026)
- Written prescription from an MHD enrolled MD, DO, podiatrist, or collaborative practice APN (speech pathologist for ACDs only), with the prescriber's NPI.
- CMN elements: procedure code and modifier, item description with brand and model, accessories, reason needed, anticipated length of need, date prescribed, diagnosis, prognosis.
- PA elements: diagnosis, prognosis, detailed explanation from the prescribing physician or therapist, length of need, projected outcome, prescriber signature.
- Custom or power wheelchair: the 8 justification points in card 1.7, plus any PT, OT, or seating clinic evaluation, and home access fit.
- Face to face encounter documentation where Medicare requires it.
- Replacement: continuing need; how the item was lost or destroyed; police report if stolen; nature of any change in condition.
- Exception requests (adults): covered alternatives tried and why they failed, and which exception condition applies (life sustaining, terminal illness quality of life, natural disaster replacement, prevents a higher level of care).

### 6.4 Home care: home health, private duty nursing, MCAW, DHSS level of care
- Home health face to face documentation: clinical findings supporting need, primary reason, date (90 days before to 30 days after start), clinician name and credentials. Ordering practitioner certification that intermittent skilled nursing or therapy is needed; plan of care reviewed at least every 60 days.
- PDN or MCAW plan of care (MFAW Manual 3.2): all pertinent diagnoses with ICD codes and relevant surgeries; mental, psychosocial, and cognitive status; services, supplies, equipment; frequency and duration; orders for discipline and treatments; prognosis; functional limitations; activities permitted; nutritional requirements; all medications (name, dose, frequency, route), treatments, allergies; safety measures including emergency department and readmission risk; participant goals with measurable outcomes; advance directive information; practitioner signature.
- DHSS level of care: DHSS assesses. Useful clinician inputs (from the rule's scoring categories): diagnoses, cognition findings, mobility and transfer needs, eating and tube feeding, physician ordered therapeutic diet, medications and treatments. Respond to any DSDS Healthcare Professional Inquiry or General Health Evaluation form (agent 08).

### 6.5 School, college, and VR accommodations
- School: an outside evaluation must be "considered" if it meets agency criteria. Give test names, scores, functional findings, and recommendations. Request the Summary of Performance at exit.
- VR: VR uses "existing data", including medical and education records and other agencies' determinations. Send the SSI or SSDI award letter (presumed eligibility) and current functional findings.
- College: AHEAD guidance says outside documentation should describe the disability, the barriers it creates, and accommodation history. Each college sets its own rules.

## 7. Pediatric to adult health care transition

Sources: [Got Transition, Six Core Elements 3.0](https://www.gottransition.org/six-core-elements/), checked 2026-10-26 (High); White PH, Cooley WC, and others, "Supporting the Health Care Transition From Adolescence to Adulthood in the Medical Home," Pediatrics 2018;142(5):e20182587 (AAP, AAFP, ACP clinical report), full text read via a [secondary host copy](https://archive.thepcc.org/sites/default/files/resources/e20182587.full_.pdf), checked 2026-10-26 (High for quoted text). MO HealthNet rules: card 1.8.

### 7.1 Timeline (Got Transition age ranges)
- Age 12–14: practice shares its transition policy (Core Element 1).
- Age 14–18: tracking and monitoring; readiness assessment; planning, including a medical summary and emergency care plan (Core Elements 2–4).
- Age 18–21: transfer of care (Core Element 5).
- Age 18–23: transfer completion and integration into adult care (Core Element 6).
- The AAP report says youth with medical complexity or IDD may need "flexibility in the age of transfer to adult care", delayed specialist transfers, more care coordination, and "strong linkages to nonhealth support systems." It says "Referral alone" is not enough; AAFP coverage of the report calls for a "warm handoff" (secondary).

### 7.2 What changes at 18
- Legal adulthood: the AAP report frames readiness "in anticipation of legally becoming an adult at age 18 years, unless alternative decision-making supports are in place." Planning for decision support "is best started at least by age 17 years, and the resulting modifications need to be documented in the medical record and communicated to new adult care clinicians." Legal options (guardianship, supported decision making, powers of attorney, health information releases): agent 04.
- SSA uses adult rules at 18; children on SSI have an age 18 redetermination (agent 04 and 06). Adult medical evidence is needed then.
- MO HealthNet HCY benefits continue to age 20 for those still eligible (eligibility categories: agent 06).
- Before 18: get the transition evidence packet (section 4, item 2); ask for the exit reevaluation; sign releases so adult clinicians and agencies can get records.

### 7.3 What changes at 21
- HCY/EPSDT ends. See the card 1.8 table: outpatient therapy, private duty nursing, several devices, enteral formula, binaural hearing aids, eye exam frequency, ABA.
- Special education age eligibility in Missouri ends around this age (agent 07).
- Before 21 (start at 19): refer to the Medically Complex Adult Waiver if on HCY PDN; refer to DMH and ask about DD waiver therapy and supplies; ask the prescriber about exception requests for HCY only devices or formula; complete clinically needed replacements of hearing aids, glasses, and mobility devices while HCY rules apply; confirm an adult primary care clinician and adult specialists who accept MO HealthNet.

### 7.4 What changes at 22
- DMH DD and SSA 12.05 both look for onset before 22. This is not an application deadline, but evidence gets harder to find after school ends. Childhood Disability Benefits also turn on disability before 22 (agent 06).
