### DMH Division of Developmental Disabilities (DD) eligibility
- Run by: Missouri Department of Mental Health (DMH), Division of Developmental Disabilities, through regional offices. Greene County: Springfield Regional Office, 1515 E. Pythian, Springfield, MO 65801, 417-895-7400. Joplin Satellite Office, 3600 E. Newman Rd., Joplin, MO 64802, 417-629-3020.
- Serves (ages, disability types): Any age. People with a "developmental disability" as defined in RSMo 630.005, which includes intellectual disability, cerebral palsy, epilepsy, head injury, autism, a learning disability related to brain dysfunction, or any other mental or physical impairment or combination.
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year): RSMo 630.005 (version effective 28 Aug 2018) requires all of these: (a) a qualifying condition; (b) the disability "manifested before the person attains age twenty-two"; (c) it is "likely to continue indefinitely"; (d) substantial functional limitations in two or more of six areas: self-care, receptive and expressive language, learning, self-direction, capacity for independent living or economic self-sufficiency, mobility; (e) a need for a combination and sequence of special, interdisciplinary, or generic care that is individually planned and coordinated. Under 9 CSR 45-2.010 (amended effective 30 Apr 2023), adults 18 and older are assessed with the Missouri Adaptive Abilities Scale (MAAS); deficits in two or more major life areas on the comprehensive evaluation count as substantial functional limitations. The screening step may screen out only a person whose disability "clearly was not manifested before age twenty-two". A diagnosis alone does not decide eligibility; the rule uses functional assessment.
- Prerequisites (what the family must have first): None. Medicaid is not required for DD eligibility. A valid application must be on the DMH form and be signed or marked by the applicant (a mark must be witnessed). For an adult with no guardian, the adult signs.
- Documents and evidence needed: Medical, psychological, and educational records that show onset before 22 (school evaluations and IEPs are useful). The rule says regional offices accept and do not duplicate reliable assessments from schools and other bodies. Persons already found eligible by another agency are automatically eligible for screening.
- How to apply in SW Missouri (steps, office, phone, URL): Call or write the Springfield Regional Office (417-895-7400) or Joplin Satellite (417-629-3020). Under 9 CSR 45-2.010(4)(B), the office must give application forms by the end of the next business day after any inquiry. If the office does not receive the application within 30 calendar days, staff must contact the person.
- Typical time or wait: Rule deadline: comprehensive evaluation within 30 business days after a valid application and sufficient records. If records are missing, the team must write a temporary action plan and may take up to 30 more business days. Written notice of eligibility within 3 business days of the decision. Individualized Support Plan (ISP) within 30 business days after eligibility; within 5 business days for a person who needs immediate services. Actual times in Springfield: see agent 01 and 02.
- Cost to family: No fee in the eligibility rule. (Cost-sharing for services is outside this card.)
- Where families get stuck or denied: (1) No records proving onset before 22, which hits adults who apply late or had a head injury near age 22. (2) MAAS scores that do not show two areas of limitation; for a claim based on intellectual disability, the rule requires the team to consider extra assessments the applicant gives (9 CSR 45-2.010(4)(E)). (3) Reassessment: DMH may reassess "as needed" and discharge a person found no longer eligible, but must give 60 calendar days written notice before a reassessment and a discharge plan with at least 60 calendar days to transition.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: 9 CSR 45-2.020. Notice of ineligibility must be in writing on a DMH form, give the factual and legal reasons, name a contact, include an appeals brochure, and be given at least 30 calendar days before the action takes effect. Step 1: appeal to regional office staff, in writing or verbally, within 30 calendar days of receiving the notice. Regional director decides within 10 business days. Step 2: ask for an appeals referee, verbally or in writing, within 30 calendar days of receipt of that decision. Hearing with 30 days notice, held within 60 calendar days of the request unless good cause. The regional office has the burden of proof. Referee decides within 30 calendar days of the hearing. Step 3: either side may seek judicial review in circuit court under RSMo Chapter 536. Services continue: "No applicant's or individual's benefits or services shall be reduced or terminated until appeal procedures are exhausted" (9 CSR 45-2.020(3)(E)). The rule says time limits are to be "liberally interpreted to decide claims on the merits" (9 CSR 45-2.020(2)(A)).
- Screener questions: Did the condition start before age 22? Is it expected to last for life or a long time? Does the person need help in at least two of: self-care, communication, learning, making decisions or self-direction, living on their own or earning a living, getting around?
- Screener rule: onset_before_22 = yes AND lifelong = yes AND count(limitation_areas) >= 2 THEN "may be eligible for DMH DD; apply to the regional office". If onset_before_22 = unknown, still refer (the regional office decides).
- Sources: [RSMo 630.005](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=630.005), checked 2026-10-26; [9 CSR 45-2 (current, 3/31/23 printing)](https://www.sos.mo.gov/cmsimages/adrules/csr/current/9csr/9c45-2.pdf), checked 2026-10-26; [DMH Springfield Regional Offices](https://dmh.mo.gov/dev-disabilities/regional-offices/springfield), checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: High

### DD Medicaid waivers: Prioritization of Need, wait list, and Utilization Review
- Run by: DMH DD operates; MO HealthNet Division (Department of Social Services, DSS) is the single state Medicaid agency. Federal law: 42 USC 1396n(c) (HCBS waivers).
- Serves (ages, disability types): People found DD eligible who meet ICF/DD level of care. Waivers named in 9 CSR 45-2.015: Comprehensive (includes residential), Community Support (no residential), Partnership for Hope (county-based, no residential), Missouri Children with Developmental Disabilities (MOCDD, under 18, living with parents, parental income and assets waived). The Autism waiver is not in this rule; see agent 08 for service detail.
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year): 9 CSR 45-2.015 and 2.017 (both amended effective 30 Apr 2023). A Prioritization of Need (PON) score from the MAAS (scale 1 to 5, 5 is highest) is required before entry to the Comprehensive, Community Support, or MOCDD waiver, or to general revenue Purchase of Service. Ties go to the person who has waited longest. People who meet "emergency criteria" get highest priority. Emergency criteria (9 CSR 45-2.017(1)(E)): (1) immediate need for life-sustaining services with no alternative; (2) immediate need to protect self or others from imminent physical harm; (3) living in an ICF/DD or nursing facility, able and wanting to move to the community; (4) losing significant waiver services due to age but still needing them; (5) in Children's Division custody with a funding agreement. Partnership for Hope priority: crisis first (serious risk of immediate harm or death; loss of primary caregiver or caregiver cannot meet needs; abuse, neglect, or exploitation), then other criteria, including a person who has used up both education and Vocational Rehabilitation (VR) or is not VR eligible and needs employment services. A person may be on only one HCBS waiver at a time (9 CSR 45-2.015(5)). Medicaid State Plan services must be used first when they meet the need (9 CSR 45-2.017(14)(B)).
- Prerequisites: DMH DD eligibility; MO HealthNet eligibility (except MOCDD, which confers it for a child); an ISP with the requested service; ICF/DD level of care.
- Documents and evidence needed: ISP that documents the service as "necessary for the individual's health, safety, and/or independence", that "alternative funding or programs are not available", and that the need is "specifically related to the person's disability" (9 CSR 45-2.017(5)). Evidence of any crisis or emergency criterion.
- How to apply in SW Missouri: Through the DD support coordinator. In Greene County, support coordination may be through the regional office or a county or private targeted case management agency; see agent 01.
- Typical time or wait: Utilization Review (UR) reviews within 6 business days; the regional director decides within 5 business days, or 5 business days after a 10-business-day request for more information; decision letter within 10 business days of the decision. The wait for a waiver slot depends on funding and turnover and has no legal deadline in the rule. Turnover funds go first to current participants with increased needs (9 CSR 45-2.015(4)).
- Cost to family: None for waiver services in the rule. MO HealthNet spend-down or premium questions: agent 06.
- Where families get stuck or denied: No legal time limit to move off the wait list. The PON score drives access, and a lower score may wait without a set end. If a family refuses an offered service, a new PON is required; for MOCDD, refusal removes the child from the list. Only the regional director or assistant regional director can deny or modify a request (9 CSR 45-2.017(1)(M)).
- Appeal rights and deadlines: Two tracks. (A) DMH track: appeal the UR decision to the regional director, in writing or verbally, within 30 calendar days of the decision letter; written decision within 10 business days (9 CSR 45-2.017(9)). If the decision denies a service based on eligibility or "adversely affects a waiver service", use the appeals referee steps in 9 CSR 45-2.020(3)(C). (B) Medicaid fair hearing track: a waiver participant may appeal to DSS "before, during, or after" the DMH process, in writing within 90 calendar days of written notice of the adverse action, to MO HealthNet Division, Constituent Services Unit, PO Box 6500, Jefferson City, MO 65102-6500, phone 800-392-2161 (9 CSR 45-2.017(10)(A)). Once the DSS appeal starts, DMH appeal rights end. Services under an existing ISP continue pending the hearing decision if the appeal is timely, but the family "may be responsible for repayment" if the decision is upheld (9 CSR 45-2.017(11)). Federal rules: 42 CFR 431.220(a)(1) gives a fair hearing to anyone whose claim is "not acted upon with reasonable promptness". See the Medicaid due process card.
- Screener questions: Is the person DMH DD eligible? Does the person have MO HealthNet now? Under 18 and living with parents? Is there a crisis now (danger, caregiver loss, abuse)?
- Screener rule: dd_eligible = yes AND (medicaid = yes OR (age < 18 AND lives_with_parents = yes)) THEN "ask support coordinator for an ISP, PON score, and wait list placement". IF crisis = yes THEN "tell the support coordinator in writing; emergency criteria give highest priority".
- Sources: [9 CSR 45-2.015 and 2.017](https://www.sos.mo.gov/cmsimages/adrules/csr/current/9csr/9c45-2.pdf), checked 2026-10-26; [42 USC 1396n](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/1396n), checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: High for the rule text; Medium for whether the PO Box and phone in the rule are still current.

### MO HealthNet (Medicaid) due process: notice, fair hearing, and benefits during appeal
- Run by: Department of Social Services (DSS). Family Support Division (FSD) decides eligibility; MO HealthNet Division (MHD) decides services. Hearings: DSS Division of Legal Services, Benefit Hearings Unit (Western Missouri office 816-325-5918; Central 573-751-0335). Federal rules: 42 CFR 431 Subpart E. State law: RSMo 208.080 and 208.100.
- Serves: Every applicant for or recipient of MO HealthNet, including HCBS waiver participants (DMH and DHSS waivers) and people on spend-down.
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year): Who gets a hearing (42 CFR 431.220(a)(1), current eCFR): any person who believes the agency acted wrongly, denied a claim for eligibility or for covered services, set spend-down or cost-sharing amounts, changed the amount or type of services, made a prior authorization decision, or "has not acted upon the claim with reasonable promptness". RSMo 208.080.1(2) gives the same right when an application "is not acted upon within a reasonable time after it is filed".
- Prerequisites: A written notice, or a claim that has gone unanswered.
- Documents and evidence needed: The notice; the evidence that supports eligibility or the need for the service (for services: the ISP or care plan, doctor letters). RSMo 208.080.2 and .6: the hearing looks at facts as they existed when the action was proposed or taken, so collect evidence for that date.
- How to apply in SW Missouri: A hearing request "may initially be made orally or in any written form" (RSMo 208.080.8) to FSD or MHD. Federal rule 42 CFR 431.221(a) requires the state to take requests by all the modes in 42 CFR 435.907(a), which include phone and internet; the internet and phone requirement has a delayed effective date tied to 42 CFR 435.1200(i). For DMH waiver services, 9 CSR 45-2.017(10)(A) names MO HealthNet Division, Constituent Services Unit, PO Box 6500, Jefferson City, MO 65102-6500, 800-392-2161.
- Typical time or wait: Federal rule: final administrative action "ordinarily, within 90 days" from the date the agency receives the hearing request (42 CFR 431.244(f)(1)(ii)). Expedited hearing for urgent eligibility claims: no later than 7 working days, under a delayed effective date (42 CFR 431.244(f)(3)).
- Cost to family: No fee. No bond or docket fee for the circuit court appeal under RSMo 208.100.3.
- Where families get stuck or denied: (1) The 10-day window. RSMo 208.080.3: a recipient has 10 days "from the date of the mailing of notice" to appeal a reduction or closure and keep benefits "undiminished or unchanged". After that, the 90-day window still allows an appeal, but benefits do not continue (RSMo 208.080.4). (2) Federal advance notice is only 10 days before the action date (42 CFR 431.211). Mail delay can use up most of the window. (3) If the family loses, the agency may recover the cost of services paid during the appeal (42 CFR 431.230(b)); this scares families out of asking for continued benefits. (4) Applicants who are denied get no benefits during the appeal.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: Reduction or closure: appeal within 10 days of the mailing date to keep benefits; otherwise within 90 days of the closure or change. Denied application: 90 days from the date of the notice (RSMo 208.080.5). Federal ceiling: the state may not allow more than 90 days from the mailing date (42 CFR 431.221(d)). Federal reinstatement: if the agency acted with no proper advance notice, it must reinstate services when the person asks within 10 days of receiving the notice (receipt is presumed 5 days after the notice date) (42 CFR 431.231(c)). Judicial review: affidavit for appeal to the circuit court of the county of residence within 90 days of the director's decision (RSMo 208.100), reviewed on the record under RSMo 536.140.
- Screener questions: Did you get a letter that stops, cuts, or denies MO HealthNet or a service? What is the date on the letter? Is the person getting the service now?
- Screener rule: IF letter = reduce_or_stop AND days_since_mailing <= 10 THEN "appeal today to keep benefits". IF letter = reduce_or_stop AND days_since_mailing between 11 and 90 THEN "you can still appeal; benefits will not continue". IF letter = denial AND days_since_notice <= 90 THEN "you can appeal". IF no decision on an application after a long time THEN "you can ask for a hearing for failure to act with reasonable promptness".
- Sources: [RSMo 208.080](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=208.080), checked 2026-10-26; [RSMo 208.100](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=208.100), checked 2026-10-26; [42 CFR 431.211, 431.220, 431.221, 431.230, 431.231, 431.244 (eCFR current)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-C/part-431/subpart-E), checked 2026-10-26; [DSS Information about Hearings](https://dss.mo.gov/dls/hearings), checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: High

### DHSS home and community based services (state plan personal care, consumer-directed services, DHSS waivers)
- Run by: Department of Health and Senior Services (DHSS), Division of Senior and Disability Services (DSDS). Hearings are held by DSS Division of Legal Services. Rules are in 19 CSR 15 and 19 CSR 30; service detail belongs to agent 08.
- Serves: MO HealthNet participants who meet nursing facility level of care (adults with physical and medically complex disabilities; some people with IDD).
- Eligibility rules: Agent 08 and 06 cover level-of-care points and income. This card covers the legal process only.
- Prerequisites: MO HealthNet; a DSDS assessment.
- Documents and evidence needed: The Adverse Action Notice; the assessment; doctor letters about care needs.
- How to apply in SW Missouri: Ask for a hearing verbally or in writing to DSDS staff. Anyone may ask for the participant, but DSDS must confirm the request directly with the participant or guardian. A DSDS supervisor reviews every hearing request within 3 business days before it goes to DSS.
- Typical time or wait: No decision deadline stated in the DHSS manual. Federal 90-day rule applies (see the Medicaid due process card).
- Cost to family: No fee. If DSDS wins, the participant or estate "may be liable" for services delivered during the appeal.
- Where families get stuck or denied: Initial denials: no services while the hearing is pending. Current participants: only 10 calendar days from the mailing date to keep services; the action takes effect on day 11.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: Initial denial: 90 calendar days from the date the Adverse Action Notice is mailed; no services pending. Reduction or closure: 10 calendar days from mailing keeps services at the current level. DSS state hearing form says "ten (10) business days" (conflict with the manual's calendar days; use the shorter one). Independent Living Waiver waiting list notice: 90 calendar days.
- Screener questions: Does the person get in-home care or consumer-directed care from DHSS now? Did a notice cut or end it? Date of the notice?
- Screener rule: IF dhss_hcbs = yes AND notice = reduce_or_stop AND days_since_mailing <= 10 THEN "ask for a hearing now; services continue".
- Sources: [DHSS HCBS Manual 6.00 Appeal and Hearing Process](https://health.mo.gov/providers/manuals/home-and-community-based-services-manual/600-appeal-and-hearing-process), checked 2026-10-26; [DHSS HCBS Manual 5.00 Appendix 4, Application for State Hearing instructions](https://health.mo.gov/providers/manuals/home-and-community-based-services-manual/500-adverse-actions/500-appendix-4), seen in search results 2026-10-26 (not opened)
- Confidence: High for the manual; Medium for the "business days" wording on the form (seen only in a search snippet).

### Medicaid community engagement (work) requirement, starting 2027: exemptions for disabled people and caregivers
- Run by: Federal law (Public Law 119-21, 2025, section 71119); CMS interim final rule at 42 CFR 435.550 to 435.556; DSS/FSD will apply it. Overlap: agent 06 covers income and program effects; agent 05 covers advocacy.
- Serves: Applies only to "applicable individuals" in the adult expansion group (Missouri adopted expansion by constitutional amendment in 2020). It does not apply to SSI-related or waiver groups.
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year): Starts 1 Jan 2027 per CMS (states may get a later date in some cases; see Open questions). 80 hours a month of work, community service, a work program, or school, or income of at least federal minimum wage times 80 hours (42 CFR 435.552). Exempt ("specified excluded individuals", 42 CFR 435.554(c)): a parent, guardian, caretaker relative, or family caregiver of a "disabled individual" as defined by the ADA (28 CFR 35.108), with a family caregiver test (lives with and gives regular care, or is a relative who gives regular care, or gives at least 80 hours a month of care); people who are "medically frail", which includes a person who is blind or disabled under SSA rules, or who has a "physical, intellectual, or developmental disability that significantly impairs their ability to perform one or more activities of daily living", or a disabling mental disorder, but only if the condition "significantly impairs the individual's ability to comply" (42 CFR 435.554(c)(5)(i)). The state must keep a list of qualifying conditions and a way for people not on the list to ask for the exemption.
- Where families get stuck or denied: Caregivers of adult children with IDD who are on expansion Medicaid may lose coverage if the state does not record the caregiver exemption. The "ability to comply" test in the CMS rule is narrower than the statute, according to a coalition comment letter dated 31 July 2026 (secondary).
- Appeal rights and deadlines: Same as the Medicaid due process card (10 days to keep coverage, 90 days to appeal).
- Screener questions: Is the adult on MO HealthNet for adults (expansion)? Does the adult care for a disabled family member? Does the adult have a disability that limits daily activities?
- Screener rule: IF expansion_adult = yes AND (caregiver_of_disabled = yes OR medically_frail = yes) THEN "likely exempt from the work requirement; tell FSD in writing and keep proof".
- Sources: [42 CFR 435.552 and 435.554 (eCFR, up to date as of 2026-10-22)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-C/part-435/subpart-L), checked 2026-10-26 (subpart letter not verified; search the section number); [CMS community engagement overview slide](https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/downloads/community-engagement-overview-slide.pdf), seen in search 2026-10-26; [DAC/CCD comment letter, 31 Jul 2026 (secondary)](https://aahd.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/DAC-CCD-comment-letter-to-CMS-Medicaid-work-and-comm-engagement-rules-July-31-2026-53-orgs.pdf)
- Confidence: High for the regulation text; Medium for the start date and how Missouri will apply it.

### Social Security (SSI and SSDI/CDB) appeals, age-18 redetermination, and Section 301
- Run by: Social Security Administration (SSA). Initial and reconsideration disability decisions are made by a state disability determination agency for SSA (name and office not verified here). Overlap: agent 06 covers benefit amounts and CDB rules.
- Serves: SSI applicants and recipients; SSDI and Childhood Disability Benefit (CDB) beneficiaries.
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year): Age-18 redetermination (20 CFR 416.987): every child SSI recipient who was eligible in the month before turning 18 is redetermined under adult rules, usually during the year after the 18th birthday. SSA must notify in writing before it starts. Section 301 continuation (20 CFR 416.1338): if SSA finds the person not disabled in the age-18 redetermination, payments can continue if the person began, before the disability ended, an appropriate program: an IEP for a student age 18 through 21, an Individualized Plan for Employment with state VR, or a Ticket to Work plan. For an IEP student 18 to 21, SSA "will find" that continuing helps.
- Prerequisites: An SSA determination notice.
- Documents and evidence needed: Medical records, school records (IEP, evaluations), VR plan if any.
- How to apply in SW Missouri: Appeal online at ssa.gov, by phone, or at a local SSA office. (Local office address: see agent 06.)
- Typical time or wait: Not in the regulations.
- Cost to family: No fee. Representative fees are regulated by SSA.
- Where families get stuck or denied: (1) The 10-day window to keep payments. (2) Families do not know about Section 301 and do not show SSA the IEP or VR plan. (3) A finding that the person needs a representative payee is appealable, but families are not told.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: Four levels, each 60 days after the date you receive the notice (receipt presumed 5 days after the notice date, 20 CFR 416.1401): reconsideration (20 CFR 416.1409), hearing before an administrative law judge (416.1433), Appeals Council (416.1468), federal court (20 CFR 422.210(c)). SSI payments continue at the old level if the appeal is filed within 10 days after receipt of the notice of intent to suspend, reduce, or end payments (20 CFR 416.1336(b)). SSDI or CDB medical cessation: ask for continued benefits within 10 days after receipt (20 CFR 404.1597a(f) and (g)). Late appeals: ask for an extension in writing and show good cause (416.1409(b), 416.1411). Payments continued during appeal may become an overpayment if the person loses; the person may waive continuation in writing (416.1336(c)).
- Screener questions: Is the person on SSI and turning 18 within a year? Is the person in an IEP, a VR plan, or a Ticket to Work plan? Did SSA send a notice that stops or cuts benefits, and when?
- Screener rule: IF ssi = yes AND age between 17 and 19 THEN "prepare for age-18 redetermination; if in IEP or VR plan, tell SSA (Section 301)". IF ssa_notice = stop_or_cut AND days_since_receipt <= 10 THEN "appeal now and ask for continued payments".
- Sources: [20 CFR 416.987](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/section-416.987), [416.1338](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/section-416.1338), [416.1336](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/section-416.1336), [416.1401](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/section-416.1401), [416.1409](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/section-416.1409), [416.1433](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/section-416.1433), [416.1468](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/section-416.1468), [404.1597a](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/section-404.1597a), [422.210](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/section-422.210), all checked 2026-10-26 (eCFR title 20 up to date as of 2026-10-22)
- Confidence: High

### SSA representative payee (alternative to conservatorship for benefit money)
- Run by: SSA. 20 CFR 416.601 to 416.665 (SSI); 20 CFR 404.2001 and following (Title II).
- Serves: A beneficiary SSA finds needs help managing benefits.
- Eligibility rules: SSA decides whether payment to a payee is in the person's interest. The choice to use a payee, and who the payee is, are both "initial determinations" that can be appealed (20 CFR 416.1402(d) and (e)). SSA must give advance written notice that names the payee and explains appeal rights (20 CFR 416.630(a)). A protest or appeal within 10 days after receiving a mailed notice delays the payee appointment until a decision (416.630(b)(2)).
- Where families get stuck or denied: Representative payee covers only SSA money. It does not give authority over health care, other income, contracts, or bank accounts. Missouri courts must consider an existing payee as a less restrictive alternative to conservatorship (RSMo 475.075.13(3)).
- Appeal rights and deadlines: As for SSA appeals (60 days; 10 days to delay the appointment).
- Screener rule: IF only_income = SSA_benefits AND needs_help_with_money = yes THEN "a representative payee may avoid a conservatorship".
- Sources: [20 CFR 416.630](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/section-416.630), [20 CFR 416.1402](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/section-416.1402), [RSMo 475.075](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=475.075), checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: High

### IDEA transition planning and transfer of rights at age 18 (school district)
- Run by: The local school district (for Springfield, Springfield Public Schools; other districts in the region), supervised by the Missouri Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (DESE), Office of Special Education. Due process hearings: Missouri Administrative Hearing Commission (RSMo 162.961). Overlap: agent 07 covers transition services in practice.
- Serves: Students with IEPs. Missouri defines "children with disabilities" as children "under the age of twenty-one years who have not completed an approved high school program" (RSMo 162.675(1)).
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year): Transition: "Beginning not later than the first IEP to be in effect when the child turns 16", the IEP must have measurable postsecondary goals based on age-appropriate transition assessments, and the transition services needed (34 CFR 300.320(b)). Transfer of rights notice: "not later than one year before" the age of majority, the IEP must state that the student has been told which rights transfer (34 CFR 300.320(c)). Missouri transfer: "When a student with a disability reaches age 18 or otherwise is emancipated", the school gives notices to both student and parents, and "All other rights accorded to parents under Part B of IDEA transfer to the student". The transfer "does not apply if the student is declared incompetent by a court" (DESE Regulation V, section L, implementing 34 CFR 300.520). Missouri has not shown, in the sources checked, a procedure under 34 CFR 300.520(b) to appoint a parent as educational representative for an adult student who is not under guardianship; see Open questions.
- Prerequisites: An IEP.
- Documents and evidence needed: IEP, transition assessments, prior written notices, procedural safeguards notice.
- How to apply in SW Missouri: Ask the IEP team in writing. An adult student can sign a release or invite a parent to IEP meetings; a supported decision-making agreement can name the parent as a supporter (no Missouri statute governs the form; see the SDM card).
- Typical time or wait: Not applicable.
- Cost to family: Free (FAPE).
- Where families get stuck or denied: (1) At 18, the school must deal with the student; a parent who is not a guardian has no IDEA rights unless the student involves the parent. This pressure leads some families to seek full guardianship when a release or SDM would do. (2) A regular diploma ends FAPE; agent 07 covers this. (3) The exact last day of eligibility at age 21 (birthday or end of school year) is not stated in the sources checked.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: State complaint to DESE: violation must be "not more than one year prior" to receipt; DESE has 60 calendar days to investigate (Regulation V). Due process complaint: within two years of when the parent or agency knew or should have known (34 CFR 300.511(e); Regulation V). Resolution meeting within 15 days; 30-day resolution period; decision within 45 days after it (Regulation V). Stay put: the student stays in the current placement during due process (34 CFR 300.518). Civil action: within 45 days to state court under RSMo Chapter 536 or to federal court (Regulation V, Civil Proceedings). After age 18 these rights belong to the student unless a court has declared the student incompetent.
- Screener questions: Age of student? Has an IEP? Is the student 17 or older? Does the student have a guardian?
- Screener rule: IF iep = yes AND age >= 15 THEN "the IEP must have transition goals by the IEP in effect at 16". IF iep = yes AND age >= 17 AND guardian = no THEN "plan for transfer of rights at 18: consider releases, supported decision-making, powers of attorney, before any guardianship".
- Sources: [34 CFR 300.320, 300.520, 300.518, 300.153, 300.511 (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/subtitle-B/chapter-III/part-300), checked 2026-10-26; [DESE Regulation V Procedural Safeguards/Discipline (2026 PDF)](https://dese.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston521/files/media/pdf/2026/07/Regulation%20V%20Procedural%20Safeguards%20Discipline.pdf), checked 2026-10-26; [RSMo 162.961](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=162.961), [RSMo 162.675](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=162.675), checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: High

### Adult guardianship (full) under RSMo Chapter 475, Greene County Probate Division
- Run by: Circuit Court of Greene County (31st Judicial Circuit), Probate Division. Location reported as the Judicial Courts Facility, 1010 N. Boonville Ave., Springfield, MO 65802, phone 417-868-4027 (secondary source; confirm). The Greene County Public Administrator serves as guardian when no family member can; the office reports about 620 wards and protectees.
- Serves: Adults found "incapacitated": unable "to receive and evaluate information or to communicate decisions" to the extent that the person cannot meet essential needs for food, clothing, shelter, safety, or other care "so that serious physical injury, illness, or disease is likely to occur" (RSMo 475.010 and 475.075.11). The definition was changed by SB 806 (2018).
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year): RSMo 475.075 (effective 28 Aug 2018): burden on the petitioner, "clear and convincing evidence" (475.075.9). Before appointing, the court "shall consider" whether needs can be met by a less restrictive alternative, including a durable power of attorney signed before the petition, a trust, a representative payee, "Supported decision-making agreements", services or assistive technology, an emergency guardian ad litem, or a limited guardian (475.075.13). The order must contain findings on supervised living, finances, and whether the person keeps the right to vote, to drive (if able to pass the test), and to marry (475.075.14).
- Prerequisites: The petitioner may be "any person" (RSMo 475.060). The petition must state the facts and specific behaviors showing why a guardian is needed (475.060.2).
- Documents and evidence needed: Petition with the items in 475.060.2; usually a physician or psychologist report (the court "may direct" an examination, 475.075.6); list of relatives to notify.
- How to apply in SW Missouri: File the petition in the Probate Division of the county where the person lives (venue: RSMo 475.035). The court appoints a lawyer for the respondent at once (475.075.4). The respondent must be served in person (475.075.2).
- Typical time or wait: No statutory deadline for a regular hearing. Emergency guardian ad litem: hearing within 5 business days, appointment up to 90 days (475.075.15).
- Cost to family: Court costs, the fee of the respondent's court-appointed lawyer (taxed as costs, 475.075.5), and any examiner fee (475.075.7). Greene County fee amounts: not verified (see Open questions).
- Where families get stuck or denied: (1) Schools, hospitals, and banks tell families they "need" guardianship at 18; the statute requires alternatives to be considered first. (2) Full guardianship removes rights the person may keep under a limited order. (3) Annual reports are required for the life of the guardianship (RSMo 475.082). (4) Restoration needs a petition and a hearing.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: The respondent has the rights to a lawyer, a jury, to be present, to remain silent, to present evidence, to cross-examine, to an open or closed hearing, and to appeal (475.075.10). Appeal: notice of appeal within 10 days after the judgment becomes final; a judgment usually becomes final 30 days after entry (Missouri Supreme Court Rules 81.04 and 81.05; secondary summary). After appointment: the ward may petition at any time, even by informal letter, to restore capacity, cut the guardian's powers, or return rights (475.083.4); a contested petition is decided by preponderance of the evidence, and may not be filed more than once every 180 days (475.083.6). The court must review every adult guardianship at least yearly (475.082.1) and must appoint a lawyer to file for restoration if the incapacity seems to have ended (475.082.4). Ward rights (475.361) include the "least restrictive form of guardianship", to be "restored to capacity at the earliest possible time", and to "participate in any health care decision-making process". Appointment of a guardian revokes an earlier health care power of attorney unless the court orders otherwise (475.361.3).
- Screener questions: Is the person 18 or older? Can the person, with help, understand and communicate choices about health, money, and living? Is there an emergency risk of serious harm now? Has the person already signed a power of attorney or SDM agreement?
- Screener rule: IF age >= 17 AND (needs_help_deciding = yes) THEN "show alternatives first (SDM, POA, health care directive, rep payee, ABLE); guardianship only if needs cannot be met by an alternative". IF emergency_risk = yes AND no_alternative = yes THEN "emergency guardian ad litem may be possible; see a lawyer".
- Sources: [RSMo 475.010](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=475.010), [475.060](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=475.060), [475.075](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=475.075), [475.082](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=475.082), [475.083](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=475.083), [475.120](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=475.120), [475.361](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=475.361), [Chapter 475 index with effective dates](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneChapter.aspx?chapter=475), all checked 2026-10-26; [Greene County Public Administrator](https://greenecountymo.gov/public_administrator), checked 2026-10-26; [Missouri Courts, Initiating an Appeal](https://www.courts.mo.gov/page.jsp?id=842), seen in search 2026-10-26; [SwiftProbate Greene County guide (secondary, for address)](https://www.swiftprobate.com/probate/missouri/greene-county)
- Confidence: High for the statute; Medium for the court address, phone, and appeal timing; Low for costs.

### Limited guardianship and limited conservatorship
- Run by: Probate Division (as above). RSMo 475.080.
- Serves: An adult who is "partially incapacitated" (lacks capacity to meet essential needs "in part") or "partially disabled" (can manage money "in part") (RSMo 475.010).
- Eligibility rules: If the court finds partial incapacity "and that the respondent's identified needs cannot be met by a less restrictive alternative, the court shall appoint a limited guardian" (475.080.1). The order must list the guardian's specific powers and the "legal disabilities" of the ward, impose only those restraints "as are necessary", and "encourage the development of maximum self-reliance and independence" (475.080.1). A limited conservator is appointed the same way for partial financial disability (475.080.2).
- Where families get stuck or denied: Petitions and orders often ask for full powers by default. The family or the respondent's lawyer must ask for a limited order and name the powers needed (for example, medical consent and benefits only).
- Appeal rights and deadlines: As for full guardianship. The ward can later petition to return more rights (475.083.4) or ask the court for specific rights such as to marry, contract, consent to medical care, choose where to live, or drive (475.361.2).
- Screener rule: IF needs_help_in_some_areas_only = yes AND alternatives_not_enough = yes THEN "ask for a limited guardianship listing only the needed powers".
- Sources: [RSMo 475.080](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=475.080), [RSMo 475.361](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=475.361), checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: High

### Conservatorship (money and property)
- Run by: Probate Division. RSMo 475.061, 475.062, 475.130, 475.270 (annual settlements).
- Serves: An adult who is "disabled" under Chapter 475: unable to receive and evaluate information or communicate decisions to the extent that the person "lacks ability to manage the person's financial resources" (475.010).
- Eligibility rules: Same hearing, rights, and clear and convincing standard as guardianship (475.075). A representative payee, a trust, or a durable power of attorney are named alternatives (475.075.13).
- Cost to family: Bond (RSMo 475.100) and annual settlements (475.270) add cost and paperwork. Amounts not verified.
- Where families get stuck or denied: Families file for conservatorship when the only income is SSI or SSDI; a representative payee plus an ABLE account may meet the need.
- Screener rule: IF assets_or_income_beyond_SSA = yes AND person_cannot_manage = yes AND no_POA = yes THEN "conservatorship may be needed; ask a lawyer about a limited conservatorship or a trust".
- Sources: [RSMo 475.075](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=475.075), [Chapter 475 index](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneChapter.aspx?chapter=475), checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: Medium (sections 475.061, 475.100, 475.270 were seen in the index only, not read)

### Durable power of attorney (financial) and durable power of attorney for health care / health care directive
- Run by: Private documents under RSMo 404.700 to 404.737 (Durable Power of Attorney Law of Missouri) and 404.800 to 404.872 (health care). Declarations about life-prolonging treatment: RSMo Chapter 459.
- Serves: An adult who has the capacity to sign the document. It must be signed before capacity is lost; a guardianship court gives weight to a DPOA "executed by the respondent before the petition was filed" (475.075.13(1)).
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year): A financial power of attorney is "durable" only if it is called a "Durable Power of Attorney", contains the statutory durability sentence, and is "subscribed by the principal, and dated and acknowledged in the manner prescribed by law for conveyances of real estate" (that is, notarized) (RSMo 404.705.1, effective 1997). Health care: unless the document says otherwise, the agent's health care powers start when two licensed physicians certify incapacity (one may be the attending physician) (RSMo 404.825). Authority to withdraw artificially supplied nutrition and hydration must be granted specifically (404.820).
- Prerequisites: Capacity to understand and sign; a willing agent; a notary.
- Cost to family: Notary fee; lawyer fee if used. Not verified.
- Where families get stuck or denied: Doubt about whether a young adult with IDD has capacity to sign; no Missouri rule sets a capacity test for a POA in the sources checked. Banks and providers may refuse a POA.
- Appeal rights: None (private document). Disputes go to circuit court.
- Screener rule: IF age >= 18 AND can_understand_choosing_a_helper = yes THEN "a durable POA and health care POA may avoid guardianship".
- Sources: [RSMo 404.705](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=404.705), [404.820](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=404.820), [404.825](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=404.825), checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: High for the quoted sections; Medium for completeness (other sections of 404.700 to 404.872 not read).

### Supported decision-making (SDM) agreement
- Run by: No agency. Missouri has no statute that defines or sets a form for an SDM agreement; Chapter 475 names "Supported decision-making agreements" only as a less restrictive alternative that a court must consider (RSMo 475.075.13(4), added by SB 806, 2018). RSMo 475.010 has no definition of the term.
- Serves: Any adult who can make decisions with help.
- Eligibility rules: None in statute. The adult chooses supporters and signs. Combine with releases (FERPA, HIPAA), a POA, and an SSA payee as needed.
- Where families get stuck or denied: Third parties (hospitals, banks, schools) have no Missouri statute that requires them to honor an SDM agreement. Some states have such a statute; Missouri does not, based on the sources checked.
- Appeal rights: None.
- Screener rule: IF adult_can_decide_with_help = yes THEN "SDM agreement plus releases first".
- Sources: [RSMo 475.075](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=475.075), [RSMo 475.010](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=475.010), checked 2026-10-26; [ICI Impact, "Supported Decision-Making: The Missouri Approach" (secondary)](https://publications.ici.umn.edu/impact/32-1/supported-decision-making-consensus-and-action-the-missouri-approach)
- Confidence: High that no definition exists in 475.010 and 475.075; Medium that no other Missouri statute defines SDM (a full search of RSMo was not done).

### ADA complementary paratransit eligibility (City Utilities Access Express, Springfield)
- Run by: City Utilities of Springfield, Transit Services (Access Express). Federal rule: ADA Title II, 49 CFR Part 37 Subpart F. Final eligibility appeals go to the Ozarks Transportation Organization (OTO). Service detail: agent 08.
- Serves: People with any disability (physical, intellectual, mental, vision) who cannot use the fixed-route bus for some or all trips; eligibility can be permanent, temporary, or limited to some trips (49 CFR 37.123(b) and (c)).
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year): Three federal categories (49 CFR 37.123(e)): (1) cannot board, ride, or get off an accessible bus without help from another person (other than the lift operator), including people who cannot navigate the system because of a cognitive or vision impairment; (2) needs a lift or ramp and no accessible vehicle is available on the route at that time; (3) a "specific impairment-related condition" prevents travel to or from a stop. Distance, terrain, or weather alone do not qualify, but their interaction with the impairment can. Service rules (49 CFR 37.131): corridor of 3/4 mile on each side of each fixed route; next-day scheduling; pickup may be negotiated within one hour of the requested time; fare may not exceed twice the full fixed-route fare; same days and hours as the bus; no trip-purpose limits; no wait lists, no trip caps, and no pattern of untimely pickups or trip denials. A personal care attendant rides free.
- Prerequisites: None beyond living in or visiting the service area.
- Documents and evidence needed: Access Express application; professional verification may be asked. The application and notices must be available in accessible formats on request (37.125(b)).
- How to apply in SW Missouri: Application on the City Utilities Access Express page. Transit Services 417-831-8711; Customer Service 417-831-8782 (Mon to Fri, 8 am to 5 pm).
- Typical time or wait: Decision within 21 days of a complete application; if no decision by day 21, the applicant "shall be treated as eligible and provided service" until a denial (49 CFR 37.125(c)).
- Cost to family: Access Express fare $2.00 a ride in 2026 (City Utilities page); MO HealthNet participants who qualify get non-emergency medical trips free.
- Where families get stuck or denied: (1) Conditional or trip-by-trip eligibility that the family does not understand. (2) Denials based on a short functional test that does not capture cognitive or seizure-related limits. (3) Suspension for no-shows; trips missed for reasons beyond the rider's control may not count (37.125(h)(1)). (4) People outside the 3/4-mile corridor have no ADA right to service.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: Federal: the transit agency may require an appeal within 60 days of the denial; the appeal must include a chance to be heard, a decision-maker not involved in the first decision, and a written decision with reasons; no service is required during the appeal, but if there is no decision within 30 days after the appeal process is complete, service must be given until a denial (49 CFR 37.125(g)). Access Express process: within 60 days of receiving the determination letter, send a letter (you or your physician) to the Access Express Coordinator, City Utilities Transit Services, 1505 N. Boonville, Springfield, MO 65803; decision within 7 days; final appeal within 7 days, in writing or by phone, to the OTO Executive Director, 2208 W. Chesterfield Blvd., Suite 101, Springfield, MO 65807, 417-865-3042 ext. 102; final decision within 14 days. Suspension appeals: within 14 days of the written suspension notice; the suspension is stayed during the appeal. An advocate may represent the rider.
- Screener questions: Does the disability stop the person from using the city bus for some or all trips? Does the person live or travel within the City Utilities bus service area?
- Screener rule: IF lives_in_CU_service_area = yes AND cannot_use_bus_some_trips = yes THEN "apply for Access Express; decision due in 21 days or service starts".
- Sources: [49 CFR 37.123, 37.125, 37.131 (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-A/part-37/subpart-F), checked 2026-10-26; [City Utilities Access Express](https://www.cityutilities.net/accessexpress), checked 2026-10-26; [Access Express Eligibility Appeals Process (PDF)](https://www.cityutilities.net/DocumentCenter/View/325), [Suspension Appeals Process (PDF)](https://www.cityutilities.net/DocumentCenter/View/324), [Policies and Procedures (PDF)](https://mo-springfieldutilities.civicplus.com/DocumentCenter/View/343), seen through search summaries 2026-10-26 (PDF text not opened directly)
- Confidence: High for federal rules; Medium for City Utilities appeal steps (summary of the PDFs, not read line by line).

### Vocational Rehabilitation (Missouri VR, DESE) appeals and order of selection
- Run by: DESE, Office of Adult Learning and Rehabilitation Services, Vocational Rehabilitation (MVR). Rules: 5 CSR 20-500. Federal: 34 CFR 361.36 (order of selection), 361.48 (Pre-ETS), 361.57 (review). Overlap: agent 07 covers services, Pre-ETS, and current category status.
- Serves: Working-age people with disabilities who need services to get or keep a job; Pre-ETS for students with disabilities.
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year): Order of selection (5 CSR 20-500.160, amended effective 30 Aug 2024): Category I, "most significant disability", serious limits in three or more of seven functional areas (self-care, communication, mobility, self-direction, work tolerance, work skills, interpersonal skills); Category II, significant disability, one or two areas and multiple services over an extended time; Category III, all other eligible. Order within a category is by application date. Services already authorized "shall not be disrupted" by the order of selection; Pre-ETS for students who got them before being found eligible continue even if placed in a closed category (500.160(12) and (14)). A person at immediate risk of losing a job is not subject to the order of selection for the services needed to keep it (500.160(8)). Agent 07 reports Category II is closed (wait list) in 2026.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: No deadline to request review appears in 5 CSR 20-500.170 to .190 (the DESE FAQ says "any time"). Services under an existing Individualized Plan for Employment (IPE) continue during review unless the person asks in writing to stop them (5 CSR 20-500.170(4); 34 CFR 361.57(b)(4)). Category assignment is appealable (500.160(4)). Requests: info@vr.dese.mo.gov or the VR Central Office, 573-751-3251, toll-free 877-222-8963. Help: Client Assistance Program (CAP) at Missouri Protection and Advocacy Services, 800-392-8667.
- Screener rule: IF vr_denied_or_waitlisted = yes THEN "you can ask for review at any time; CAP can help free". IF at_risk_of_losing_job = yes THEN "ask VR for job-retention services outside the wait list".
- Sources: [5 CSR 20-500 (current, 6/30/26 printing)](https://www.sos.mo.gov/cmsimages/adrules/csr/current/5csr/5c20-500.pdf), checked 2026-10-26; [34 CFR 361.57 (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-361.57), checked 2026-10-26; [MVR FAQ](https://dese.mo.gov/adult-learning-rehabilitation-services/vocational-rehabilitation/vr-frequently-asked-questions-faq), checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: High

### County developmental disability boards (SB40 boards), RSMo 205.968 to 205.972
- Run by: A county board of nine directors appointed by the county commission after voters approve a levy; two must be related to a person with a disability; at least seven must live in the county (RSMo 205.970.1). Agent 01 lists the boards in the region.

### Civil rights backstop: ADA Title II, Section 504, Olmstead integration, Fair Housing Act reasonable accommodation
- Run by: Federal agencies and courts. ADA Title II complaints: any designated federal agency, a funding agency, or the U.S. Department of Justice (28 CFR 35.170). Housing: HUD Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity or a HUD-certified state or local agency (24 CFR 103.30). State law: Missouri Commission on Human Rights (RSMo Chapter 213).
- Serves: Any person with a disability dealing with a state or local government program (Title II), a program that gets federal money (Section 504), or housing (Fair Housing Act).
- Eligibility rules: Olmstead v. L.C., 527 U.S. 581 (1999): unjustified isolation in an institution is discrimination under Title II; a state must serve a person in the community when treatment professionals find it appropriate, the person does not oppose it, and it can be reasonably accommodated given state resources and the needs of others. A federal court in Missouri has held that people at risk of institutionalization (not only people already in an institution) can bring an integration claim (Hiltibran v. Levy, 793 F. Supp. 2d 1108 (W.D. Mo. 2011), citing Fisher v. Oklahoma Health Care Authority, 335 F.3d 1175 (10th Cir. 2003)). Fair housing: it is unlawful to refuse "reasonable accommodations in rules, policies, practices, or services" needed for equal use of a dwelling (24 CFR 100.204).
- Appeal rights and deadlines: ADA Title II administrative complaint: within 180 days of the discrimination, unless extended for good cause (28 CFR 35.170(b)). HUD fair housing complaint: within one year (42 USC 3610(a)(1)(A)(i)); federal or state court suit within two years (42 USC 3613(a)(1)(A)). Missouri Commission on Human Rights: verified complaint within 180 days (RSMo 213.075.1) (the quoted text names employment and public accommodations; housing filing rules not checked). A lawsuit under Title II or Section 504 does not require an administrative complaint first. School cases: A.J.T. v. Osseo Area Schools, 605 U.S. 335 (2025), rejected the Eighth Circuit's "bad faith or gross misjudgment" test; ADA and Section 504 claims about education use the same standards as other disability claims.
- Screener rule: IF denied_service_because_of_disability OR at_risk_of_institution_because_services_missing THEN "civil rights complaint may be possible; contact Missouri Protection and Advocacy Services".
- Sources: [28 CFR 35.170](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-28/section-35.170), [24 CFR 100.204](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-100.204), [24 CFR 103.30](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-103.30), [42 USC 3610](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/3610), [42 USC 3613](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/3613), [RSMo 213.075](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=213.075), all checked 2026-10-26; case links in the case law table below.
- Confidence: High

## 2. Order and dependencies
1. DMH DD eligibility (9 CSR 45-2.010) comes before any DD waiver, PON score, or DD-funded service. Medicaid is not needed for DD eligibility.
2. An ISP comes before Utilization Review; UR and a PON score come before wait list placement (9 CSR 45-2.017(2)).
3. MO HealthNet eligibility comes before the Comprehensive, Community Support, and Partnership for Hope waivers. MOCDD (under 18) gives Medicaid through the waiver.
4. Medicaid State Plan services must be used first when they meet the need (9 CSR 45-2.017(14)(B)). A state plan denial (for example equipment or personal care) is a separate MO HealthNet appeal.
5. Partnership for Hope priority for employment services needs proof that school and VR benefits are used up or that the person is not VR eligible (9 CSR 45-2.015(3)(A)2.B). So a VR application (or denial) may come before PfH employment funding.
6. Transfer of IDEA rights happens at 18 (DESE Regulation V, section L). Plan decision-making supports (releases, SDM, POA) in the IEP year before 18, because 34 CFR 300.320(c) requires the notice one year before.
7. A durable power of attorney must be signed before a guardianship petition is filed to count fully as an alternative (RSMo 475.075.13(1)), and only while the person has capacity to sign.
8. SSI age-18 redetermination happens in the year after the 18th birthday (20 CFR 416.987). An IEP or VR plan must be in place before the disability is found to have ended for Section 301 continuation (20 CFR 416.1338(a)(2)).
9. A court must consider less restrictive alternatives before appointing a guardian or conservator (475.075.13); a limited guardianship must be considered before full guardianship (475.080).
10. Appointment of a guardian revokes an earlier health care power of attorney unless the court orders otherwise (475.361.3). Choose the order of documents with care.
11. For every adverse notice: the 10-day clock (to keep benefits) comes before the 60-day or 90-day clock (to appeal at all).

## 3. Gaps and failure points
- Short "keep your benefits" windows. MO HealthNet: 10 days from the mailing date (RSMo 208.080.3). DHSS HCBS: 10 calendar days (manual) or 10 business days (form). SSI: 10 days after receipt. Paratransit: no service during appeal. Mail time eats the window.
- Repayment risk deters appeals. MO HealthNet (42 CFR 431.230(b)), DMH waiver (9 CSR 45-2.017(11)(A)), and DHSS (manual) can recover the cost of services given during a losing appeal.
- No legal clock on the DD waiver wait list. The rules set times for eligibility (30 business days) and UR (6 plus 5 business days), but no time limit to move from the wait list to a funded slot. PON score and funding decide.
- Two DD waiver appeal tracks that cancel each other: starting a DSS hearing ends DMH appeal rights (9 CSR 45-2.017(10)(A)). Families may pick the wrong track.
- Inconsistent deadlines across agencies: VR has no request deadline; RSB has 90 days; DMH has 30 days per step; Medicaid has 10 and 90 days; SSA has 10 and 60 days; paratransit has 60 then 7 days; HUD has 1 year; ADA Title II and MCHR have 180 days; IDEA due process has 2 years and state complaints 1 year.
- SB40 county boards: services depend on the county; no statutory appeal right for a denied person (RSMo 205.968.2 discretion).
- Guardianship pressure at 18: IDEA rights transfer at 18; Missouri has no statute that defines or requires third parties to honor supported decision-making agreements; schools, hospitals, and banks may push families to court. A form judgment can recite that alternatives were considered (example: Crocker, 2021).
- Guardianship costs (court costs, respondent's attorney fee taxed as costs) fall on the family; local fee amounts were not available from official Greene County sources online (see Open questions).
- Enforcement gap: after Medina (2025) and Armstrong (2015), private suits to enforce Medicaid Act provisions such as reasonable promptness are harder. The ADA and Section 504 (Olmstead) remain available, and so do fair hearings.
- New 2027 Medicaid work rule: caregivers and people with disabilities on expansion Medicaid are exempt under 42 CFR 435.554, but the "ability to comply" test and state documentation demands may cause wrongful loss of coverage.

## 4. Recommendations from your field
1. Family and provider: on every notice, write the mailing date and day 10 on the envelope. Appeal and ask for continued benefits within 10 days, in writing, and keep proof. (Family, provider, support coordinator.)
2. Provider and support coordinator: in the IEP year before 18, give a packet with releases, an SDM agreement template, a durable POA and health care POA referral, and SSA representative payee information; record it in the ISP. (Provider, county board, school district.)
3. Probate court: adopt a local form that requires the petition to list alternatives tried and why they failed, and that offers limited guardianship as the default. (Circuit Court, 31st Circuit; Missouri Supreme Court for statewide forms.)
4. Legislature: enact a supported decision-making agreement statute that defines the agreement, gives a form, and requires third parties to accept it, as other states have done. (Missouri General Assembly, amend RSMo Chapter 475.)
5. Legislature or DMH rule: set a maximum time for moving people with emergency criteria from the wait list to funded services, and publish wait list data by region and PON score. (DMH rule change to 9 CSR 45-2.015 and 2.017; General Assembly appropriations.)
6. DSS and DHSS: align "keep your benefits" windows to 10 days from the date of receipt (not mailing), and state calendar or business days the same way in every notice and form. (DSS and DHSS rule and form changes; RSMo 208.080.3 change for the mailing-date rule.)
7. DMH: amend 9 CSR 45-2.017(10)(A) so that a DSS fair hearing request does not end DMH appeal rights, or require the notice to explain the trade-off in plain language. (DMH rule.)
8. County SB40 boards: adopt and publish a written appeal process for people denied board-funded services. (Each county board; the legislature could require it in RSMo 205.970.)
9. Advocates: use ADA and Section 504 integration claims (Olmstead) for people at risk of institutionalization because of service gaps, since Medicaid Act private enforcement is narrower after 2025. (Protection and Advocacy, legal aid.)
10. DSS: build the 2027 caregiver and medically frail exemptions into data matching (DMH DD eligibility, SSI, waiver enrollment) so families do not have to prove them by paper. (DSS/FSD; CMS approval as needed.)

## 8. Lobbying limits for 501(c)(3) nonprofits (short note; agent 05 covers advocacy)
- A 501(c)(3) may lobby only as an "insubstantial part" of its activities unless it elects the expenditure test under 26 USC 501(h). Under the 501(h) election, the lobbying limit is set by 26 USC 4911(c)(2): 20 percent of the first $500,000 of exempt purpose expenditures, $100,000 plus 15 percent of the next $500,000, $175,000 plus 10 percent of the next $500,000, $225,000 plus 5 percent above $1,500,000, capped at $1,000,000 a year. Grassroots lobbying may be no more than 25 percent of that amount (4911(c)(4)). A 501(c)(3) may not take part in any political campaign for or against a candidate (26 USC 501(c)(3)). Source: [26 USC 4911](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4911), checked 2026-10-26. Confidence: High for 4911(c); the "insubstantial part" and campaign rule are from 501(c)(3) text not opened today (Medium).
