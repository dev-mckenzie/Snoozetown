## 0. Top findings (read this first)

1. **New DD waiver wait list, effective 15 October 2026.** DMH DDD stopped new enrollment into the Partnership for Hope, Community Support and Comprehensive waivers "due to current budget limitations". There is "no estimated end date". People already on a waiver keep it. The MOCDD (children) waiver continues as usual. Source: [DDD memo, 15 Oct 2026](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston541/files/media/pdf/2026/10/wait%20list%20memo%202026.pdf) and [DMH HCBS Waiver Enrollment page](https://dmh.mo.gov/node/10511). Confidence: High.
2. **The FY2027 budget law itself caps new waiver slots.** HB 2010 (2026), Section 10.2015: "No funds shall be expended in furtherance of waiver slots assigned on July 1, 2026 except for" 50 crisis residential slots, 27 nursing home transition slots, 39 slots for children leaving Children's Division waiver slots, and 11 slots for children leaving the MOCDD waiver (127 total). So the fix is a budget fix: the FY2027 supplemental bill (spring 2027) and the FY2028 HB 10 (passed by 7 May 2027). Source: [HB 2010 truly agreed, p. 57](https://documents.house.mo.gov/billtracking/bills261/hlrbillspdf/2010H.06T.pdf). Confidence: High.
3. **The budget law already makes DMH report the unmet need every month.** HB 2010 Section 10.3005 requires monthly reports to the House Budget and Senate Appropriations chairs and ranking members, including "the number of individuals presenting for service that have not been assigned a waiver slot" and the cost to serve them. Advocates can ask legislators for these reports. Same source, p. 59 to 60. Confidence: High.
4. **Springfield lawmakers hold the budget keys for 2027.** Rep. Bill Owen (HD 131, Springfield) is House Budget Chairman. Rep. Darin Chappell (HD 137, Rogersville) chairs the House appropriations subcommittee for Health, Mental Health and Social Services; Rep. John Black (HD 129, Webster County) is vice chair; Rep. Sources: [HB 2010 truly agreed, p. 57 to 58](https://documents.house.mo.gov/billtracking/bills261/hlrbillspdf/2010H.06T.pdf); [Governor press release, 30 Jun 2026](https://governor.mo.gov/press-releases/archive/governor-kehoe-takes-action-fy27-state-budget-bills); [FY27 veto list](https://budplan.oa.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston231/files/media/pdf/2026/06/FY%2027%20Vetoes.pdf). ### Missouri General Assembly: 2027 session calendar
- Run by: Missouri General Assembly (104th General Assembly, first regular session, elected 3 Nov 2026).
- Serves (ages, disability types): all; it writes the laws and the budget for DMH, DHSS, DSS and DESE.
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year): anyone may contact their own legislator or testify at a public hearing. Paid or employed advocates may need to register as lobbyists (see Section 9).
- Prerequisites (what the family must have first): know your House district and Senate district. Use the [Senate Legislator Lookup](https://www.senate.mo.gov/BillTracking/LegislatorLookup/).
- Documents and evidence needed: a short written statement; for committee testimony, written copies as the committee asks (check each hearing notice).
- How to apply in SW Missouri (steps, office, phone, URL): Key dates for 2027, computed from the Missouri Constitution and statute:
  - 3 Nov 2026 (Tuesday): general election. All 163 House seats and the even-numbered Senate seats are on the ballot (source for the election date: [SOS 2026 Ballot Measures](https://www.sos.mo.gov/petitions/2026BallotMeasures)).
  - 1 Dec 2026 (Tuesday): prefiling of bills opens. [RSMo 21.600](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=21.600) defines the filing period as starting "on December first next preceding the opening day".
  - 6 Jan 2027 (Wednesday): session convenes, "the first Wednesday after the first Monday in January". [Mo. Const. Art. III Sec. 20](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=III%20%20%2020&bid=31876).
  - By about 5 Feb 2027: the Governor must submit the budget "within thirty days after it convenes". [Mo. Const. Art. IV Sec. 24](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=IV%20%20%2024&bid=31931). The State of the State address usually carries the budget plan.
  - 60th legislative day: last day to introduce a bill other than an appropriation bill. [Mo. Const. Art. III Sec. 25](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=III%20%20%2025&bid=31882). In 2026 the Senate filing cutoff was 27 Feb (Senate Rule 48) per the [Senate Major Dates page](https://www.senate.mo.gov/BillTracking/MajorDates/).
  - 7 May 2027 (Friday), 6:00 p.m.: no appropriation bill may be taken up after this time ("first Friday following the first Monday in May"). Art. III Sec. 25. This is the FY2028 budget deadline.
  - 14 May 2027 (Friday), 6:00 p.m.: all bills left on the calendar are tabled ("first Friday following the second Monday in May"). [Mo. Const. Art. III Sec. 20(a)](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=III%20%20%2020(a)&bid=31877).
  - After adjournment: the Governor has 45 days to act on bills. [Mo. Const. Art. III Sec. 31](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=III%20%20%2031&bid=31889). In 2026 the budget bills were signed 30 June and the last bills on 13 July ([Senate list of Governor actions, 2026](https://www.senate.mo.gov/BillTracking/BillStatus/GovernorActiononTATFP?year=2026&session=R)).
  - 1 Jul 2027: FY2028 begins (FY2027 bills run 1 Jul 2026 to 30 Jun 2027 per [OA appropriation bill list](https://budplan.oa.mo.gov/appropriation-bills-fiscal-year-2027)).
  - 15 Sep 2027 (Wednesday): veto session, "the first Wednesday following the second Monday in September", up to ten days. [Mo. Const. Art. III Sec. 32](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=III%20%20%2032&bid=31890).
- Typical time or wait: a bill can pass in one session (January to mid May). Most bills do not pass in their first year.
- Cost to family: none to contact a legislator; travel to Jefferson City for hearings.
- Where families get stuck or denied: hearings are posted on short notice; a bill must get a hearing, a committee vote, and floor time in both chambers before 14 May. Budget language is decided in subcommittee and conference, often in March to early May.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: not applicable. The next chance after a lost vote is the veto session or the next year's session.
- Screener questions: not applicable (policy card, not an eligibility program).
- Screener rule: not applicable.
- Sources: links above, checked 2026-10-26. Dates for 2027 are computed by me from the constitutional rules; the House and Senate will post official 2027 calendars.
- Confidence: High (rules); Medium (2027 dates are computed, not yet posted by the legislature).

### DMH DDD waiver wait list (October 2026)
- Run by: DMH Division of Developmental Disabilities (Director Sheila Wunning; Department Director Valerie Huhn), local Regional Office.
- Serves: people with DMH DD eligibility who need a Partnership for Hope (PfH), Community Support (CSW) or Comprehensive (Comp) waiver.
- Eligibility rules: access is by priority of need (PON) 1 to 5, then by date added. PON 1 to 4 comes from the Missouri Adaptive Ability Scale (MAAS) score; PON 5 is crisis under 9 CSR 45-2.017(1)(E) ([DMH page](https://dmh.mo.gov/node/10511); rule: [9 CSR 45-2](https://www.sos.mo.gov/cmsimages/adrules/csr/current/9csr/9c45-2.pdf)).
- Prerequisites: DMH DD eligibility (role 01 covers the detail).
- Documents and evidence needed: role 01.
- How to apply in SW Missouri: Springfield Regional Office, 1515 E. Pythian, Springfield, MO 65801, 417-895-7400; Joplin Satellite Office, 3600 E. Newman Rd., Joplin, MO 64802, 417-629-3020 ([DMH](https://dmh.mo.gov/dev-disabilities/regional-offices/springfield)).
- Typical time or wait: "no estimated end date" (memo, 15 Oct 2026). The monthly wait list report heading exists on the DMH page, but on 26 Oct 2026 it showed no numbers.
- Exceptions: people funded through Inter-Divisional Agreements and people whose participation is mandated by federal rule continue to have access (memo).
- Cost to family: none for the list.
- Where families get stuck: same as role 01. Political point: the cap is in the budget law, so only a supplemental appropriation, a change in Part 2 language, or the FY2028 budget can lift it.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: role 01 and role 04.
- Screener questions: Does the person have DMH DD eligibility? Is the person in crisis (PON 5)? Is the person leaving a nursing home, Children's Division placement, or the MOCDD waiver?
- Screener rule: IF has DD eligibility AND needs PfH, CSW or Comp AND NOT already enrolled THEN "likely placed on wait list as of 15 Oct 2026; ask Regional Office about PON and exceptions".
- Sources: [DDD memo](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston541/files/media/pdf/2026/10/wait%20list%20memo%202026.pdf); [DMH page](https://dmh.mo.gov/node/10511); [HB 2010 Sec. 10.2015](https://documents.house.mo.gov/billtracking/bills261/hlrbillspdf/2010H.06T.pdf); checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High.

### Missouri Ethics Commission lobbyist rules (for staff and paid advocates)
- Run by: Missouri Ethics Commission (registration and reports); law in Chapter 105 RSMo.
- Serves: anyone who lobbies the legislature, the executive branch, or certain local officials.
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year): A "legislative lobbyist" includes a person who acts to influence the General Assembly and (a) does it in the ordinary course of employment whose primary purpose is to influence legislation on a regular basis (not "occasional basis only"), or (b) is paid for it, or (c) is designated as a lobbyist by an entity including a "nonprofit corporation", or (d) spends $50 or more in a calendar year for the benefit of public officials or legislative employees. An "executive lobbyist" has parallel tests for the executive branch, including "acting in the ordinary course of employment on behalf of ... such person's employer". An "elected local government official lobbyist" is a person "employed specifically" to influence a local elected official in a county or city with an annual operating budget over $10 million. ([RSMo 105.470](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=105.470))
- Not lobbying by itself: "Testifying as a witness before the general assembly or any committee thereof"; responding to a request for information from a legislator; for the executive branch, "Participating in public hearings or public proceedings on rules, grants, or other matters" (RSMo 105.470).
- Registration: by 5 January each year "or five days after beginning any activities as a lobbyist"; fee $10. Monthly reports are due "at the close of business on the tenth day of the following month" and list expenditures on officials, staff and families by category ([RSMo 105.473](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=105.473)).
- Gift ban: no member or employee of the General Assembly may accept a gift "of any tangible or intangible item, service, or thing of value from any paid lobbyist or lobbyist principal" ([Mo. Const. Art. III Sec. 2(b)](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=III%20%20%20%202&bid=31862)). No dollar threshold appears in the text I read.
- Practical rule: unpaid parents speaking for their own family are generally not lobbyists unless designated by an organization or spending $50 or more on officials. A case manager or agency staff member who contacts legislators as part of the job should ask the employer whether to register. Role 04 should confirm.
- Confidence: High (statute text read today).

### IRS rules for 501(c)(3) lobbying and elections
- Run by: Internal Revenue Service.
- Rules: A 501(c)(3) may lobby but must not intervene in any candidate campaign. The IRS says such groups are "absolutely prohibited from directly or indirectly participating in, or intervening in, any political campaign" for or against a candidate; nonpartisan voter education, candidate forums, voter registration and get out the vote drives are allowed when nonpartisan ([IRS page](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/the-restriction-of-political-campaign-intervention-by-section-501c3-tax-exempt-organizations)).
- 501(h) election: file IRS Form 5768. The lobbying limit is 20% of the first $500,000 of exempt purpose expenditures; $100,000 plus 15% of the next $500,000; $175,000 plus 10% of the next $500,000; $225,000 plus 5% of the rest; never more than $1,000,000. Over the limit: 25% excise tax on the excess; repeated excess over four years can cost exempt status ([IRS expenditure test](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/measuring-lobbying-activity-expenditure-test)). The grassroots limit is 25% of the lobbying limit ([26 U.S.C. 4911(c)(4)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4911)).
- What counts: direct lobbying is a communication with a legislator or legislative staff about specific legislation; grassroots lobbying is asking the public to contact legislators about specific legislation (a "call to action" is required). Ballot measure advocacy counts as direct lobbying because "the general public ... constitutes the legislative body". "Legislative body" does not include executive, judicial, or administrative bodies, so advocacy to DMH, DSS, or DHSS on rules and rates is not lobbying under 501(h). Nonpartisan analysis and discussion of broad social problems are excluded ([26 CFR 56.4911-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/56.4911-2); [26 U.S.C. 4911(d)(2)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/4911)).
- Confidence: High.
