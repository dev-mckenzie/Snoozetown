# 07 Transition and employment findings
Checked: 2026-10-26. Area covered: Greene, Christian, Webster, Polk, Dallas, Dade, Douglas, Laclede, Wright, Stone, Taney counties (the Springfield North and Springfield South Missouri VR office areas), with notes on the nearby Joplin (Barry, Barton, Jasper, Lawrence, McDonald, Newton), West Plains (Carter, Howell, Oregon, Ozark, Ripley, Shannon, Texas) and Nevada (Bates, Cass, Cedar, Hickory, St. Clair, Vernon) VR office areas.

Writing notes: Program names are spelled out on first use. "MVR" means Missouri Vocational Rehabilitation (DESE). "RSB" means Rehabilitation Services for the Blind (Department of Social Services, Family Support Division). "DMH DD" means the Department of Mental Health, Division of Developmental Disabilities. "IEP" means Individualized Education Program. "IPE" means Individualized Plan for Employment (the VR plan). "Pre-ETS" means Pre-Employment Transition Services. "CRP" means community rehabilitation program (a VR-contracted employment provider).

## Top findings (summary for the lead)

1. **MVR has a wait list now.** MVR is under an order of selection. Priority Categories II and III closed on December 9, 2024. Only Category I (most significant disability) is open. MVR estimates 5,204 eligible people will not get services in federal fiscal year 2027. Source: MVR section of the Missouri Combined State Plan modifications, PY 2026-2027 (draft, posted February 2026).
2. **Pre-ETS is not blocked by the wait list.** Students age 14-21 with an IEP or 504 plan can get Pre-ETS as "potentially eligible" without a VR application. A student who got Pre-ETS before being found eligible keeps getting Pre-ETS even if placed in a closed category. Families who wait until after graduation to apply lose this protection.
3. **Missouri transition planning starts earlier from 2027-28.** The Missouri State Plan for Special Education (Part B, effective July 30, 2026) keeps the age 16 rule for now, but from the 2027-28 school year transition services must be in the IEP "before the student exits eighth grade."
4. **Missouri school services stop at age 21, with no "finish the school year" rule in the State Plan.** The State Plan says public agencies are not required to provide FAPE to "youth with disabilities who reach the age of 21." A regular diploma also ends services. A certificate of attendance or a graduation ceremony without a regular diploma does not.
5. **The handoff to long-term job support is the weakest link, and it just got weaker.** MVR pays for supported employment only until case closure (on-the-job supports up to 24 months, extendable). After that, "the CRP or other identified provider is responsible for securing long-term funding." For most adults with IDD that funder is a DMH DD waiver (Partnership for Hope, Community Support, or Comprehensive). Effective October 15, 2026, DMH DD put all three on a wait list for new enrollment, with "no estimated end date" (DDD memo, Oct 15, 2026; agent 01 has detail). A 2026-27 school leaver with IDD can now face a closed MVR category (if not Category I) and a closed waiver at the same time. Category I students can still get VR job placement, but the long-term support that keeps the job may have no payer.
6. **No Project SEARCH or employer-based transition training site was found in SW Missouri.** Springfield Public Schools runs a school-based job-shadow program (B.A.S.E. Project, age 16+ with IEP). Missouri State University runs Bear POWER, a five-semester nondegree certificate program for students with IDD.

## 1. Program and resource cards

### Missouri special education transition rules (IDEA Part B, Missouri State Plan)
- Run by: Local school district (the "public agency" or LEA). DESE Office of Special Education monitors compliance.
- Serves (ages, disability types): Students with an IEP, ages 3 to 21. Transition rules apply from age 16 now, and from before exit from 8th grade starting in the 2027-28 school year.
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year):
  - Transition services must be in the IEP "beginning not later than the first IEP to be in effect when the student turns 16, or younger if determined appropriate by the IEP team, and updated annually thereafter." (State Plan Regulation IV, effective July 30, 2026.)
  - "Effective the 2027-28 school year, postsecondary transition services must be included in the IEP before the student exits eighth grade, or younger if determined appropriate by the IEP team." (Same source.)
  - The plan must have measurable postsecondary goals based on age-appropriate transition assessments in training, education, employment, and where appropriate independent living skills, plus the transition services and courses of study needed to reach them.
  - Age of majority notice: "Beginning not later than one year before the student reaches age 18, IEPs must include a statement that the student has been informed of his or her rights under Part B of IDEA and that those rights will transfer to the student upon reaching the age of majority." In practice this is the IEP in effect at age 17.
  - Transfer of rights at 18: "When a student with a disability reaches age 18 ... All other rights accorded to parents under Part B of IDEA transfer to the student." The school sends notices to both student and parents. "The transfer does not apply if the student is declared incompetent by a court of competent jurisdiction." (Regulation V, section L.) Legal detail: see agent 04.
  - Summary of Performance: "A public agency must provide a student whose eligibility terminates due to graduation from the public agency with a regular diploma or due to reaching the age of twenty-one (21) a summary of the student's academic achievement and functional performance, which shall include recommendations on how to assist the student in meeting the student's post secondary goals." (Regulation III.)
  - When services end: FAPE is not required for "Youth with disabilities who reach the age of 21" or for students who graduate "with a regular high school diploma." A certificate of attendance or High School Equivalency (HSE) certificate is not a regular diploma. Students who "participated in a graduation ceremony" or got an HSE "but have not been awarded a regular high school diploma, continue to be eligible to receive FAPE if they are under twenty-one (21) years of age." Graduation with a regular diploma "constitutes a change in placement, requiring prior written notice." (Regulation IV.) Missouri statute defines children with disabilities as "children under the age of twenty-one years who have not completed an approved high school program" (RSMo 162.675).
  - Agency invitations: "with the consent of the parents or a student who has reached the age of majority," the school must invite a representative of any agency "likely to be responsible for providing or paying for transition services" (for example MVR, RSB, DMH DD).
  - If an outside agency fails to deliver a transition service in the IEP, the school must reconvene the IEP team "to identify alternative strategies."
- Prerequisites (what the family must have first): An IEP. (Students with only a 504 plan do not get IDEA transition plans, but can get Pre-ETS.)
- Documents and evidence needed: None from the family to start. Ask for transition assessment results, the IEP transition page (Missouri IEP Form C), and the Summary of Performance in writing.
- How to apply in SW Missouri (steps, office, phone, URL): Ask the student's case manager or district special education director to hold an IEP meeting that addresses transition. Ask in writing that MVR (and RSB if blind or low vision, and DMH DD if eligible) be invited. Give written consent for each agency invitation.
- Typical time or wait: Must be in place by the first IEP in effect at 16 (now), or before exit from 8th grade (2027-28 onward).
- Cost to family: None.
- Where families get stuck or denied: Goals copied from templates and not based on assessment; no agency invited because consent was never asked; diploma awarded at 18 without the family understanding it ends all services (including transition services) that day; Summary of Performance not given or too thin to use for VR or college disability services; family not told that rights transfer at 18 (see agent 04 for guardianship and supported decision-making).
- Appeal rights and deadlines: IDEA procedural safeguards (state complaint, mediation, due process). The State Plan Regulation V governs. Diploma is a change of placement that needs prior written notice, so families can dispute it before it is issued. Detail on procedures: see agent 04.
- Screener questions: Does the person have an IEP now? What is the person's age? Is the person in 8th grade or above? Will the person get a regular diploma, and when? Has the person turned 17?
- Screener rule: has IEP AND (age >= 16 OR (school year >= 2027-28 AND grade <= 8 at exit)) -> "transition plan required now." has IEP AND age = 17 -> "IEP must state rights transfer at 18." has IEP AND age < 21 AND no regular diploma -> "still eligible for school services."
- Sources: [DESE State Plan for Special Education page (Part B effective July 30, 2026)](https://dese.mo.gov/state-plan-special-education), [Regulation IV FAPE/IEP/LRE, revised July 2026](https://dese.mo.gov/media/pdf/regulation-iv-fapeieplre-state-plan-special-education-part-b), [Regulation III Identification and Evaluation](https://dese.mo.gov/media/pdf/regulation-iii-identification-and-evaluation-state-plan-special-education-part-b), [Regulation V Procedural Safeguards](https://dese.mo.gov/media/pdf/regulation-v-procedural-safeguardsdiscipline-state-plan-special-education-part-b), [RSMo 162.675](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=162.675), all checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: High

### Missouri Vocational Rehabilitation (MVR), general program
- Run by: DESE, Office of Adult Learning and Rehabilitation Services, Missouri Vocational Rehabilitation.
- Serves (ages, disability types): People with any disability except those served by RSB (blind or visually impaired). Youth services ages 14-24; adults with no upper age limit stated.
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year): Missouri rule 5 CSR 20-500.150: (A) a disability diagnosis by a qualified professional; (B) a physical or mental impairment that is a substantial impediment to employment and can benefit from an employment outcome; (C) requires VR services to prepare for, secure, retain, advance in, or regain employment; (D) Missouri resident (no length of residency); (E) authorized to work in the United States. People who are deaf or hard of hearing need an audiologist or ear physician evaluation; hearing loss must be at least mild to moderate in both ears with functional limits. Federal rule 34 CFR 361.42(a)(3): anyone found eligible for SSI or SSDI (title XVI or title II) is "presumed eligible" and "considered an individual with a significant disability." Federal rule 34 CFR 361.41(b)(1): "an eligibility determination must be made within 60 days" after application, unless an agreed extension or a trial work exploration.
- **Order of selection (wait list), 2026:** "The VR agency is implementing an order of selection with one or more priority categories closed." "Priority Category II ... and Priority Category III ... closed on December 9, 2024." Category I (most significant disability, "seriously limited in three or more" functional areas: self-care, communication, mobility, self-direction, work tolerance, work skills, interpersonal skills) is served. FFY 2027 estimates: Category I 14,745 eligible, 0 not served; Category II 14,449 eligible, 4,998 not served; Category III 580 eligible, 205 not served. Order within a category is by date of application. Exceptions: diagnostic and eligibility services are not affected; Pre-ETS continues for students who were getting Pre-ETS before being placed in a closed category; people at immediate risk of losing a job can get the specific services or equipment needed to keep it.
- Prerequisites (what the family must have first): Proof of disability. For SSI or SSDI recipients, proof of benefits. Parent or guardian signs if under 18 or if under guardianship.
- Documents and evidence needed: Disability documentation (medical, psychological, school evaluation, IEP or 504 plan), proof of SSI or SSDI award if any, ID and work authorization, Missouri residence. For some services MVR applies a financial needs test; a parent or guardian completes the financial application when the client is a dependent (5 CSR 20-500 definitions). Release form: [VR Common Release Form](https://dese.mo.gov/adult-learning-rehabilitation-services/vocational-rehabilitation/media/pdf/vr-common-release-form).
- How to apply in SW Missouri (steps, office, phone, URL): Use the online "Getting Started" form linked from the [MVR home page](https://dese.mo.gov/adult-learning-rehabilitation-services/vocational-rehabilitation) or call the local office. MVR assigns a counselor to every public school district (554 districts). Springfield offices:
  - **Springfield North:** 613 East Kearney, Springfield, MO 65803. Phone 417-895-5863, toll-free 1-877-222-8965. Serves Dade, Dallas, Douglas, Laclede, Polk, Webster, Wright counties and Greene County ZIP codes 65801, 65802, 65803, 65806, 65807.
  - **Springfield South:** 1735 W. Catalpa, Suite C, Springfield, MO 65807. Phone 417-895-5720, toll-free 1-877-222-8967. Serves Christian, Stone, Taney counties and Greene County ZIP codes 65804, 65805, 65808, 65809, 65810, 65814, 65817, 65619.
  - **Joplin:** 801 East 15th Street, Suite B, Joplin, MO 64804-0922. Phone 417-629-3067, toll-free 1-877-222-8964. Barry, Barton, Jasper, Lawrence, McDonald, Newton.
  - **West Plains:** 3417 Division Drive, Suite 2, West Plains, MO 65775. Phone 417-895-6400, toll-free 1-877-222-8959. Carter, Howell, Oregon, Ozark, Ripley, Shannon, Texas.
  - **Nevada:** 621 East Highland, Suite 2, Nevada, MO 64772-3971. Phone 417-448-1332, toll-free 1-800-598-3471. Bates, Cass, Cedar, Hickory, St. Clair, Vernon.

  - State office: phone 573-751-3251, toll-free 1-877-222-8963, email info@vr.dese.mo.gov. (The MVR web page lists 205 Jefferson St., Jefferson City, MO 65101 as main office; current brochures list 3024 Dupont Circle, Jefferson City, MO 65109-6188.)
- Typical time or wait: Eligibility within 60 days of application (federal rule). IPE within 90 days of eligibility, and for students before they exit high school (VR-SEA agreement FY26 and State Plan). Category II and III: wait list of unknown length; the state plan gives no projected opening date. Average months to an employment outcome: Category I about 23, Category II about 40, Category III about 32 (state plan estimate).
- Cost to family: "Most MVR services are provided at no cost," but "payment for some VR services is based on individual and family income/resources." Comparable benefits (insurance, Medicaid, Pell grants) must be used first.
- Where families get stuck or denied: Placed in Category II and put on the wait list (common for people with one or two functional limits, for example some learning disabilities, ADHD, mild hearing loss, or some physical disabilities); family does not appeal the category; student applies after graduation and loses the Pre-ETS carryover protection; family not told SSI/SSDI recipients are presumed eligible; deaf or hard of hearing applicants lack a current audiogram.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: Informal review "within 20 days of the request." Due process hearing "within 60 days of the request." Written decision within 30 days of the hearing. Either party may appeal to the Commissioner of Education "within 20 days of the decision." Then civil action. Mediation available. Services under an existing IPE continue during appeal. Category assignment can be appealed. Requests go to info@vr.dese.mo.gov. Client Assistance Program (CAP): Missouri Protection and Advocacy Services, 925 S. Country Club Drive, Jefferson City, MO 65109-0352, toll-free 800-392-8667.
- Screener questions: Does the person have a disability that makes it hard to get or keep a job? Does the person want to work? Is the person a Missouri resident authorized to work in the US? Does the person get SSI or SSDI? Is the person blind or visually impaired (send to RSB)? How many of these areas are seriously limited: self-care, communication, mobility, self-direction, work tolerance, work skills, interpersonal skills?
- Screener rule: wants to work AND has disability that impedes employment AND MO resident AND work authorized AND NOT primarily blind/low vision -> "may be eligible for MVR." gets SSI or SSDI -> "presumed eligible, at least Category II." serious limits in >= 3 areas -> "likely Category I, served now." serious limits in 1-2 areas -> "likely Category II, wait list as of 2026." 
- Sources: [MVR home](https://dese.mo.gov/adult-learning-rehabilitation-services/vocational-rehabilitation), [MVR offices](https://dese.mo.gov/adult-learning-rehabilitation-services/vocational-rehabilitation/vr-offices), [MVR section, Combined State Plan modifications PY 2026-2027 (draft)](https://dese.mo.gov/media/pdf/vr-section-combined-plan-py-26-27), [VR Due Process Brochure](https://dese.mo.gov/adult-learning-rehabilitation-services/media/pdf/vr-due-process-brochure), [5 CSR 20-500.150 (LII copy)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/missouri/5-CSR-20-500-150), [5 CSR 20-500.170 (LII copy)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/missouri/5-CSR-20-500-170), [34 CFR 361.42](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/34/361.42), [34 CFR 361.41](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/34/361.41), [VR-SEA Transition Agreement FY26](https://dese.mo.gov/adult-learning-rehabilitation-services/vocational-rehabilitation/media/pdf/state-educational-agency-transition-agreement), all checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: High for offices, appeal steps, and order of selection categories (primary sources). Medium for the "closed" status today: the source is the draft PY 2026-2027 plan modification (February 2026) and the SRC 2025 annual report; no newer public notice of reopening was found.

### Pre-Employment Transition Services (Pre-ETS), MVR and University of Missouri
- Run by: MVR. MVR contracts with the University of Missouri (MU) College of Education for Pre-ETS specialists statewide. RSB provides Pre-ETS for blind or visually impaired students. Some Centers for Independent Living (CILs) and CRPs also deliver Pre-ETS.
- Serves (ages, disability types): Students with disabilities ages 14-21 in a secondary, postsecondary, or other recognized education program who have an IEP or a 504 plan (or other documentation of disability).
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year): MVR defines a student with a disability as a person "not younger than age fourteen (14) and ... not older than twenty-one (21)" who is in school and has an IEP or is a student with a disability under section 504 (VR-SEA agreement FY26). Students can be "potentially eligible" (no VR application needed). "Verification of potential eligibility must be provided (via presence of IEP, 504 plan, or documentation of another disability) prior to provision of services." "Parent/guardian must provide consent for Pre-ETS students under age 18." Five required activities: job exploration counseling; work-based learning experiences; counseling on comprehensive transition or postsecondary programs; workplace readiness training; instruction in self-advocacy (including peer mentoring).
- Prerequisites: IEP or 504 plan; enrolled as a student; parent consent under 18.
- Documents and evidence needed: Consent and release forms (collected by the school), copy of IEP or 504 plan.
- How to apply in SW Missouri: Ask the school case manager or counselor to refer the student to the MU Pre-ETS specialist and the MVR counselor assigned to the district. Students, parents, or anyone can also self-refer through MVR (toll-free 877-222-8963) or the Springfield office for the student's ZIP code (see MVR card). MU Pre-ETS: phone 844-901-7387, email pre-ets@umsystem.edu, 2800 Maguire Boulevard, Columbia, MO 65211; regional contacts at [pre-ets.missouri.edu/find-a-contact](https://pre-ets.missouri.edu/find-a-contact/). MU employs 66 Pre-ETS specialists statewide; 12 of them serve the Missouri Schools for the Severely Disabled (MVR state plan PY 2026-2027 draft).
- Note on conflicting public information: the DESE Postsecondary Transition page labels the MU link "Pre-Employment Transition Services (ages 16-21)", while the FY26 VR-SEA agreement, the MVR overview sheet, and the MU site say 14-21. The 14-21 range is the governing one (signed agreement). Families reading the DESE page may wrongly think a 14 or 15 year old cannot get Pre-ETS.
- Typical time or wait: Not subject to the wait list for potentially eligible students. PY 2024: "over 13,000 potentially eligible students received at least one pre-employment transition service." MVR serves about 17,000 high school students and youth per year with transition services including Pre-ETS (SRC 2025 report).
- Cost to family: None.
- Where families get stuck or denied: School never refers; parent consent form not returned; student in a small rural district rarely sees the MU specialist; families think Pre-ETS is only for college-bound students; students headed for sheltered work are not referred, though the agreement says they should be.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: MVR appeal process (see MVR card).
- Screener questions: Is the person 14-21? Is the person enrolled in school (high school, college, or other recognized program)? Does the person have an IEP or 504 plan?
- Screener rule: age >= 14 AND age <= 21 AND enrolled in school AND (has IEP OR has 504 plan OR has other disability documentation) -> "can get Pre-ETS now, no wait list."
- Sources: [VR-SEA Transition Agreement FY26, signed Nov 4, 2025](https://dese.mo.gov/adult-learning-rehabilitation-services/vocational-rehabilitation/media/pdf/state-educational-agency-transition-agreement), [VR Pre-ETS Overview Sheet (rev 2024)](https://dese.mo.gov/media/pdf/vr-pre-ets-overview-sheet), [MVR Youth Services](https://dese.mo.gov/adult-learning-rehabilitation-services/vocational-rehabilitation/youth-services), [SRC 2025 Annual Report](https://dese.mo.gov/adult-learning-rehabilitation-services/media/pdf/vr-2025-mo-src-annual-report), [MVR state plan PY 2026-2027 draft](https://dese.mo.gov/media/pdf/vr-section-combined-plan-py-26-27), all checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: High

### MVR Summer Work Experience
- Run by: MVR with CRPs, high schools, and employers. CILs run a separate CIL Summer Pre-Employment program.
- Serves: VR-eligible students with disabilities (still in high school).
- Eligibility rules: Must be VR eligible (an open MVR case). The state plan describes it as "a paid, six-week, 20-hours-per-week work-based learning experience with supports in a competitive, integrated setting." Summer 2025: 31 CRPs, 1,817 VR-eligible students, more than 200 schools, more than 400 job sites; 149 students offered permanent jobs.
- Prerequisites: VR application and eligibility (and, under the order of selection, an open category or Pre-ETS carryover; see Open questions).
- Documents and evidence needed: VR application documents.
- How to apply in SW Missouri: MVR's own checklist says to talk to a VR counselor about it in the junior year. Ask the district's MVR counselor in fall or winter of junior year.
- Typical time or wait: Applications must be made well before summer; exact deadline not published.
- Cost to family: None. Student is paid.
- Where families get stuck: Student not VR-eligible in time; no CRP or job site near rural homes; transportation.
- Appeal rights: MVR process.
- Screener questions: Is the student in high school? Does the student have an open MVR case?
- Screener rule: in high school AND VR eligible -> "ask the VR counselor about Summer Work Experience."
- Sources: [MVR state plan PY 2026-2027 draft](https://dese.mo.gov/media/pdf/vr-section-combined-plan-py-26-27), [SRC 2025 Annual Report](https://dese.mo.gov/adult-learning-rehabilitation-services/media/pdf/vr-2025-mo-src-annual-report), [MVR School to Work Transition Checklist (10/2025)](https://dese.mo.gov/media/pdf/vr-school-work-checklist), checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: Medium (program details High; local SW Missouri sites and deadlines not published)

### Cooperative Work Experience Program (COOP)
- Run by: Local school district with MVR. A certified special education teacher approved as Work Experience Coordinator (WEC) supervises.
- Serves: High school students age 16 and older with an IEP that shows a need for competitive work experience.
- Eligibility rules: Age 16 or older; IEP; VR eligible with an IPE; able to work "with limited to no support"; paid at least minimum wage in a competitive integrated job. Credit: 10-19 hours per week = 1.0 credit per semester; 20 or more hours = 2.0 credits; maximum 4.0 credits per school year; released from school at least one hour per day. Student must provide own transportation.
- Prerequisites: IEP with COOP documented; VR eligibility letter; a WEC assigned to the building.
- Documents and evidence needed: VR eligibility letter, pay stubs to the WEC.
- How to apply: Ask the high school's special education department whether it has a WEC and COOP. The WEC schedules the VR intake.
- Typical time or wait: Depends on VR eligibility (60 days) and the order of selection.
- Cost to family: None. Student earns wages.
- Where families get stuck: Small or rural districts may have no WEC; own transportation required; students needing job coaching are not a fit.
- Appeal rights: IEP disputes through IDEA; IPE disputes through MVR.
- Screener questions: Age 16 or older? IEP? Can work with little support? Has transportation?
- Screener rule: age >= 16 AND has IEP AND VR eligible AND can work with limited support -> "ask about COOP."
- Sources: [COOP At A Glance (DESE, 2026)](https://dese.mo.gov/media/pdf/vr-coop-glance), [VR-SEA Transition Agreement FY26](https://dese.mo.gov/adult-learning-rehabilitation-services/vocational-rehabilitation/media/pdf/state-educational-agency-transition-agreement), checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: High

### Employer-Based Transition Training (EBTT) and Project SEARCH
- Run by: Five required partners: school district, MVR, a CRP, a long-term support provider (often DMH DD funded), and a host business.
- Serves: Students with intellectual disabilities in their "fifth and final year of high school," ages 18-21, whose goal is competitive integrated employment.
- Eligibility rules: Ages 18-21; final year of school eligibility; VR eligible with an IPE; "may qualify for services through" DMH DD. One school year of internship rotations (one to three) at a host business.
- Prerequisites: VR eligibility; usually DMH DD eligibility for long-term follow-along; the district must have a site and a local MOU with MVR.
- Documents and evidence needed: VR application, IEP, DMH DD eligibility letter if available.
- How to apply in SW Missouri: Ask the district transition coordinator in the student's second-to-last school year. Local sites: no Project SEARCH or EBTT site in SW Missouri was found (see the "Local school programs" card and Open questions). The MVR state plan names EBTT partners only in North Kansas City, Park Hill, St. Joseph, and Hannibal.
- Typical time or wait: Application and selection usually in the spring before the program year (verify with each site).
- Cost to family: None.
- Where families get stuck: Few sites; selection is competitive; student must agree to delay the diploma to use the final year; rural students live too far from host sites.
- Appeal rights: IEP and MVR processes.
- Screener questions: Age 18-21? Has IEP and has not taken a regular diploma? Intellectual disability? Goal is a paid community job?
- Screener rule: age >= 18 AND age <= 21 AND has IEP AND no regular diploma AND goal = competitive job -> "ask about Project SEARCH or EBTT."
- Sources: [VR-SEA Transition Agreement FY26](https://dese.mo.gov/adult-learning-rehabilitation-services/vocational-rehabilitation/media/pdf/state-educational-agency-transition-agreement), [MVR state plan PY 2026-2027 draft](https://dese.mo.gov/media/pdf/vr-section-combined-plan-py-26-27), checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: High for rules; see local card for sites.

### Rehabilitation Services for the Blind (RSB), transition and VR
- Run by: Missouri Department of Social Services, Family Support Division, Rehabilitation Services for the Blind.
- Serves (ages, disability types): Blind or visually impaired people. Children's Services ages 0-14; VR transition services "ages 14 and over"; adult VR with no upper age stated. RSB also provides Pre-ETS for blind or visually impaired students (MVR does not).
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year): VR: "legally blind or have another severe visual impairment" and the impairment "creates a substantial barrier to employment" (RSB Job Assistance page). The client handbook says the impairment must "severely limit" the ability to get or keep a job. No acuity number is published on these pages. A vision exam is arranged at no cost if records are not enough. School referral: RSMo 162.1139 requires DESE special education to run a joint referral system; every student identified as eligible is referred to RSB once a parent (or the student if 18 or older) consents in writing.
- Prerequisites: Visual impairment documented by eye records, or RSB-arranged exam.
- Documents and evidence needed: Eye report; school referral form signed by parent (not needed if student is 18+); IEP if any.
- How to apply in SW Missouri: Online referral form ([formsportal link](https://formsportal.mo.gov/content/forms/af/moa/my-dss/rehab-services-for-the-blind/blind-referral-form.html)) or 1-800-592-6004. RSB says it will contact the person "within 2 weeks." Southwest RSB Office: 149 Park Central Sq, Rm 640, Springfield, MO 65806, phone 417-895-6386. Schools use the DESE "School District Referral to RSB" form sent to the Deputy Director, RSB, P.O. Box 2320, 615 Howerton Court, Jefferson City, MO 65102-2320. RSB staff come to the home and will attend IEP meetings on request.
- Typical time or wait: Contact within 2 weeks of referral. Order of selection status not published on RSB pages checked (see Open questions).
- Cost to family: Transition services described as "free." Comparable benefits used first.
- Where families get stuck or denied: School never sends the referral (often because no one asks for consent); student with low vision plus another disability is routed to MVR instead of RSB, or the reverse; counties served by the Springfield RSB office are not listed in text on the RSB page (district map only).
- Appeal rights and deadlines: Request review "in writing within 90 days of the determination date." Options: administrative review, mediation, due process hearing. Send to Deputy Director, RSB, P.O. Box 2320, 615 Howerton Court, Jefferson City, MO 65102. CAP: 1-800-392-8667.
- Screener questions: Is the person blind or does the person have a severe visual impairment? Age? Wants to work or is age 14+ in school?
- Screener rule: visual impairment = severe AND age < 14 -> "RSB Children's Services." visual impairment = severe AND age >= 14 -> "RSB transition and VR (not MVR)."

### Missouri Employment First Act (RSMo 209.700) and the Model Employer order
- Run by: All state agencies that provide employment-related services or services to people with disabilities (DESE, MVR, DMH, DSS, DHEWD, and others).
- Serves: "Working age" means "sixteen years of age or older." "Youth with a disability" means "any person fourteen years of age or older and under eighteen years of age who has a disability."
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year): Effective August 28, 2023. Agencies "shall": treat competitive integrated employment "as the first and preferred outcome" for working-age people; "Offer information on competitive integrated employment to all working-age persons with disabilities," including "an explanation of the relationship between a person's earned income and his or her public benefits, information on Achieving a Better Life Experience (ABLE) accounts, and information on accessing assistive technology"; and "At least once each year, discuss basic information about competitive integrated employment with the parents or guardians of a youth with a disability" (ages 14-17), with the same benefits, ABLE, and AT content. The Act does not require work to keep benefits, and "Nothing in this section shall be construed to eliminate any supported employment services or sheltered workshop settings as options." Agencies must sign MOUs with each other. Related: Executive Order 19-16 (2019), "Missouri as a Model Employer."
- Prerequisites: None. This is a duty on agencies, not a program a family applies for.
- Documents and evidence needed: None.
- How to apply in SW Missouri: Families and advocates can ask any state agency contact (VR counselor, DD support coordinator, school) for the annual Employment First discussion and the benefits, ABLE, and AT information the Act requires.
- Typical time or wait: Annual for youth 14-17.
- Cost to family: None.
- Where families get stuck: The Act gives no individual enforcement route and no named agency in charge of the annual youth discussion; families are not told they are owed it.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: None stated in the Act.
- Screener questions: Age 14-17 with a disability? Age 16 or older?
- Screener rule: age >= 14 AND age < 18 -> "state agencies must discuss work, benefits, ABLE and AT with the family at least once a year." age >= 16 -> "state agencies must offer information on competitive integrated employment."
- Sources: [RSMo 209.700](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=209.700), [Missouri as a Model Employer (GCD)](https://disability.mo.gov/model-employer.htm), checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: High

### Extended Employment Sheltered Workshops and the Section 511 rules (subminimum wage)
- Run by: Nonprofit corporations certified by DESE (Office of Special Education, Sheltered Workshops section). Wages under a US Department of Labor certificate (FLSA section 14(c)).
- Serves: State statute defines "Disabled persons" for workshops as people "sixteen years of age or over" with "a productive work capacity in a sheltered environment" who are "nonemployable in competitive business and industry" (RSMo 178.900).
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year):
  - Before hire, "individuals must be assessed by the DESE Certification Specialists" and certified (DESE FAQ). "There are 89 workshop corporations located around the state of Missouri." Pay is by productivity, for example 50% productivity earns 50% of the prevailing wage.
  - State aid: at least $21 "for each six-hour or longer day worked" by a certified employee, up to 38 hours per week (RSMo 178.931). FY2024 budget description: about 5,360 adults employed; "Number of Individuals on Waiting List to Hire" 460 in FY2022 (actual), 360 projected for FY2023-FY2025.
  - Section 511 (federal, applied in the VR-SEA agreement): a school or DESE may not contract with a subminimum wage employer for anyone "age 24 or younger." "As long as the individual is a student of an LEA, the student cannot be involved in any subminimum wage employment" (workshop work, subminimum work experiences, or non-integrated work experiences). Before a youth 24 or younger can start subminimum wage work, the school documents transition and Pre-ETS on the "Summative Transition Outcomes form," MVR opens a case and provides career counseling and information and referral, and MVR gives documentation after a determination of "unable to benefit from VR services."
  - Federal: the US Department of Labor withdrew its December 4, 2024 proposal to phase out 14(c) certificates, on July 7, 2025 (90 FR, July 7, 2025). Subminimum wage certificates remain available in 2026.
- Prerequisites: For age 24 and under: completed Pre-ETS and transition services, MVR case with career counseling, MVR documentation. DESE certification.
- Documents and evidence needed: MVR Section 511 documentation; DESE certification.
- How to apply in SW Missouri: Contact DESE Sheltered Workshops, 573-751-3547, sesw@dese.mo.gov, for the certification specialist and the list of workshops serving the area. A list of SW Missouri workshops was not found in a public source (see Open questions).
- Typical time or wait: Hiring wait list exists statewide (360 projected, FY2023-FY2025).
- Cost to family: None. Worker earns wages (often subminimum).
- Where families get stuck: Student in school cannot be placed in a workshop; family does not know the Section 511 steps; MVR wait list does not block the required career counseling, but families may assume it does.
- Appeal rights: MVR appeal for Section 511 documentation issues.
- Screener questions: Age? Still in school? Has the person completed Pre-ETS and an MVR career counseling contact?
- Screener rule: in school -> "no subminimum wage work allowed." age <= 24 AND wants workshop -> "needs MVR Section 511 documentation first." age >= 16 AND not in school -> "may be assessed by DESE certification specialist."
- Sources: [DESE Sheltered Workshops](https://dese.mo.gov/special-education/sheltered-workshops), [DESE Sheltered Workshop FAQs](https://dese.mo.gov/special-education/sheltered-workshops/faqs), [RSMo 178.900](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=178.900), [RSMo 178.931](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=178.931), [OA budget program description, Sheltered Workshops (FY2024 budget)](https://oa.mo.gov/sites/default/files/dese_sheltered_workshops24.pdf), [VR-SEA Agreement FY26, section 7](https://dese.mo.gov/adult-learning-rehabilitation-services/vocational-rehabilitation/media/pdf/state-educational-agency-transition-agreement), [Federal Register, July 7, 2025, withdrawal of 14(c) NPRM](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-07-07/html/2025-12534.htm), all checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: High for rules; Medium for wait list numbers (budget projections from FY2024 document).
