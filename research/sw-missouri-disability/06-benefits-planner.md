## 1. Program and resource cards

### SSI (Supplemental Security Income)
- Run by: Social Security Administration (SSA).
- Serves (ages, disability types): Children under 18 and adults 18+ who meet the SSA disability or blindness definition, and adults 65+. All disability types in scope (IDD, physical, medically complex, sensory).
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year):
  - Federal benefit rate (FBR), individual: 2026 $994 per month; 2027 $1,029 per month.
  - FBR, couple: 2026 $1,491; 2027 $1,544.
  - One-third reduction (VTR) when the person lives in another person's household and gets shelter there: 2026 $331.33 (payment max $663); 2027 $343.00 (payment max $686).
  - Presumed maximum value (PMV) for in-kind shelter = one-third of FBR + $20: 2026 $351.33; 2027 $363.00 (computed from the 20 CFR 416.1140 formula and the POMS FBR table).
  - Resource limit: $2,000 individual, $3,000 couple (unchanged since 1989, 20 CFR 416.1205).
  - Income: $20 general exclusion; $65 plus one-half of the rest of earned income excluded. Unearned income (for example SSDI or CDB) reduces SSI dollar for dollar after the $20.
  - Break-even earnings (no other income, own household): 2026 about $2,073 per month; 2027 about $2,143 per month (computed: FBR x 2 + $85).
  - Student earned income exclusion (under age 22 and regularly attending school): 2026 $2,410 per month, $9,730 per year; 2027 $2,500 per month, $10,080 per year.
  - Missouri pays no state supplement to people who live independently. The SSA 1619(b) table shows Missouri's state supplement component as $0.
  - Parent deeming: a child's SSI counts parent income and resources through the month the child turns 18 (20 CFR 416.1165). From the next month, only the young adult's own income and resources count.
- Prerequisites: Disability or blindness under SSA rules; U.S. citizen or qualifying noncitizen; Social Security number; must file for other benefits the person may get (for example CDB on a parent's record).
- Documents and evidence needed: Birth certificate, SSN, proof of citizenship, bank statements, lease or rent statement, pay stubs, school records (IEP, 504 plan), medical records and provider contacts, names of all doctors and clinics.
- How to apply in SW Missouri: Call SSA or visit the local SSA field office. Adults can start online at ssa.gov for some claims; child SSI needs an interview. Use the SSA office locator to find the Springfield office (agent 01 or 08 to confirm the address). A protective filing date is set by the first contact.
- Typical time or wait: Initial disability decisions often take many months. Not verified today; see Open questions.
- Cost to family: None to apply.
- Where families get stuck or denied:
  - Resources over $2,000 at the first moment of the month (saved SSI, gifts, a parent's account in the young adult's name, an UTMA account).
  - In-kind shelter after 18 when the young adult pays nothing or less than the PMV for rent.
  - Not reporting wages by the 6th of the next month, which creates overpayments.
  - Age 18 redetermination under adult rules (see the SSI age 18 card).
- Appeal rights and deadlines: Reconsideration, then hearing before an administrative law judge, then Appeals Council, then federal court. 60 days from receipt of notice (SSA presumes receipt 5 days after the date on the notice). To keep benefits paid during an appeal of a cut or stop, file within 10 days of receipt. (Standard SSA rule; SSA site was not reachable today. Confidence Medium for the appeal timing.)
- Screener questions:
  1. Is the person under 18, 18 to 64, or 65+?
  2. Does the person have a disability or blindness that limits daily function or work and has lasted or will last 12 months?
  3. Countable resources in the person's name (and a parent's, if under 18 and living with the parent): at or under $2,000?
  4. Monthly earnings? Monthly unearned income (SSDI, CDB, child support, other)?
  5. Does the person pay their full share of rent and utilities, or at least the PMV?
- Screener rule: (disabled OR blind OR age >= 65) AND countable resources <= $2,000 (individual) AND countable income < FBR ($994 in 2026, $1,029 in 2027). Flag "check SSI" if earnings alone are under the break-even point. If age < 18 and living with parents, flag "parent deeming applies; SSI may be $0 now; reapply at 18."
- Sources: [POMS SI 02001.020 SSI federal benefit rates (TN 36 (10-26), 2027 rates)](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0502001020), checked 2026-10-26; [POMS SI 00820.510 Student earned income exclusion (TN 101 (10-26))](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0500820510), checked 2026-10-26; [POMS SI 00501.020 Student definition](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0500501020), checked 2026-10-26; [20 CFR 416.1205 resource limits (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-III/part-416/subpart-L/section-416.1205), checked 2026-10-26; [20 CFR 416.1140 presumed value rule](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-III/part-416/subpart-K/section-416.1140), checked 2026-10-26; [20 CFR 416.1165 deeming from parents](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-III/part-416/subpart-K/section-416.1165), checked 2026-10-26; [POMS SI 02302.200 1619(b) thresholds](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0502302200), checked 2026-10-26; secondary for the COLA date: [Accounting Today, 3.6% COLA for 2027](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/social-security-to-increase-benefits-3-6-in-2027).
- Confidence: High for numbers (POMS and eCFR checked today). Medium for appeal timing and processing time.

### SSI in-kind support and maintenance (ISM), rules in force since 30 Sep 2024
- Run by: SSA.
- Serves: Every SSI applicant and recipient, most often young adults living with parents.
- Eligibility rules:
  - Food is no longer counted as ISM. Only shelter counts: room, rent, mortgage, property tax, heat, gas, electricity, water, sewer, garbage (20 CFR 416.1130(b)(1), 89 FR 21199, effective 30 Sep 2024).
  - Business arrangement rule, now nationwide: if required rent is at least the PMV (2026: $351.33; 2027: $363.00), there is no ISM from rent. If rent is less, SSA counts the difference up to the PMV or the current market rent, whichever is less (89 FR 25514).
  - One-third reduction (VTR) applies only if the person lives in another person's household, gets shelter from them, and others in the household provide all meals (20 CFR 416.1130(b)(2)). Otherwise the PMV rule applies.
  - Public assistance household: if another household member gets SNAP, TANF, SSI, or other need-based aid, SSA treats the person as getting no ISM from household members (20 CFR 416.1142, SNAP added by 89 FR 28622).
  - No 2025 or 2026 rule to undo these changes was found.
- Prerequisites: SSI claim or eligibility.
- Documents and evidence needed: Written rent agreement, proof of payment, household expense amounts, proof that a household member gets SNAP or other need-based aid.
- How to apply: Report living arrangements at the SSI application and at every change.
- Typical time or wait: Effect starts the month of the change.
- Cost to family: None. The young adult can pay rent from SSI.
- Where families get stuck: Families still think food counts. Families do not write down a rent agreement. Families do not know that a SNAP household member removes ISM.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: As for SSI (60 days; 10 days to keep payment).
- Screener questions: Does the person live with family? Does the person pay rent of at least $351.33 (2026) or $363.00 (2027)? Does anyone else in the home get SNAP, TANF, or SSI?
- Screener rule: IF lives with others AND pays rent < PMV AND no household member gets SNAP/TANF/SSI THEN warn "SSI may be reduced by up to $351.33 (2026) for free or cheap shelter".
- Sources: [20 CFR 416.1130 via eCFR API, version 2026-10-01](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-III/part-416/subpart-K/section-416.1130), checked 2026-10-26; [20 CFR 416.1142](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-III/part-416/subpart-K/section-416.1142), checked 2026-10-26; [Federal Register final rule 89 FR 21199, Omitting Food From ISM](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-03-27/pdf/2024-06464.pdf), checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High. Note: POMS SI 00835.020 still shows the old food language (TN 108 (08-23)). The regulation controls. Families and workers who read POMS may be misled.

### SSI age 18 redetermination and the adult application at 18
- Run by: SSA and Missouri Disability Determinations (DDS).
- Serves: Child SSI recipients turning 18; young adults who were over the deeming limit as children.
- Eligibility rules:
  - SSA must redetermine a child SSI recipient within one year of the 18th birthday under the adult initial disability standard. Medical improvement rules do not apply. The SGA step is not applied (POMS DI 23570.020, TN 50 (03-26)).
  - Parent deeming stops after the month of the 18th birthday (20 CFR 416.1165). A young adult who got no SSI as a child because of parent income can apply for adult SSI the month after the 18th birthday.
  - The student earned income exclusion continues to age 22 if the person regularly attends school (grades 7 to 12: 12 hours a week; college: 8 hours a week).
- Prerequisites: SSN; medical and school records; representative payee review if the young adult cannot manage funds.
- Documents and evidence needed: Current medical records, IEP and evaluations, teacher and job coach statements, list of providers, any guardianship or supported decision-making papers (agent 04 owns the legal part).
- How to apply: SSA starts the redetermination. For a new adult claim, contact SSA in the birthday month to set a protective filing date.
- Typical time or wait: Not verified today.
- Cost to family: None.
- Where families get stuck: No current medical evidence; missed SSA forms; savings over $2,000 in the young adult's name; family does not set a rent arrangement, so ISM cuts the check.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: Request reconsideration within 60 days. Ask for continued benefits within 10 days (Section 1631(a)(7) benefit continuation). Confirm with SSA notice. Confidence Medium.
- Screener questions: Age 16 or 17? Gets child SSI now? Was child SSI denied for parent income? Savings in the child's name over $2,000?
- Screener rule: IF age >= 17 AND (child SSI now OR denied for parent income) THEN show "Adult SSI step: plan for age 18 redetermination or new application in the month after the 18th birthday".
- Sources: [POMS DI 23570.020 age 18 redeterminations](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0423570020), checked 2026-10-26; [20 CFR 416.1165](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-III/part-416/subpart-K/section-416.1165), checked 2026-10-26; [20 CFR 416.1856 who is a child](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-III/part-416/subpart-R/section-416.1856), checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High for rules; Medium for appeal continuation details.

### SSI work incentives: 1619(a), 1619(b), PASS, IRWE, BWE
- Run by: SSA.
- Serves: SSI recipients who work.
- Eligibility rules:
  - 1619(a): SSI cash continues while earnings exceed SGA, until countable income reaches the break-even point.
  - 1619(b): Medicaid continues when earnings end the SSI check, if the person needs Medicaid to work and gross earnings are under the state threshold. Missouri threshold 2026: $55,181 per year (base $24,876 plus Medicaid component $30,305). DSS lists the monthly 1619 figure as $4,599 (2026). Resource limit stays $2,000.
  - 1619(b) applies in 209(b) states if the person had Medicaid in the month before 1619 status began (POMS SI 01715.015).
  - PASS (Plan to Achieve Self-Support): income or resources set aside for an approved work goal are excluded. Written plan approved by SSA.
  - IRWE and BWE: impairment-related or blind work expenses are deducted from earnings.
- Prerequisites: SSI eligibility; for 1619(b), Medicaid the month before.
- Documents and evidence needed: Pay stubs; receipts for IRWE or BWE; PASS form SSA-545 for a PASS.
- How to apply: Report wages to SSA monthly. Ask a WIPA counselor (see WIPA card) for PASS and 1619 planning.
- Typical time or wait: PASS review time not verified.
- Cost to family: None.
- Where families get stuck: Not reporting wages; resources above $2,000 from saved wages (ABLE solves this); MO HealthNet not told about 1619(b) status, so FSD closes the case.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: As for SSI.
- Screener questions: Gets SSI? Works or plans to work? Gross yearly earnings? Has MO HealthNet now?
- Screener rule: IF SSI AND works AND gross yearly earnings < $55,181 (2026) AND resources <= $2,000 THEN "Medicaid may continue under 1619(b)".
- Sources: [POMS SI 02302.200 1619(b) thresholds (effective 01/20/2026)](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0502302200), checked 2026-10-26; [POMS SI 01715.015 Medicaid protected groups (TN 10 (09-26))](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0501715015), checked 2026-10-26; [DSS Appendix J, Eligibility Standards for Non-MAGI Programs (10/2026)](https://dssmanuals.mo.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/mhabd-appendix-j.pdf), checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High. 2027 Missouri 1619(b) threshold not yet published (Open questions).

### SSDI (Social Security Disability Insurance) and SSDI work rules
- Run by: SSA.
- Serves: Workers with enough work credits who become disabled; adults of working age.
- Eligibility rules:
  - Substantial gainful activity (SGA), non-blind: 2026 $1,690 per month; 2027 $1,770.
  - SGA, blind (Title II only): 2026 $2,830; 2027 $2,950.
  - Trial work period (TWP) service month: 2026 $1,210; 2027 $1,270. TWP ends after 9 service months within a rolling 60-month period.
  - Extended period of eligibility (36 months after TWP), expedited reinstatement, and extended Medicare are standard SSDI rules. Not re-checked today (Confidence Medium).
  - Medicare starts after 24 months of entitlement to disability benefits.
  - Medicare Part B premium 2026: $202.90 per month (DSS Appendix J). 2027 Part B premium: not found.
- Prerequisites: Insured status (work credits), disability under SSA rules.
- Documents and evidence needed: Work history, medical records, SSN, bank account.
- How to apply: SSA online, phone, or field office.
- Typical time or wait: Five-month waiting period before SSDI pays (standard rule; not re-checked today). Medicare after 24 months.
- Cost to family: None to apply.
- Where families get stuck: SSDI income often pushes a person over SSI and over the MO HealthNet non-spend-down limit ($1,131 in 2026), which starts a spend down.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: 60 days for reconsideration.
- Screener questions: Has the person worked and paid Social Security tax? Gets SSDI now? Monthly SSDI amount? Monthly earnings?
- Screener rule: IF SSDI AND earnings > TWP amount ($1,210 in 2026) THEN "TWP month used; get benefits counseling". IF earnings > SGA after TWP THEN "risk of SSDI cash stop; check EPE and Medicare continuation".
- Sources: [POMS DI 10501.015 SGA tables (TN 25 (10-26))](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0410501015), checked 2026-10-26; [POMS DI 13010.060 TWP (TN 97 (10-26))](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0413010060), checked 2026-10-26; [DSS Appendix J (10/2026)](https://dssmanuals.mo.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/mhabd-appendix-j.pdf), checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High for SGA and TWP numbers; Medium for EPE and waiting period details.

### Childhood Disability Benefits (CDB), also called Disabled Adult Child (DAC)
- Run by: SSA.
- Serves: Adults 18+ whose disability began before age 22, on a parent's work record.
- Eligibility rules:
  - The parent must be entitled to Social Security retirement or disability benefits, or must have died fully or currently insured.
  - The adult child must be unmarried, dependent on the parent, and disabled before 22.
  - Marriage ends CDB, except marriage to a Title II beneficiary (not a child under 18 or a student child aged 18 or 19). Marriage to a person who gets only SSI does end CDB.
  - Re-entitlement: if CDB ended because of SGA work, the person can be re-entitled at any time. If CDB ended for medical improvement, disability must recur within 84 months. Marriage since last entitlement blocks re-entitlement.
  - Medicare Part A after 24 months of CDB entitlement; Part B available.
  - SGA rule applies (2026 $1,690; 2027 $1,770). Work above SGA can end CDB.
- Prerequisites: Parent retires, becomes disabled, or dies. The adult child must meet the adult disability standard.
- Documents and evidence needed: Parent's SSN and record; proof of relationship; medical records showing onset before 22 (school and early medical records are key).
- How to apply: SSA. Apply when the parent files for retirement or disability, or when the parent dies.
- Typical time or wait: Not verified today.
- Cost to family: None.
- Where families get stuck:
  - CDB often replaces SSI. If CDB exceeds the SSI FBR, SSI ends and the family fears loss of Medicaid.
  - Marriage to a person not on Title II ends CDB for good on that record.
  - Families do not know to apply when a parent claims retirement at 62.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: 60 days.
- Screener questions: Did disability start before 22? Is a parent getting Social Security retirement or disability, or has a parent died after working? Is the person married? To whom?
- Screener rule: age >= 18 AND onset < 22 AND unmarried (or married to a Title II beneficiary) AND (parent gets RIB or DIB OR parent died insured) THEN "Check CDB".
- Sources: [POMS RS 00203.001 child's benefits entitlement](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0300203001), checked 2026-10-26; [POMS RS 00203.035 terminating events, marriage](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0300203035), checked 2026-10-26; [POMS RS 00203.015 re-entitlement](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0300203015), checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High.

### DAC Medicaid protection (Section 1634(c)) and other Medicaid protected groups
- Run by: Federal rule (SSA notice); Missouri FSD applies it for MO HealthNet.
- Serves: CDB beneficiaries who lost SSI.
- Eligibility rules:
  - A CDB beneficiary who lost SSI because of CDB entitlement or a CDB increase (on or after 1 July 1987), and who would still get SSI without that CDB income, must be treated as an SSI recipient for Medicaid.
  - Pickle: former SSI recipients who lost SSI because of Title II COLAs stay Medicaid protected if they would be SSI eligible without the COLAs.
  - Early widow(er)s (1634(d)) and disabled widow(er)s (1634(b)) are other protected groups.
  - SSA sends notice paragraphs to people 18+ with CDB (BIC "C") who lost SSI for excess income.
- Prerequisites: Prior SSI; CDB entitlement.
- Documents and evidence needed: SSA award letter for CDB; SSI termination notice.
- How to apply in SW Missouri: Report the change to FSD and ask FSD to review under DAC rules. FSD phone for applications: 855-373-9994.
- Typical time or wait: Not verified.
- Cost to family: None.
- Where families get stuck: Missouri is a 209(b) state. Its MHABD income test excludes "all SSI payments" but counts CDB. A DAC person can drop from non-spend-down to spend-down if FSD does not apply the DAC disregard. I could not see how FSD applies 1634(c) because the MHABD manual is password protected. See Open questions.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: FSD hearing request (see MO HealthNet card).
- Screener questions: Did the person get SSI before CDB started? Did SSI stop because of CDB?
- Screener rule: IF CDB AND SSI stopped because of CDB AND would be SSI eligible without CDB THEN "Medicaid protected as DAC; tell FSD".
- Sources: [POMS SI 01715.015 Medicaid protected groups (TN 10 (09-26), effective 09/30/2026)](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0501715015), checked 2026-10-26; [POMS SI 01715.010 State Medicaid types; Missouri 209(b)](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0501715010), checked 2026-10-26; [DMH Spend Down page, MHABD income deductions](https://dmh.mo.gov/medicaid-eligibility/spend-down), checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High for the federal rule. Low for how Missouri FSD applies it in practice.

### MO HealthNet for the Aged, Blind, and Disabled (MHABD): non-spend-down and spend down
- Run by: Missouri Department of Social Services, Family Support Division (FSD) for eligibility; MO HealthNet Division (MHD) for coverage and spend down payments.
- Serves: Adults 18+ who are permanently and totally disabled (SSA definition), blind (Missouri definition), or 65+. Children under 18 through MO HealthNet for Disabled Children (MHDC) with parent deeming.
- Eligibility rules (2026):
  - Non-spend-down income limit, aged or disabled: $1,131 single, $1,533 couple (85% FPL, effective 1 Apr 2026). 2025 figure was $1,109 single.
  - Blind: $1,330 single, $1,804 couple (100% FPL, effective 1 Apr 2026).
  - Resource limit: $6,220.50 single, $12,441.00 couple (effective 1 Jul 2026). The FY2026 limit was $6,068.80 and $12,137.55. RSMo 208.010 sets annual CPI increases since fiscal year 2022.
  - Income deductions: first $65 of earned income and one-half of the rest; $20 personal exemption; all SSI payments; health insurance premiums.
  - Spend down: no income limit. The spend down amount is countable income above the non-spend-down limit. Coverage is for each month the spend down is met.
  - Missouri's disability definition for 209(b) purposes does not include nonblind children under 18; children use MHDC with deeming.
  - Missouri blindness definition: 5/200, decided by the FSD state ophthalmologist (different from SSA's).
- Prerequisites: SSA disability decision (SSI or SSDI) or an FSD Medical Review Team decision; Missouri residence; citizenship or qualifying status; SSN.
- Documents and evidence needed: Application plus the Supplemental Form (required if age 65+, blind or disabled, gets Social Security, lives in a facility, or has Medicare or VA care); proof of income, resources, insurance premiums.
- How to apply in SW Missouri: Online portal; phone 855-373-9994; mail to FSD, P.O. Box 2700, Jefferson City, MO 65102; fax 573-526-9400; upload at mydssupload.mo.gov; or in person at a local FSD Resource Center.
- Typical time or wait: FSD says contact them if there is no word after 45 days. Disability-based decisions take longer. Nursing and SNC pages say 45 to 90 days.
- Cost to family: Spend down amount each month, if any.
- Spend down mechanics:
  - Pay-in: pay the monthly spend down to MHD online (mymohealthportal.com), by mail to MHD Premium Collections Unit, P.O. Box 808001, Kansas City, MO 64180, or by automatic withdrawal on the 10th for the next month. Pay-in before the 1st of the month gives coverage for the whole month.
  - Incurred bills: submit unpaid or paid medical bills (participant form IM-29SDP; provider form IM-29PROV). Coverage starts on the day bills reach the spend down. Upload, email FSD.IM.SPENDDOWNDOCS@DSS.MO.GOV, fax 855-600-3754, or mail to Spend Down Unit, 16798 Oak Hill Drive, Suite 600, Houston, MO 65483.
  - Up to a year back can be met. Spend down questions: 855-600-4412.
  - Refund of pay-in not used by claims: DMH says after 12 months; older DSS manual says 13 months. Not resolved.
  - An open spend down case speeds MOCDD waiver enrollment for children on the wait list (DMH).
- Where families get stuck:
  - SSDI or CDB just over $1,131 creates a spend down that is larger than the family can pay.
  - Families do not know DD waiver or HCB eligibility can remove the spend down (agent 01 and 08 own waiver detail).
  - Families do not know that a person without Medicare and with income up to 138% FPL may use the Adult Expansion Group instead of spend down (see card).
  - The eligibility manual is not public.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: FSD fair hearing. Deadline is on the FSD notice; I could not confirm the current number of days today (Open questions).
- Screener questions: Age? Disabled or blind? Monthly countable income? Countable resources? Has Medicare? Gets SSI?
- Screener rule: (age >= 65 OR disabled OR blind) AND resources <= $6,220.50 (single, from 1 Jul 2026) THEN eligible category. IF countable income <= $1,131 (aged/disabled) or <= $1,330 (blind) THEN non-spend-down ELSE spend down = income minus limit.
- Sources: [DSS Appendix J, Eligibility Standards for Non-MAGI Programs (10/2026)](https://dssmanuals.mo.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/mhabd-appendix-j.pdf), checked 2026-10-26; [DSS Appendix K, MO HealthNet Eligibility for Non-MAGI Programs (07/2026)](https://dssmanuals.mo.gov/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/appendix_k.pdf), checked 2026-10-26; [RSMo 208.010 resource limits and CPI increase](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=208.010), checked 2026-10-26; [DSS Apply for MO HealthNet](https://mydss.mo.gov/healthcare/apply), checked 2026-10-26; [DSS Spend Down page](https://mydss.mo.gov/spend-down), checked 2026-10-26; [DMH Spend Down page](https://dmh.mo.gov/medicaid-eligibility/spend-down), checked 2026-10-26; [DMH ME codes explained](https://dmh.mo.gov/medicaid-eligibility/understanding-types-medicaid/me-codes/explained), checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High for 2026 numbers and payment methods. Medium for the refund timing.

### MO HealthNet Adult Expansion Group (AEG)
- Run by: DSS FSD.
- Serves: Adults 19 to 64.
- Eligibility rules: Income at or below 138% FPL (MAGI); not pregnant; not entitled to or enrolled in Medicare Part A or B; not receiving SSI; not eligible for a mandatory category such as MHABD non-spend-down.
- Key interaction: a disabled adult on MHABD spend down who has no Medicare (for example, in the SSDI or CDB 24-month Medicare wait) may qualify for AEG with no spend down if MAGI income is at or under 138% FPL. Once Medicare starts, AEG ends.
- Prerequisites: Missouri residence; MAGI income test.
- Documents and evidence needed: Income proof.
- How to apply: Same FSD application channels as above.
- Typical time or wait: Not verified.
- Cost to family: None.
- Where families get stuck: FSD may place a disabled adult in spend down when AEG would cost nothing. Federal 2025 law (P.L. 119-21) adds community engagement rules and more frequent renewals for expansion adults; I did not verify Missouri's start dates or exemptions today (Open questions; agent 04 or 05 may cover).
- Screener questions: Age 19 to 64? Has Medicare? Gets SSI? Household MAGI income?
- Screener rule: age 19 to 64 AND no Medicare AND no SSI AND MAGI <= 138% FPL THEN "Check Adult Expansion".
- Sources: [DMH ME codes explained, ME code E2](https://dmh.mo.gov/medicaid-eligibility/understanding-types-medicaid/me-codes/explained), checked 2026-10-26.

### Ticket to Work Health Assurance (TWHA), Missouri's Medicaid buy-in
- Run by: DSS FSD (eligibility) and MHD (premiums).
- Serves: Working people with a disability, age 16 through 64 (DSS age range; the statute does not state an age).
- Eligibility rules (2026):
  - Must be employed with Social Security and Medicare taxes withheld (self-employed must prove payment of these taxes).
  - Disabled under SSI rules except for earnings, or a TWWIIA "medically improved" worker. SGA does not apply.
  - Income test: countable income at or under 250% FPL, after disregarding: first $50,000 of a spouse's earned income; $20; health insurance premiums; $75 dental and optical deduction; all SSI; first $50 of SSDI; one-half of the worker's earned income (impairment-related work expense deduction). Worker earnings between 250% and 300% FPL are also disregarded.

  - Report income changes within 10 days.
  - Statute status: the 2023 text of RSMo 208.146 said the section "shall expire August 28, 2025." HB 2372 (2026) re-enacted the section, effective 28 Aug 2026, and the current text has no expiration clause. DSS kept publishing TWHA standards in 2026.

- Typical time or wait: Not verified.
- Cost to family: Premiums above.
- Where families get stuck: Families compare a spend down (which can be hundreds of dollars) and do not know TWHA premiums are often much lower for workers; cash wages without tax withholding do not count; age 65 ends TWHA.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: FSD hearing (see MHABD card).
- Screener questions: Age 16 to 64? Works for pay with taxes withheld? Disability? Monthly gross earnings, SSDI, other income? Resources?
- Screener rule: age 16 to 64 AND disabled AND has taxed earnings AND resources <= $6,220.50 AND gross income <= $3,990 (single, 2026) THEN "Check TWHA"; show the premium band.

### ABLE accounts (federal rules) and SSI and MO HealthNet treatment
- Run by: State ABLE programs under 26 USC 529A; SSA and FSD set the benefit effects.
- Serves: People whose blindness or disability began before age 46 (from 1 Jan 2026; before 2026 the age was 26). Eligibility by SSI or SSDI receipt based on that disability, or by a disability certification.
- Eligibility rules:
  - Annual contribution limit, all sources combined: 2026 $20,000 (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32 section 4.34; the gift tax exclusion stays $19,000). 2027 limit: IRS has not published it as of 26 Oct 2026.
  - ABLE to Work: a working owner who does not contribute to an employer retirement plan can add earnings up to the lesser of earnings or the prior year one-person poverty line ($15,650 in 2026 for the 48 states).
  - SSI: the first $100,000 is excluded from resources. Above $100,000, the excess counts. If only the excess puts the person over $2,000, SSI is suspended (no time limit) and Medicaid continues. Distributions are never income. Non-housing qualified expense money is excluded while kept, identifiable, and meant for the expense. Housing distributions count as a resource if kept into the next month. Third-party contributions are not income to the owner. Wages deposited directly still count as income.
  - Medicaid payback: at death, after qualified expenses (including funeral), the state may file a claim for Medicaid paid. Whether MO HealthNet files such claims in practice was not confirmed (Open questions).
- Prerequisites: Onset before 46; one ABLE account per person.
- Documents and evidence needed: SSA award letter, or a signed disability certification (with a doctor's diagnosis kept on file).
- How to apply: Open online with MO ABLE or any state's ABLE plan. See the MO ABLE card.
- Typical time or wait: Same day online (not verified for MO ABLE).
- Cost to family: Plan fees (MO ABLE fee schedule not verified today).
- Where families get stuck: Saved SSI or wages push the person over $2,000; families do not know ABLE is the fix; families keep housing distributions past month end.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: Not applicable to ABLE; SSA or FSD appeal applies if benefits are cut.
- Screener questions: Did the disability begin before age 46? Savings now or expected? Works?
- Screener rule: onset < 46 THEN "ABLE eligible". IF SSI AND resources near $2,000 THEN "Use ABLE".
- Sources: [POMS SI 01130.740 ABLE accounts (TN 135 (04-26), effective 02/26/2026)](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0501130740), checked 2026-10-26; [IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32, section 4.34](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf), checked 2026-10-26; [ABLE National Resource Center, Funding strategies 2026 (secondary)](https://www.ablenrc.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/CaseSummary5-RangeOfStrategiesForFundingABLE-2026.pdf), checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High for SSI and 2026 limit. Low for Missouri payback practice.

### SNAP (food assistance) rules for people with disabilities
- Run by: DSS FSD under USDA rules.
- Serves: Low-income households. Special rules for households with an elderly (60+) or disabled member.
- Eligibility rules (federal fiscal year 2027, from 1 Oct 2026):
  - Missouri income limits as of 1 Oct 2026, 1 person: gross $20,352 per year (about $1,696 per month), net $15,660 per year (about $1,305 per month); 2 people: gross $27,504, net $21,156 per year.
  - A household with an elderly or disabled member must meet only the net income test, not the gross test (7 CFR 273.9(a)).
  - Excess shelter deduction has no cap for a household with an elderly or disabled member (7 CFR 273.9(d)(6)(ii)).
  - Medical deduction: out-of-pocket medical costs over $35 per month for an elderly or disabled member, including health insurance premiums and MO HealthNet spend down, service animal costs, and some transport.
  - Resources: $3,000; $4,750 if any member is 60+ or disabled (DSS page; effective date not shown).
  - "Disabled member" includes a person who gets SSI, or Social Security disability or blindness payments (7 CFR 271.2).
  - Household composition: a person under 22 who lives with a parent must be in the parent's SNAP household (7 CFR 273.1(b)(1)(ii)). A person 60+ who cannot buy and cook food because of a permanent disability can be a separate household.
  - FY2027 maximum allotment for 1 person is reported as $306 and the shelter cap for households without an elderly or disabled member as $769 (USDA memo, read through secondary reports; USDA site blocked today).
  - Work rules from 1 Nov 2025 (Missouri): able-bodied adults must show 80 hours per month of work or training. Exempt: a person who does not work because of a physical or mental health reason; a person caring for a child under 6 or for someone who needs help caring for themselves; under 18 or over 64; others listed by DSS.
- Prerequisites: SSN for each member; Missouri residence.
- Documents and evidence needed: Income, rent and utility bills, medical receipts (send receipts to claim the medical deduction).
- How to apply: Online at mydss.mo.gov; FSD, P.O. Box 2700, Jefferson City, MO 65102; fax 573-526-9400; FSD Information Line 855-373-4636; interview callback 855-823-4908.
- Typical time or wait: Within 30 days; expedited in 7 days or less for some.
- Cost to family: None.
- Where families get stuck:
  - A young adult under 22 living with parents cannot get a separate SNAP case, so parent income counts.
  - Families do not report medical costs, so they lose the medical deduction.
  - SNAP receipt by another household member removes SSI ISM from household members (see ISM card). Families do not know this link.
  - Mid-certification and recertification letters are missed.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: FSD fair hearing; deadline on notice (not verified today).
- Screener questions: Household size? Anyone 60+ or getting SSI or Social Security disability? Gross and net monthly income? Rent and utilities? Medical costs over $35? Is the disabled person under 22 and living with a parent?
- Screener rule: IF elderly or disabled member THEN test net income only (<= $1,305 per month for 1 person, FY2027) and resources <= $4,750 ELSE test gross (<= $1,696) and net.
- Sources: [DSS Benefit Program Income Limits, as of 10/01/2026](https://mydss.mo.gov/benefit-program-income-limits), checked 2026-10-26; [DSS Apply for SNAP](https://mydss.mo.gov/food-assistance/apply-for-snap), checked 2026-10-26; [DSS SNAP medical deductions](https://mydss.mo.gov/snap-medical-deductions-elderly-and-disabled-missourians), checked 2026-10-26; [DSS My SNAP Benefit, work requirements](https://mydss.mo.gov/food-assistance/food-stamp-program), checked 2026-10-26; [7 CFR 273.9 (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-C/part-273/subpart-E/section-273.9), checked 2026-10-26; [7 CFR 273.1 (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-C/part-273/subpart-A/section-273.1), checked 2026-10-26; [7 CFR 271.2 (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-C/part-271/section-271.2), checked 2026-10-26; [USDA FY2027 SNAP COLA memo (could not open; numbers via secondary)](https://www.usda.gov/sites/default/files/guidance-documents/fna.snap-cola2027.pdf).
- Confidence: High for Missouri limits and federal rules; Medium for FY2027 allotment and shelter cap.
- Data note: the DSS income chart has typing errors (for example $774,059 for kids in a 7-person household, and the same net figure for 4 and 5 people). The screener should not copy the chart without a check.

### Medicare for SSDI and CDB beneficiaries (short card)
- Run by: SSA (enrollment) and CMS.
- Rules: Part A after 24 months of SSDI or CDB entitlement. Part B premium 2026 $202.90 per month. MSP (QMB, SLMB, QI-1) can pay Part B. Adult Expansion Medicaid ends when Medicare starts. People with both Medicare and MO HealthNet get Part D drug coverage; Extra Help details not verified today.
- Screener rule: IF SSDI or CDB for 24 months THEN "Medicare starts; apply for MSP; check if AEG ends and MHABD spend down starts".
- Sources: [POMS RS 00203.001 (CDB Medicare after 24 months)](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0300203001), checked 2026-10-26; [DSS Appendix J (Part B 2026)](https://dssmanuals.mo.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/mhabd-appendix-j.pdf), checked 2026-10-26; [DMH ME code E2](https://dmh.mo.gov/medicaid-eligibility/understanding-types-medicaid/me-codes/explained), checked 2026-10-26.
