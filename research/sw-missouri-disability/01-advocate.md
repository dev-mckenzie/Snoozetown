### 1.2 DDD eligibility determination (intake) (DONE)
- Run by: DDD Regional Office Intake Unit.
- Serves: any age.
- Eligibility rules (DDD "Division Eligibility" sheet, revised 10 Sep 2024):
  - Intellectual disability presenting **before age 18**, and/or developmental disability presenting **before age 22**.
  - Lifelong need for specialized coordination and supports.
  - Functional limitation test by age:
    - Birth through 4: participates in First Steps, OR scores "at least 87% below average" in ONE major life area on the Missouri Adaptive Ability Scale (MAAS).
    - Ages 5 through 17: "at least 87% below average" in TWO major life areas.
    - Adults 18 and older: "at least 95% below average" in TWO major life areas, OR "at least 95% below average" in ONE major life area. (Quoted exactly. The sheet places the 87% two-area rule in the 5 to 17 column and the 95% rules in the 18+ column; the layout is a three-column table and the "OR" lines may belong to different columns. See Open questions.)
  - Major life areas: self-care, communication (receptive and expressive language), learning, mobility, self-direction, capacity for independent living or economic self-sufficiency.
  - Statutory definitions: 630.005.1(9) RSMo (DD) and 630.005.1(20) RSMo (ID); rule 9 CSR 45-2.010.
  - "MAAS" may be replaced by "other standardized, comprehensive, norm-referenced, psychometrically-sound adaptive functioning assessment." A valid assessment done in the last **3 years** avoids a new MAAS.
- Prerequisites: none. Medicaid (MO HealthNet) is NOT required for DDD eligibility. It decides which service the person gets after eligibility (see 1.3).
- Documents and evidence needed (DDD "Records for Eligibility Determination", 11 Mar 2024):
  - Education: First Steps enrollment, IEP, IEP Evaluation Report, 504 Plan, Missouri Schools for the Severely Disabled record.
  - Medical: genetic testing, neurological testing.
  - Psychological: IQ testing, psychological evaluations, adaptive behavior assessments.
  - Social Security: Disability Determination form **SSA-831**. DDD says it **cannot** use the SSA "Benefit Verification Letter."
  - Legal: guardianship or custody court orders, if any.
  - Signed forms returned to DDD: application (signed referral), Authorization for Disclosure of Consumer Medical-Health Information, Authorized Representative form, Notice of Privacy Practices receipt, Application Acknowledgment form.
- How to apply: call the Regional Office (card 1.1) or use the online referral. Referral data: name, date of birth, Medicaid number if any, disability, communication needs, addresses, phone, email, responsible person data, referrer data.
- Typical time or wait (DDD "Referral and Application Process", 12 Mar 2024):
  - Intake contacts family: within 1 business day of complete referral.
  - Family must return signed packet within **2 months** or referral becomes inactive.
  - DDD requests records: about 5 business days after paperwork returns.
  - Records arrive: "could take up to three (3) months or more."
  - MAAS (if needed): DDD has up to **60 business days** after all paperwork and records arrive. MAAS is virtual (video): about 30 minutes with the person, about 60 minutes with an informant, about 90 minutes total. Results about 5 business days later.
  - Determination: about 5 business days after assessment results.
  - Eligible notice: written within 3 business days of determination. Ineligible: contact within 1 business day and written "Notice of Denial of Eligibility and Right to Appeal."
  - Realistic total: about 1 to 6 months, mostly driven by records. (Advocate estimate from the above steps, not a DDD figure.)
- Cost to family: none.
- Where families get stuck or denied:
  - Records gap: no childhood record shows onset before 18 or 22. Common for adults diagnosed late (for example autism found in adulthood) and for families who moved from another state.
  - Sending the SSA Benefit Verification Letter instead of the SSA-831.
  - Missing the 2-month packet deadline.
  - No device or private space for the virtual MAAS. DDD lists Regional Office, providers, SB40 offices, school, public library, local resource center as places to do it.
  - Adult guardianship papers not provided.
  - Person with only physical disability, or only mental illness, or only a learning disability without functional limits, is not eligible.
- Appeal rights and deadlines (DDD "Eligibility Determination" sheet and 9 CSR 45-2.020): appeal verbally or in writing to the Regional Office Director within **30 calendar days** of the notice; written decision within 10 business days. Then hearing before a non-DDD hearing referee, request within **30 calendar days** of the director's decision; decision within 30 calendar days. Then circuit court under Chapter 536 RSMo. Missouri Protection and Advocacy (800-392-8667) helps "at no charge." If the family hires an attorney or advocate, they must tell the Regional Office Director within 10 business days. Agent 04 owns appeals law.
- Re-opening: a person found eligible before can usually re-open without new records or a new MAAS ("Re-Establishing Division Services," 30 Sep 2024), unless legal status changed (new application needed) or earlier eligibility was based only on "at-risk" criteria for ages 0 to 4.
- Screener questions:
  1. Does the person have a diagnosis of intellectual disability, autism, cerebral palsy, epilepsy, Down syndrome, brain injury, or another condition that started in childhood? (yes/no)
  2. Did the condition start before age 22? (before 18 for intellectual disability) (yes/no/unsure)
  3. Is it expected to last for life? (yes/no)
  4. Does the person need a lot of help in at least 2 of these areas: self-care, communication, learning, moving around, making decisions, living on their own or working? (count 0 to 6)
  5. Do you have school records (IEP), test reports, or an SSA disability decision? (yes/no)
- Screener rule: (has_dd_condition = yes) AND (onset_before_22 = yes OR unsure) AND (lifelong = yes) AND (areas_needing_help >= 2) → "may qualify for DDD services: call Regional Office." If areas_needing_help = 1 and age >= 18 → "may still qualify; ask for an assessment." If onset = unsure → "may qualify; gather childhood records."
- Sources, all checked 2026-10-26: [DDD Eligibility Determination page](https://dmh.mo.gov/dev-disabilities/regional-offices/eligibility); [Division Eligibility sheet, rev. 9/10/24](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston541/files/media/pdf/2024/09/divisioneligibility.pdf); [Referral and Application Process](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston541/files/media/pdf/2024/03/referralandapplicationprocess.pdf); [Records for Eligibility Determination](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston541/files/media/pdf/2024/03/recordsforeligibilitydetermination.pdf); [Intake MAAS Assessment](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston541/files/media/pdf/2024/03/intakemaasassessment.pdf); [Eligibility Determination and appeal rights](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston541/files/media/pdf/2024/03/eligibilitydetermination_0.pdf); [Re-Establishing Division Services](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston541/files/media/pdf/2024/10/restablisingdivisionservices.pdf); [Responsible Person and Authorized Representative](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston541/files/media/pdf/2024/03/responsiblepersonandauthorizedrepinfo.pdf); [DDD Navigating the System flow chart 2026](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston541/files/media/pdf/2026/02/Navigating_the_Division_of_DDD_System_Flow_Chart_2026.pdf).
- Confidence: High.

### 1.3 What happens after eligibility: Support Coordination vs Resource and Referral (DONE)
- Run by: DDD, or a contracted Targeted Case Management (TCM) entity (often the county SB40 board or a nonprofit).
- Rule: the service a person gets depends on **Medicaid status AND county**.
  - Has active MO HealthNet: **Support Coordination** (TCM) in every county, from DDD or a contracted provider.
  - No MO HealthNet: either **Division Resource and Referral** ("Information Specialist System") or **Provider Resource and Referral** ("County Support Specialist"), depending on county. Some counties "have agreed to serve individuals who do not have Medicaid" with full support coordination.
- SW Missouri county split for people WITHOUT Medicaid (DDD "Division Services", 14 Mar 2024):
  - Division Information Specialist (statewide phone line 1-800-241-2741, Information.Specialist@dmh.mo.gov, Monday to Friday 8:00 to 5:00): **Greene, Henry, Laclede, Ozark, Stone, Taney.**
  - County Support Specialist (contracted local provider): **Barry, Barton, Cedar, Christian, Dade, Dallas, Douglas, Hickory, Jasper, Lawrence, McDonald, Newton, Polk, St. Clair, Vernon, Webster, Wright.**
  - Practical meaning: a non-Medicaid adult in Greene County (Springfield) gets a statewide phone line, not a local support coordinator.
- What Support Coordination does: a support coordinator writes the Person-Centered Service Plan (PCSP, also called ISP) with the family at least once a year, links to services, requests waiver funding, does the annual ICF/IID level of care review for waiver users.
- Why Medicaid matters: "New requests for funding require that the individual in need has active Medicaid." The only exception named by DDD is Autism Project funds (card 1.9). Benefit math is agent 06.
- Scale: in FY2022, 110 counties plus St. Louis City had an SB40 board or nonprofit approved to provide support coordination; local support coordination served "nearly 72%" of people eligible for DDD; DDD regional offices employed 218 support coordinators and 22 supervisors. Source: [DMH budget program description, DD Support Coordination (FY2024 budget book)](https://oa.mo.gov/sites/default/files/dmh_support_coordination24.pdf). Confidence: Medium (2022 to 2023 figures).
- Screener questions: Does the person have Missouri Medicaid (MO HealthNet) now? Which county?
- Screener rule: DDD_eligible AND has_medicaid → "You will get a support coordinator." DDD_eligible AND NOT has_medicaid AND county in {Greene, Henry, Laclede, Ozark, Stone, Taney} → "You will get the DDD Information Specialist line, 1-800-241-2741. ### 1.5 DD Medicaid waivers: shared rules (DONE)
- Run by: DDD (operating agency) with MO HealthNet Division (Medicaid agency). Federal 1915(c) waivers.
- Four DD waivers in 2026: **Comprehensive**, **Community Support (CSW)**, **Partnership for Hope (PfH)**, **Missouri Children with Developmental Disabilities (MOCDD)**, also called the **Sarah Jian Lopez Waiver**. (The old DDD Autism Waiver expired 30 June 2017.)
- General DD waiver eligibility (DDD "DD Waiver Eligibility" sheet, 09/2024):
  1. ID before 18 or DD before 22 (that is, DDD eligible).
  2. Eligible for MO HealthNet in a category with federal match. (MOCDD is the exception: it waives parental income for children.)
  3. Meet ICF/IID level of care, and be at risk of ICF/IID placement without waiver services.
  4. Score "at least 95% below average in at least **THREE** major life areas" on the MAAS. (Note: stricter than DDD eligibility, which needs two.)
  5. Need at least one waiver service every month.
- One waiver at a time: a person cannot be on a DD waiver and a waiver from another state agency (for example DHSS waivers) at the same time (9 CSR 45-2.015(5)). Overlap with agent 08 (DHSS home care).
- Funding: about 36% state general revenue and 64% federal, except PfH, where the state share is split equally with local SB40 boards (DDD Waiver Fact Sheet, 22 Jan 2025).
- Access order when funding is limited (9 CSR 45-2.015 and 2.017, current rule version effective 30 April 2023; DDD waiting list page):
  - Priority of Need (PON) score 1 to 5. PON 1 to 4 comes from the overall MAAS score (4 = highest need). PON 5 = verified emergency criteria.
  - Higher PON goes first; ties go to the person who has waited longest.
  - Emergency criteria (9 CSR 45-2.017(1)(E)): (1) immediate need for life-sustaining services (food and shelter, or protection from harm) with no alternative to DDD funding; (2) immediate services needed to protect self or others from imminent physical harm; (3) living in an ICF/DD or nursing facility, can and wants to live in the community; (4) was receiving significant DDD waiver services, still needs them, but lost eligibility due to age; (5) in Children's Division custody with a formal funding agreement, or in a Voluntary Placement Agreement.
  - If a person on the list refuses an offered waiver, a new PON assessment is done (Comprehensive) or the person is removed (MOCDD).
  - A new PON must be done when the person's circumstances change (9 CSR 45-2.017(2)(B)). **Advocate tip: report every change (caregiver illness, hospital stay, school exit, behavior crisis) to the support coordinator in writing and ask for a new PON.**
- Utilization Review (UR): Regional Office reviews the plan, budget and PON within 6 business days; director or designee decides within 5 business days, or asks for more information (support coordinator has 10 business days to answer, then 5 more business days to decide). Decision letter within 10 business days. Only the regional director or assistant regional director can deny or modify (9 CSR 45-2.017(1)(M), (7), (8)).
- Appeals of UR or waiver decisions: to the Regional Director within **30 calendar days** of the decision letter (decision in 10 business days); and/or to the Department of Social Services (MO HealthNet) within **90 calendar days** of written notice. Once the DSS appeal starts, DMH appeal rights end. DSS appeals: MO HealthNet Division, Constituent Services Unit, PO Box 6500, Jefferson City, MO 65102-6500, phone 1-800-392-2161. Services continue during a timely appeal unless the family asks in writing to stop them (9 CSR 45-2.017(9) to (11)). Agent 04 owns detail.
- Rights: waiver eligibility determined "within 60 days of a request" (DDD Waiver Fact Sheet, 2025).
- Services common to waivers: assistive technology, ABA (for children without autism and for adults), community networking, day habilitation, home and vehicle modifications, individualized skill development, personal assistant (self-direction option, Electronic Visit Verification required), remote supports, specialized medical equipment and supplies, support broker, transportation, respite (not PfH per the 2025 fact sheet).
- Sources, checked 2026-10-26: [DD Waiver Eligibility sheet, 09/2024](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston541/files/media/pdf/2024/09/ddwaivereligibility.pdf); [DDD Waiver Fact Sheet, 22 Jan 2025](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston541/files/media/pdf/2025/01/waiverfactsheet012225.pdf); [DDD Medicaid HCBS Waivers page](https://dmh.mo.gov/dev-disabilities/programs/waiver/medicaid-hcb); [9 CSR 45-2 (Secretary of State, current through 3/31/23 update)](https://www.sos.mo.gov/cmsimages/adrules/csr/current/9csr/9c45-2.pdf); [DDD waiting list page](https://dmh.mo.gov/dev-disabilities/waiting-list).
- Confidence: High.

### 1.6 Comprehensive Waiver (DONE)
- Serves: children and adults; only DD waiver with residential services (group home, shared living or host home, Individualized Supported Living (ISL)).
- Rules: ICF/IID level of care; at risk of ICF/IID; needs cannot be met in the Community Support Waiver; "individual requires residential supports" (2025 fact sheet). No individual annual cost cap.
- Size: "over 8,000 people" (DDD page, undated).
- Access in 2026: **new enrollment wait-listed from 15 Oct 2026.** Priority to people meeting emergency criteria who need out-of-home residential care or whose needs exceed other waiver limits (9 CSR 45-2.015(3)(B)).
- Where families get stuck: aging parent caregiver with no crisis yet. Under PON rules, "loss of primary caregiver" counts only when it happens. Families report waiting until a crisis (advocate field observation; see Section 3 for evidence).
- Screener rule: DDD_eligible AND has_medicaid AND needs_24hr_or_residential_support → "Comprehensive Waiver may fit. New slots are wait-listed since 15 Oct 2026; ask the support coordinator to submit the request and PON now to get a place on the list."
- Sources: [DDD Comprehensive Waiver page](https://dmh.mo.gov/node/10486); fact sheet and rule above; checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High.

### 1.7 Community Support Waiver (CSW) (DONE)
- Serves: children and adults who have a place to live in the community, usually with family.
- Rules: ICF/IID level of care; no residential services; **annual waiver cost limit $42,000 per person** (DDD CSW page; DDD fact sheet 22 Jan 2025 says "This limit may be exceeded on a case by case basis"). Year of figure: stated on the 2025 fact sheet, still on the DDD page in Oct 2026.
- Includes: respite (in-home and out-of-home), personal assistant, day habilitation, employment services (career planning, job development, supported employment, prevocational), benefits planning, home-delivered meals, crisis intervention, therapies (OT, PT, speech).
- Size: "over 3,800 individuals" (DDD page, undated).
- Access in 2026: **new enrollment wait-listed from 15 Oct 2026.**
- Screener rule: DDD_eligible AND has_medicaid AND lives_with_family_or_own_home AND NOT needs_residential → "CSW may fit (up to $42,000 a year). Wait-listed since 15 Oct 2026."

### 1.8 Partnership for Hope Waiver (PfH) (DONE)
- Serves: children and adults in participating counties.
- Rules: Medicaid eligible; DDD eligible; ICF/IID level of care; needs can be met within **$12,362 per year** (up to **$15,000** in "special circumstances," per the 2025 fact sheet); no residential services; lives in a participating county; meets crisis or priority criteria.
- PfH access criteria (9 CSR 45-2.015(3)(A)): crisis first (serious risk of immediate harm or death; loss of primary caregiver or caregiver cannot meet needs; abuse, neglect or exploitation). If no crisis, priority criteria by waiting time: needs substantial accommodation the caregiver cannot provide; has used up school and Vocational Rehabilitation benefits (or is not VR eligible) and needs employment services; has received local (county) funded supports for 3 months or more that the waiver can cover; leaving a non-Medicaid residential care facility.
- Unique services: dental, family peer support, temporary residential services.
- Funding: county SB40 boards pay half of the state share.
- **Participating counties in our area (DDD map dated September 2025):** all 23 counties EXCEPT **Wright** and **Douglas**. Douglas and Wright residents cannot use PfH. Source: [DDD PfH county map, Sept 2025](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston541/files/media/pdf/2025/11/PartnershipforHopeMap.pdf) (read from the map image).
- Size: "over 2,100 individuals" (DDD page, undated).
- Access in 2026: **new enrollment wait-listed from 15 Oct 2026.**
- Screener rule: DDD_eligible AND has_medicaid AND county NOT IN {Douglas, Wright} AND needs_under_about_12k_per_year → "PfH may fit. Wait-listed since 15 Oct 2026."
- Sources: [DDD PfH page](https://dmh.mo.gov/node/10501); [PfH map](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston541/files/media/pdf/2025/11/PartnershipforHopeMap.pdf); [DDD Waiver Fact Sheet 2025](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston541/files/media/pdf/2025/01/waiverfactsheet012225.pdf); 9 CSR 45-2.015; checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High (cap, rules); Medium (county list read from a map image).

### 1.9 MOCDD (Sarah Jian Lopez) Waiver (DONE)
- Serves: children **under 18** living at home with family. Relevant to our 16 and 17 year olds only.
- Rules: DDD eligible; ICF/IID level of care; not eligible for MO HealthNet for Kids or MO HealthNet for Disabled Children because of parental income or resources; must be found disabled through Family Support Division using medical records; needs at least one waiver service monthly. Parental income and resources are not counted ("deeming" waived).
- Capacity: **416 children** statewide. Placed on a wait list when eligible; "On a quarterly basis, available MOCDD waiver slots are offered to children on the wait list who have the most critical unmet needs."
- Not for: children who need only state plan services such as private duty nursing.
- At 18: MOCDD ends. Advocate tip: begin adult Medicaid (SSI-related) planning and adult waiver request before the 18th birthday; emergency criterion 4 (lost eligibility due to age) may give PON 5. Overlap with agents 06 and 07.
- Exempt from the Oct 2026 wait-list memo ("will continue to be managed as usual"), but it already had its own wait list.

## 2. Order and dependencies (DONE)

1. **DDD eligibility comes before everything in the DD system**: support coordination, any DD waiver, SWMAP, DD Health Home, and almost all county SB40 board help.
2. **Records come before the eligibility decision.** Gather IEP, evaluation reports, IQ and adaptive tests, SSA-831 before or at referral. A valid adaptive test from the last 3 years can replace the MAAS.
3. **MO HealthNet (Medicaid) comes before support coordination in 6 SW counties** (Greene, Henry, Laclede, Ozark, Stone, Taney) and **before any new DDD-funded service** except SWMAP. Medicaid rules: agent 06.
4. **Support coordinator comes before a waiver request.** Only the support coordinator submits the plan, budget and PON to Utilization Review.
5. **PON and ISP submission come before a place on the wait list.** Ask the support coordinator to submit now, even with the October 2026 freeze, so the waiting date starts.
6. **Waiver criteria (3 major life areas, ICF/IID level of care, monthly service need) are checked after DDD eligibility (2 areas).** A person can be DDD eligible and still not waiver eligible.
7. **One waiver at a time.** Choosing a DHSS waiver (agent 08) blocks a DD waiver at the same time, and the reverse.
8. **MOCDD ends at 18.** Adult Medicaid and adult waiver planning must start before the 18th birthday (agents 06 and 07).
9. **School and VR come before PfH employment priority.** PfH priority criterion: the person has used up school and VR benefits or is not VR eligible.
10. **County local funding for 3+ months can create PfH priority.** Getting SB40-funded supports first can help PfH access later.
11. **Appeal deadlines start on the date of the letter**: 30 calendar days (DMH) and 90 calendar days (DSS). File within 30 days to keep the DMH route and to keep services going.

## 3. Gaps and failure points (DONE)

1. **New waiver wait list with no end date and no public count.** From 15 Oct 2026 new enrollment into Comprehensive, CSW and PfH is wait-listed for budget reasons. The DDD page's monthly processing list and wait list report sections hold no numbers. Families and professionals cannot see list length or movement. Source: [Wait List Memo](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston541/files/media/pdf/2026/10/wait%20list%20memo%202026.pdf); [waiting list page](https://dmh.mo.gov/dev-disabilities/waiting-list).
2. **Crisis-driven access.** Priority goes to PON 5 (verified emergency) and higher MAAS scores. Rule text makes "loss of primary caregiver" count when it happens, not when it is predictable (aging parent). Source: 9 CSR 45-2.015(3)(A), 2.017(1)(E).
3. **Medicaid gate for DD-eligible adults.** In Greene (Springfield), Henry, Laclede, Ozark, Stone and Taney, a DD-eligible person without Medicaid gets only the statewide Information Specialist line. Across all counties, "New requests for funding require that the individual in need has active Medicaid." Only SWMAP (autism) and county boards fund non-Medicaid people. Source: [Division Services](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston541/files/media/pdf/2024/03/divisionservices_1.pdf); [DDD family info](https://dmh.mo.gov/dev-disabilities/individual-family-guardian-info).
4. **Eligible for DDD but not for any waiver.** DDD eligibility for adults needs 2 areas; waivers need 3 areas plus ICF/IID level of care. Source: [Division Eligibility](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston541/files/media/pdf/2024/09/divisioneligibility.pdf); [DD Waiver Eligibility](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/g/files/zuston541/files/media/pdf/2024/09/ddwaivereligibility.pdf).
5. **Geographic inequality.** 10 of 23 counties have no SB40 levy (MACDDS map). Douglas and Wright are not in PfH (DDD map Sept 2025). A family in Stone County can get up to $3,000 a year from its board; a family in Dade or Vernon has no county fund.
6. **Slow, paper-heavy intake.** Records "could take up to three (3) months or more"; MAAS up to 60 business days after records; referral closes if the packet is not back in 2 months; SSA Benefit Verification Letter is rejected (SSA-831 needed); MAAS needs video access. Source: DDD intake sheets (card 1.2).
7. **Late-diagnosed adults.** Adults diagnosed after 22 (for example autism in adulthood) must prove childhood onset with records that may not exist.
8. **Errors in official contact information** (screener and guide must not copy blindly):
   - DMH lists Stone County as "Stoney."
   - Springfield Regional Office ZIP: 65801 (office page) vs 65810 (DDD directory); Stone board lists "515 E. Pythian, PO Box 5030."
   - DMH Constituent Services mailing address prints "Jefferson City, Mo 6510" (ZIP incomplete).
   - Abilities First prints 417-866-0404 and 417-886-0404.
   - RSB brochure contact link returns 404.
   - MODDC wait list page mixes DD waiver steps with home care and Medicaid application steps (card 1.21).
9. **MOCDD is tiny and ends at 18**: 416 slots statewide; own wait list; quarterly offers.
10. **Autism Project money is small for the area**: SWMAP FY2026 $1,845,138 for 23 counties, and it is last resort.
11. **Sensory: no local front door.** MCDHH is in Jefferson City and mainly refers; no Deaf-led service agency found in Springfield; DMH Deaf specialized outpatient care is in St. Louis and Lee's Summit only (secondary); DeafBlind SSP vendor is in St. Louis. MSB and MSD outreach stop at 21.
12. **No local Arc advocacy chapter or confirmed People First chapter.** The Arc of the Ozarks is a provider. Families who want an independent advocate must use statewide bodies (Mo P&A, MOF2F, MPACT).
13. **TCM provider identity is hard to find.** No public DMH list maps each county to its TCM provider. Families learn it only from the eligibility letter.

## 6. Advocate's escalation playbook (DONE)

Rule zero: **get it in writing.** Ask for every decision, denial or "no slot" answer as a written letter with the reason and appeal rights. Keep a log: date, person, what was said. Deadlines run from the letter date. Legal detail: agent 04.

**Level 1. The worker.** Intake Specialist (before eligibility) or support coordinator (after). Ask: "What is missing? What is the date you sent my request? What is my PON and wait list date?"

**Level 2. The supervisor.** Intake Supervisor at the Regional Office, or the support coordinator's supervisor or agency director (for example Abilities First, CC Links, LCBDD, WC Partners, Center for Human Services, LinkAbility). For county board grant denials, the board's executive director (Stone County has a written appeal route).

**Level 3. The Regional Office leadership.**
- Springfield Regional Office: 417-895-7400; Regional Director Julie Lillich.
- Joplin Satellite Office: 417-629-3020; Assistant Satellite Office Director Russell Harbaugh.
- Assistant Deputy Director, West/Central Region: Melissa Jones.
- The DDD eligibility rights sheet gives a "Right to discuss the decision with the Intake Specialist, Intake Supervisor, or Regional Office Director/Designee." Only the Regional Director or Assistant Regional Director can deny or modify a plan in Utilization Review.

**Level 4. DDD central office.** Division Director Sheila Wunning, 1706 East Elm Street, P.O. Box 687, Jefferson City, MO 65102; 573-751-4054 (memo letterhead, 15 Oct 2026). The wait list memo says questions go to the local Regional Office Director or Assistant Director first.

**Level 5. DMH Office of Constituent Services** (advocate for DMH consumers; rights, abuse, neglect, provider concerns): toll-free 1-800-364-9687; local 573-751-8088; constituentsvcs@dmh.mo.gov. Source: [DMH Constituent Services](https://dmh.mo.gov/constituent-services).

**Level 6. Missouri Protection and Advocacy**: 1-800-392-8667 (TDD 1-800-735-2966). Free help with DD eligibility appeals is named in DDD's own appeal rights sheet.

**Formal appeals (short form; agent 04 owns detail):**
- DDD eligibility denial: Regional Office Director within 30 calendar days of the notice (verbal or written) → hearing referee within 30 calendar days → circuit court (Chapter 536 RSMo). Tell the Regional Office Director within 10 business days if you get a lawyer or advocate.
- Plan, budget or waiver denial or cut (Utilization Review): Regional Director within 30 calendar days of the decision letter (decision in 10 business days), and/or DSS MO HealthNet hearing within 90 calendar days (MO HealthNet Division Constituent Services Unit, PO Box 6500, Jefferson City, MO 65102-6500; 1-800-392-2161). Starting the DSS appeal ends the DMH appeal route. A timely appeal keeps current services in place.

**Parallel tools while waiting:**
- Ask for a **new PON** after any change (caregiver illness or death, hospital stay, school exit, behavior crisis, eviction). Ask in writing whether the situation meets an **emergency criterion** in 9 CSR 45-2.017(1)(E); if yes, ask for PON 5 verification by the Regional Director.
- Ask for SWMAP funds (autism), county SB40 funds, DD Health Home, and state plan Medicaid services that do not need a waiver (agent 06 and 08).
- Abuse, neglect or exploitation: Adult Abuse and Neglect Hotline 800-392-0210 (as listed by MODDC; DHSS line, agent 08 to confirm), plus DMH Constituent Services.
- Systemic problem (wait list, budget): MODDC, legislators. Agent 05 owns political action.
