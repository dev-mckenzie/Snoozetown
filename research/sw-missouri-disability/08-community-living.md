## 1. Program and resource cards

### A. Help at home: Missouri DHSS, Division of Senior and Disability Services (DSDS)

### DSDS Home and Community Based Services (HCBS): front door, assessment, and level of care
- Run by: Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services (DHSS), Division of Senior and Disability Services (DSDS). MO HealthNet (Department of Social Services, DSS) pays.
- Serves (ages, disability types): Adults 18 and older with any disability or age-related need (physical, IDD, sensory, medically complex, cognitive). "18 years of age or older" is the minimum for all HCBS programs. No homebound rule.
- Eligibility rules (exact numbers and year):
  - Active MO HealthNet (Medicaid) with a matching Medicaid Eligibility (ME) code. DSDS will not accept a referral for a person who is not Medicaid eligible or not age eligible on the date of the request. Exception: people who appear eligible for "Home and Community Based (HCB) Medicaid" are sent to the DSS Family Support Division (FSD) first. Spenddown cases are not processed until the spenddown is met (Miller trust exception through FSD). Income and spenddown math: see agent 06.
  - Nursing facility level of care (LOC): the person must score **18 points or more** on the DSDS level of care algorithm (interRAI HC tool with Missouri changes). LOC section revised September 2024. 12 scored categories: Cognition, Eating, Behavioral, Toileting, Bathing, Treatments, Dressing and Grooming, Rehabilitation, Meal Prep, Medication Management, Mobility, Safety. Most categories score 0, 3, 6, 9, or 18. History: the threshold was 21, then 24 from 1 July 2017, then lowered to 18 (secondary/older sources).
  - An unmet need that HCBS can meet.
  - Must agree to a face-to-face assessment, sign the participant choice statement, and make a person-centered care plan (PCCP).
  - Annual reassessment.
- Cost cap (2026): HCBS is capped at a "cost maximum" tied to the average statewide monthly nursing facility cost. Effective 1 July 2026: 100% = $5,593.32 per month; 60% = $3,355.99 per month. Basic personal care (60% column) max 412 units (15-minute units) per month. Consumer directed personal care (state plan) max 641 units per month.
- Prerequisites: MO HealthNet first. A Missouri address. For waivers, see each waiver card.
- Documents and evidence needed: Medicaid number (DCN). Diagnosis and medical information. DSDS can send a "Healthcare Professional Inquiry" or a General Health Evaluation form to the doctor. DSDS has a "Preparing for an assessment" brochure (2026).
- How to apply in SW Missouri:
  1. Call the HCBS Call Center: **866-835-3505** (statewide; the line is "reserved for participants and their supports"). Hours are not posted on the current page.
  2. Or use the online referral: https://modhss.entellitrak.com/etk-modhss-prod/page.request.do?page=portal.referral (for people NOT already authorized for HCBS).
  3. Or email the HCBS-1 form to HCBSCallCenterReferrals@health.mo.gov.
  4. Anyone can refer: family, friends, doctors, providers.
  5. DSDS (or a contracted provider for reassessments) visits the home for the interRAI assessment.
- Typical time or wait: Not published. The intake manual (revised April 2025) gives no day count from referral to assessment. State plan personal care has no waiting list. Some waivers have slot limits (see below).
- Cost to family: None for the service. Spenddown or patient liability may apply (agent 06).
- Where families get stuck or denied:
  - No active Medicaid on the referral date, so the referral is refused. Families think DSDS will "do Medicaid" for them.
  - Score below 18 points. Young adults with IDD who can do physical tasks but need prompts and supervision may score low, because the tool scores hands-on help more than supervision. (Field observation; not verified against data. Open question.)
  - The interRAI visit happens on a "good day" or the person over-reports what they can do.
  - Being in a DMH DD waiver: a person can be in only one Medicaid HCBS waiver at a time.
- Appeal rights and deadlines (HCBS Manual 6.00 and 5.00): DSDS mails an Adverse Action Notice (HCBS-12) within 3 business days of a decision to deny, reduce, or close. **Appeal within 10 calendar days** of the mail date to keep services at the current level during the appeal (the family may owe the cost if DSDS wins). **90 calendar days** from the mail date to appeal a denial. The DSS Division of Legal Services (DLS) holds the hearing. A request may be verbal or written; anyone can make it, but DSDS must confirm it with the participant or guardian (3 tries on 3 business days, else not processed). Legal detail: agent 04.
- Screener questions:
  1. Is the person 18 or older?
  2. Does the person have MO HealthNet (Medicaid) now?
  3. Does the person need hands-on help or a lot of supervision with bathing, dressing, toileting, eating, moving around, medications, or meals?
  4. Does the person live in Missouri in a private home (not a nursing facility)?
- Screener rule: age >= 18 AND has MO HealthNet (or may qualify; send to FSD first) AND needs help with daily tasks THEN "May qualify for DSDS HCBS; call 866-835-3505." Final LOC (18 points) is decided only by DSDS assessment; the screener cannot decide it.
- Sources:
  - [DHSS HCBS Referrals, Requests and Eligibility Determination](https://health.mo.gov/partners-providers/home-community-based-services-provider-information/hcbs-referrals-requests-eligibility-determination), checked 2026-10-26
  - [DHSS HCBS Eligibility, Process and Program Limits](https://health.mo.gov/senior-disability-services/home-community-based-services/hcbs-eligibility-process-program-limits), checked 2026-10-26
  - [HCBS Manual 4.10 Explanation of Level of Care Determination (rev. Sept 2024)](https://health.mo.gov/providers/manuals/home-and-community-based-services-manual/400-home-and-community-based-services-17), checked 2026-10-26

### DSDS State Plan Personal Care, agency model (In-Home Services)
- Run by: DSDS authorizes; a Medicaid-enrolled home care agency employs the aide.
- Serves: Adults 18+ who meet HCBS rules above.
- Eligibility rules: Same as HCBS front door (Medicaid, 18+, LOC 18 points). Services: Basic Personal Care, Advanced Personal Care (devices or procedures, for example transfer devices, catheter care), Authorized Nurse Visits (stable chronic conditions; 26 units in 6 months). Basic personal care max 412 units (103 hours) per month in 2026 (60% column). Advanced personal care and nurse visits can go up to 100% of the cost maximum.
- Prerequisites: MO HealthNet; DSDS assessment.
- Documents: as above.
- How to apply: HCBS Call Center 866-835-3505 or online referral. The person picks an enrolled agency from the list DSDS gives.
- Typical time or wait: No waiting list for the state plan service. The real wait is finding an agency with a worker (see Gaps).
- Cost to family: None (spenddown may apply).
- Where families get stuck: Under the agency model "the caregiver cannot be an immediate family member." Agencies in rural counties may accept the case but not staff it.
- Appeal rights and deadlines: as HCBS front door (10 days to keep services; 90 days for denial).
- Screener questions: As HCBS front door. Plus: Does the family want an agency to send a worker?
- Screener rule: HCBS front door rule AND family does not need to hire its own worker.
- Sources: [DHSS HCBS Services](https://health.mo.gov/partners-providers/home-community-based-services-provider-information/hcbs-services); [DHSS HCBS FAQ](https://health.mo.gov/senior-disability-services/home-community-based-services/frequently-asked-questions-about-hcbs); [Cost Maximums 2026](https://health.mo.gov/providers/manuals/home-and-community-based-services-manual/300-available-home-and-community-15); all checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: High

### DSDS Consumer Directed Services (CDS), state plan Personal Care Assistance
- Run by: DSDS authorizes. The participant (or a representative, see Open questions) hires and directs the attendant. A CDS "vendor" (a private agency enrolled with the Missouri Medicaid Audit and Compliance Unit, MMAC) does payroll, training, and EVV.
- Serves: Adults 18+ with a physical disability.
- Eligibility rules (HCBS Manual 3.25, rev. January 2026):
  - At least 18 years old.
  - "Physically disabled" as defined in 19 CSR 15-8.100: needs another person's help for routine tasks because of loss, or loss of use, of neurological, muscular, or skeletal function.
  - Able to "self-direct": supervise the attendant, verify wages and time, monitor Electronic Visit Verification (EVV), report changes.
  - Active Medicaid with the right ME code; nursing facility LOC (18 points).
  - No prior Medicaid fraud.
  - Cap: "CDS shall not exceed 60% of the cost maximum" (2026: $3,355.99 per month; 641 units per month). Combined CDS and agency personal care also capped at 60%.
- Who can be the paid attendant: anyone except a legal spouse or the legal guardian. A parent who is NOT the legal guardian can be paid. The attendant cannot be a current CDS participant.
- Prerequisites: MO HealthNet; LOC; pick an enrolled CDS vendor.
- Documents: as HCBS front door. Attendant needs employment and background paperwork through the vendor.
- How to apply: 866-835-3505 or online referral. Tell DSDS the person wants CDS. DSDS uses a "Self-Direction Assessment Questions" form (Manual 4.00 Appendix 10).
- Current vendor model (2026): Multiple private CDS vendors, chosen by the participant. **New CDS vendor enrollment is frozen.** From 1 August 2026, DSS/MO HealthNet and MMAC, with CMS, put a 6-month temporary moratorium on new CDS/Personal Care and DME provider enrollments statewide (can extend in 6-month steps up to 1 year). Enrolled vendors keep serving participants. I found no evidence of a move to one single statewide CDS vendor (see Open questions).
- Typical time or wait: No waiting list for state plan CDS.
- Cost to family: None for the service.
- Where families get stuck or denied:
  - **Guardianship conflict:** a parent who becomes legal guardian can no longer be the paid CDS attendant. This links guardianship choice (agent 04 and 07) to home care pay. Families are rarely told before court.
  - "Self-direct" test: a person with significant IDD may be found unable to self-direct. The 19 CSR 15-8 framework is built around physical disability.
  - The participant must manage EVV and timesheets; errors are treated as fraud risk (HB 1682, 2020, timesheet fraud notice).
- Appeal rights: as HCBS front door.
- Screener questions: (1) 18+? (2) Medicaid? (3) Physical disability that needs hands-on help? (4) Can the person (or a representative, if allowed) direct the worker? (5) Is the hoped-for worker the spouse or legal guardian?
- Screener rule: HCBS front door rule AND physical disability AND can self-direct AND chosen worker is not spouse and not legal guardian.
- Sources:
  - [HCBS Manual 3.25 Personal Care Assistance, CDS (rev. Jan 2026)](https://health.mo.gov/providers/manuals/home-and-community-based-services-manual/300-available-home-and-community-based-4), checked 2026-10-26
  - [DHSS HCBS FAQ, "Who can be my caregiver?"](https://health.mo.gov/senior-disability-services/home-community-based-services/frequently-asked-questions-about-hcbs), checked 2026-10-26
  - [DHSS memo INFO 07-26-03, CDS Moratorium (posted 27 July 2026)](https://health.mo.gov/our-programs-services/home-and-community-based-services/memos/info-07-26-03-consumer-directed), checked 2026-10-26
  - [MMAC notice, DME and CDS temporary new enrollment moratoria (posted 24 July 2026)](https://mmac.mo.gov/?p=11413), checked 2026-10-26
  - [MMAC CDS Proposal for Contract page ("current moratorium")](https://mmac.mo.gov/providers/provider-enrollment/home-and-community-based-services/contract-proposal-information/test-page-cds-cds-proposal-for-contract/), checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: High for rules and moratorium. Low for "single state vendor" question.

### DSDS waivers (one waiver at a time; DMH DD waivers count)
Rule for all: "Medicaid participants can only receive services through one Medicaid HCBS waiver at a time," whichever agency runs it. A person can get DSDS **state plan** personal care and DMH DD personal assistant services together, but state plan personal care must be used first. People in DD Shared Living, Residential Habilitation, or Individualized Supported Living (ISL) cannot get DSDS state plan personal care. DD waiver detail: agent 01. Source: [HCBS Manual 4.35.1 DMH Service Coordination](https://health.mo.gov/providers/manuals/home-and-community-based-services-manual/400-home-and-community-based-services-22), checked 2026-10-26.

#### Aged and Disabled Waiver (ADW)
- Run by: DSDS. Serves: age **63 or over**, nursing facility LOC. Services: homemaker, chore, respite, home delivered meals, adult day care.
- Respite under ADW (Manual 3.50, rev. July 2026): participant at least 63; has an unpaid caregiver who regularly gives care; respite aide must be an agency employee and not immediate family; Basic and Advanced respite; max 49 hours per week; max 868 units per month combined.
- Screener rule: age >= 63 AND HCBS front door rule.
- Relevance: aging adults with IDD who are 63+ and not in a DD waiver; also aging parents themselves.
- Sources: [DSS MO HealthNet ADW page](https://dss.mo.gov/mhd/waiver/aged-and-disabled); [HCBS Manual 3.50 Respite (rev. July 2026)](https://health.mo.gov/providers/manuals/home-and-community-based-services-manual/300-available-home-and-community-based-5); checked 2026-10-26. Confidence: High.

#### Adult Day Care Waiver (ADCW)
- Run by: DSDS. Serves: disabled adults **18 to 63**; first authorization only ages 18 to 62; at 63 move to ADW. Nursing facility LOC; active Medicaid (not Blind Pension). Licensed adult day care center. Up to 10 hours a day, 5 days a week; transport up to 120 minutes per day.
- Where families get stuck: needs a licensed adult day center that takes Medicaid within reach. Few in rural counties (not verified; Open questions).
- Screener rule: 18 <= age <= 62 (new) AND HCBS front door rule AND a licensed adult day care center is within reach.
- Sources: [HCBS Manual 3.31 ADCW (rev. Jan 2018)](https://health.mo.gov/providers/manuals/home-and-community-based-services-manual/300-available-home-and-community-11); [DHSS HCBS Services](https://health.mo.gov/partners-providers/home-community-based-services-provider-information/hcbs-services); checked 2026-10-26. Confidence: High (manual section is old but current page repeats ages).

#### Independent Living Waiver (ILW)
- Run by: DSDS. Serves: ages **18 to 64 at first enrollment** with a physical disability (19 CSR 15-8) who can self-direct CDS; cognitive impairment counts only if onset is at or after age 22 (so most people with IDD do not fit). Can stay after 65 if still able to self-direct.
- Services: CDS personal care above state plan limits (max 868 units per month), case management (required), Environmental Accessibility Adaptations (max **$5,000 in 5 years**), Specialized Medical Equipment (max **$5,000 in 5 years**), Specialized Medical Supplies (for example incontinence supplies), Financial Management Services.
- Slots: limited each waiver year (1 July to 30 June); waitlist prioritized by LOC score; managed by Bureau of Federal Programs. ILW waitlist notices carry the 90-day appeal right.
- Screener rule: 18 <= age <= 64 AND physical disability AND can self-direct AND in or eligible for CDS AND HCBS front door rule.
- Sources: [HCBS Manual 3.55 ILW (rev. July 2026)](https://health.mo.gov/providers/manuals/home-and-community-based-services-manual/300-available-home-and-community-based-7), checked 2026-10-26. Confidence: High.

#### Medically Complex Adult Waiver (MCAW), formerly Medically Fragile Adult Waiver
- Run by: DHSS Special Health Care Needs (SHCN). Serves: age **21+** who have aged out of the Medically Complex Children and Youth (MCCY) Program and were eligible for private duty nursing there. MO HealthNet fee-for-service (not managed care), federally matched ME code, ICF level of care, not in another waiver.
- Services: private duty nursing, advanced personal care, personal care aide visits, RN visits, specialized medical supplies, service coordination.
- How to apply: SHCN regional office referral; phone **573-751-6246**; email shcn@health.mo.gov.
- Screener rule: age >= 21 AND had MCCY private duty nursing AND fee-for-service MO HealthNet.
- Where families get stuck: the transition at 21 from children's private duty nursing. Plan before 20. (Transition detail: agent 07.)
- Sources: [DHSS MCAW program page](https://health.mo.gov/senior-disability-services/special-health-care-needs/medically-complex-adult-waiver-program), checked 2026-10-26. Confidence: High.

#### Brain Injury Waiver (BIW)
- Run by: DHSS Bureau of Long-Term Services and Supports. Serves: ages **21 to 65** with medical documentation of a traumatic brain injury (TBI), fee-for-service MO HealthNet, nursing home LOC, not in another waiver.
- Cap: BIW services may not exceed **$32,000 a year** per participant (fact sheet, created January 2025).
- Services: ABA, assistive technology, cognitive rehab, environmental access and modification, neuropsych evaluation, OT, PT, speech, personal care after state plan.
- How to refer: Waivers.LTSS@health.mo.gov; **573-751-6246** or toll-free **800-451-0669**.
- Screener rule: 21 <= age <= 65 AND documented TBI AND HCBS front door rule.
- Sources: [BIW Fact Sheet PDF](https://health.mo.gov/sites/health/files/media/pdf/2026/03/BIW%20Fact%20Sheet.pdf), checked 2026-10-26. Confidence: High.

#### Structured Family Caregiving Waiver (SFCW)
- Run by: DSDS. Serves: age **21+ at entry** with **Alzheimer's or a related dementia** diagnosed by a Missouri-licensed physician (RSMo 172.800). Must live full time with the primary caregiver, who already lives there and gives care. Caregiver may be family or legal guardian. Per diem based on 60% of the nursing facility daily rate; provider keeps no more than 35%. Limited slots per waiver year.
- Relevance to IDD: adults with Down syndrome have high dementia risk; a parent who lives with an adult child with IDD and dementia could be paid even as guardian. Check eligibility case by case. (Medical detail: agent 03.)
- Screener rule: age >= 21 AND dementia diagnosis AND lives full time with caregiver AND HCBS front door rule.
- Sources: [HCBS Manual 3.60 SFCW (rev. March 2025)](https://health.mo.gov/providers/manuals/home-and-community-based-services-manual/300-available-home-and-community-based-8), checked 2026-10-26. Confidence: High.

### B. A break for the caregiver: respite

Summary for the screener: for an adult with IDD under 63, the main paid respite routes are (1) DMH DD waiver respite (agent 01), (2) a county SB40 board, if that county funds it, and (3) the Area Agency on Aging National Family Caregiver Support Program when the parent is 55 or older. DSDS respite is only for people 63+ (ADW). Missouri has **no** State Respite Coalition and **no** active Lifespan Respite program on the ARCH national map.

### DMH Division of Developmental Disabilities respite (pointer card)
- Run by: DMH Division of DD, through the Springfield Regional Office (417-895-7400, 1515 E. Pythian, Springfield) or the Joplin Satellite Office (417-629-3020, 3600 E. Newman Rd., Joplin), and through support coordination (often a county SB40 board).
- Serves: People found eligible for DMH DD services. Respite is a DD waiver service and may also be paid with state general revenue "pending availability of funds" (secondary source).
- Eligibility rules, wait lists, and waiver caps: **agent 01 owns this.**
- Where families get stuck: no respite worker available, even with an authorization (see Workforce gap).
- Screener rule: has DMH DD eligibility THEN ask support coordinator about respite in the plan.
- Sources: [DMH Springfield Regional Office](https://dmh.mo.gov/dev-disabilities/regional-offices/springfield), checked 2026-10-26.
- Confidence: High for office contacts. Respite rules deferred to agent 01.

### Area Agency on Aging: National Family Caregiver Support Program (NFCSP), older relative caregivers
- Run by: The local Area Agency on Aging (AAA), with federal Older Americans Act funds through DHSS.
- Serves: This is the key route for **aging parents**. Missouri DHSS lists as eligible "Older relatives, including parents, aged 55 or older providing care to adults with disabilities aged 18-59." Also caregivers of anyone 60+, and caregivers of a person of any age with Alzheimer's or dementia.
- Services: information, counseling, support groups, caregiver training, **respite care**, and supplemental services (for example minor home modifications, medical or incontinence supplies, assistive technology, one-time help). An older AAA resource sheet (secondary) says respite is awarded on a "caregiver burden" basis and the caregiver does not have to live with the care receiver.
- Eligibility rules: caregiver age 55+ AND care receiver age 18-59 with a disability (for the parent route). No income test stated on the DHSS page. Funds are limited.
- Prerequisites: none stated (no Medicaid needed).
- How to apply: Missouri Senior Resource Line **1-800-235-5503** (enter ZIP code, transfers to the local AAA). AAAs for our 23 counties (from the Missouri Association of Area Agencies on Aging, ma4web.org):
  - **SeniorAge** (Springfield), 417-862-0762 or 800-497-0822, 1735 S. Fort Ave., Springfield 65807: Barry, Christian, Dade, Dallas, Douglas, Greene, Lawrence, Ozark, Polk, Stone, Taney, Webster, Wright (plus Howell, Oregon, Shannon, Texas outside our area).
  - **Region X AAA** (Joplin), 417-781-7562: Barton, Jasper, McDonald, Newton.
  - **Care Connection for Aging Services** (Warrensburg), 660-747-3107 or 800-748-7826: Cedar, Henry, Hickory, St. Clair, Vernon.
  - **Aging Best** (Columbia), 573-443-5823 or 800-369-5211: Laclede.
- Typical time or wait: not published. Funds may run out within a year (Open question).
- Cost to family: none stated; contributions may be asked.
- Where families get stuck: parents of adults with IDD do not think of the "aging" network as theirs. The SeniorAge caregiver page lists "respite care resources" but no rules or amounts.
- Appeal rights: AAA grievance process (not researched; Open question).
- Screener questions: (1) Is the main unpaid caregiver 55 or older? (2) Is the person cared for 18 to 59 with a disability? (3) Or is the person cared for 60+, or living with dementia?
- Screener rule: (caregiver_age >= 55 AND 18 <= person_age <= 59 AND has disability) OR person_age >= 60 OR dementia THEN "Call 1-800-235-5503 and ask for Family Caregiver Support respite."
- Sources:
  - [DHSS AAA Caregiver Services](https://health.mo.gov/partners-providers/area-agencies-aging/caregiver-services), checked 2026-10-26
  - [DHSS Area Agencies on Aging](https://health.mo.gov/partners-providers/area-agencies-aging), checked 2026-10-26
  - [ma4web.org Find Your Local Agency (association; secondary)](https://www.ma4web.org/find-local-agency/), checked 2026-10-26
  - [SeniorAge home and caregiver support](https://www.senioragemo.org/caregiver-support), checked 2026-10-26
  - [ACL NFCSP fact sheet 2014 (older federal wording excluded parents; superseded, shown for context only)](https://acl.gov/sites/default/files/news%202017-03/National_Family_Caregiver_Support_Program2014.pdf), checked 2026-10-26
- Confidence: High for eligibility wording and contacts. Medium for county-to-AAA map (association site, matches DHSS reference).

### Missouri Caregiver Program (dementia)
- Run by: DHSS, contracted to Memory Care Home Solutions.
- Serves: caregivers who live with a person with probable Alzheimer's disease or a related dementia, in Missouri; not in a long-term care facility. Free. First come, first served; depends on funding.
- Services: caregiver training, in-home assessment, "Customized Caregiver Relief" (money and assistive technology for respite and products). Will not pay for anything Medicare or Medicaid pays.
- How to apply: Memory Care Home Solutions **(314) 645-6247**, memorycarehs.org. Questions: DHSS Senior Programs 573-526-4542.
- Relevance: adults with Down syndrome who develop dementia; aging parents with dementia.
- Screener rule: dementia diagnosis (probable) AND caregiver lives in same home AND not in a facility.
- Sources: [DHSS Missouri Caregiver Program](https://health.mo.gov/senior-disability-services/senior-programs/missouri-caregiver-program), checked 2026-10-26. Confidence: High.

### C. Getting around: transport

### City Utilities of Springfield (CU Transit) Access Express paratransit
- Run by: City Utilities of Springfield, Transit (CU Transit). ADA complementary paratransit.
- Serves: any age; people who cannot use the CU fixed-route bus because of a disability or health condition, for all trips or some trips.
- Eligibility rules (Policies and Procedures, updated 1 July 2025):
  - Eligibility is functional: inability to use the fixed route bus. Not diagnosis based.
  - "A physician, health professional, or rehabilitation professional must verify all applications" (Professional Verification page is part of the form).
  - Eligibility codes: Code B (unconditional), Code C (conditional, trip by trip; may be sent to fixed route). Code A exists in policy for bus-not-accessible situations.
  - Service area: within **3/4 mile of any City Utilities bus route** (ADA). Curb-to-curb as the basic mode; origin-to-destination help on request (drivers do not go inside).

### MO HealthNet Non-Emergency Medical Transportation (NEMT)
- Run by: DSS MO HealthNet Division (MHD), through one statewide broker. The NEMT Manual posted on dss.mo.gov (file dated April 2026) names **MTM Health** as the broker. Older DSS "hot tip" pages (2014, 2015) still name "LogistiCare Solutions" and should be ignored.
- Serves: MO HealthNet participants going to a MO HealthNet-covered medical service, with no other free transport.
- Eligibility rules (NEMT Manual 2026):
  - Not covered: ME codes listed in the manual (for example 02 Blind Pension, 55 QMB only, 82 Missouri Rx, presumptive eligibility non-subsidized, some premium CHIP and foster care codes); people with free transport available; people with Medicare NEMT; hospice (for the terminal illness); **people in a MO HealthNet Managed Care health plan** (the plan arranges rides; call the number on the plan card).
  - **Not for DD waiver or DHSS waiver services.** "NEMT services are arranged by those programs." So NEMT will not take a person to day habilitation, adult day care, or other waiver services; it will take them to doctors, hospital outpatient, labs, therapy covered by MO HealthNet.
  - Travel standards by county: **Urban: Greene**. **Basic: Christian, Jasper, Newton, Taney**. **Rural: all other counties in our area**. Maximum mileage depends on county type and provider type.
  - Up to 3 legs (2 stops) per day without prior authorization.
  - Attendant and one parent or guardian are covered only for **children under 21**. Adults who need an escort: not addressed (Open question).
  - Public transit will not be assigned if the walk to a stop is more than 4 blocks or 1/2 mile, and in some medical cases.
- How to apply or book: call **(866) 269-5927**; non-urgent trips Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 6 p.m.; urgent trips 24/7. Book at least **2 business days ahead (urban county: Greene)** or **3 business days ahead (basic or rural county: all others in our area)**. MTM mobile app also available. Late ride ("Where's My Ride?"): (866) 269-5944. Complaints to broker: (866) 436-0457; MHD Constituent Services (800) 392-2161.
- Gas reimbursement: if the participant or a family member or friend drives, mileage may be reimbursed at no less than the IRS medical mileage rate; submit within **60 days** of the trip.
- Cost to family: none.
- Where families get stuck: managed care members call the wrong number; waiver services (day programs) excluded; adults needing an escort; rural 3-day notice; rides late.
- Appeal rights: denial, reduction, or stop of NEMT goes to a state fair hearing (manual Section 8.1; detail agent 04). Grievance acknowledgment within 10 business days.
- Screener questions: (1) Has MO HealthNet? (2) Fee-for-service or a managed care plan (check the card)? (3) Is the trip to a medical service (not a waiver day program)? (4) Any free ride available?
- Screener rule: has MO HealthNet (NEMT-eligible ME code) AND trip is to a MO HealthNet-covered medical service AND no free ride THEN if fee-for-service "Call MTM (866) 269-5927" ELSE "Call the number on your health plan card."

### G. Aging caregivers, future planning, and emergencies

### Adult Protective Services (DHSS) and the Adult Abuse and Neglect Hotline
- Run by: DHSS DSDS. "Eligible adult": **18-59 with a disability, or 60 and over**, who is unable to protect their own interests or meet essential needs.
- Hotline **1-800-392-0210**, 7 days a week, 7 a.m. to 8 p.m.; online https://moapss.health.mo.gov/; Relay Missouri 1-800-735-2966.
- What APS does: investigates, then helps arrange personal care, respite, chore, home-delivered meals, financial or legal protection, counseling, referrals; may pursue guardianship or nursing home placement.
- Use in an emergency: when a sole caregiver dies, is hospitalized, or can no longer give care and the adult with a disability cannot meet essential needs. APS can open the door to DSDS services quickly. It is not a placement agency.
- Screener rule: (18 <= age <= 59 AND disability) OR age >= 60, AND caregiver suddenly unable AND person unsafe alone THEN "Call 1-800-392-0210 (and 911 if in danger)."
- Source: [DHSS Stop Adult Abuse](https://health.mo.gov/report-adult-abuse/stop-adult-abuse), checked 2026-10-26. Confidence: High.

### DMH DD emergency pathway (pointer card)
- For a person with DMH DD eligibility: a lost primary caregiver is a crisis criterion for Partnership for Hope, and "immediate need for life-sustaining services (food and shelter, or protection from harm) with no alternative" is an emergency criterion (Priority of Need 5) for waiver access even during the Oct 2026 wait list. Call the support coordinator or the Springfield Regional Office (417-895-7400) or Joplin Satellite (417-629-3020). Detail: agent 01.
- Field point: people **not yet found DD eligible** have no DD emergency door. Get eligibility while the parent is healthy.

### Show-Me Home (Money Follows the Person), DSDS
- For people who have lived in a **skilled nursing facility for at least 60 consecutive days**, on MO HealthNet, who want to move to a home, apartment with own lease, or a residence with no more than 4 unrelated people. RCF and ALF stays do not count. Referral: DSDS HCBS Intake, DSDS staff, SMH contractor, or the Long-Term Care Ombudsman. Relevance: adults with disabilities placed in a nursing home in an emergency can use this route back out. (ICF/IID transitions: agent 01.)
- Source: [HCBS Manual 7.00 Show-Me Home](https://health.mo.gov/providers/manuals/home-and-community-based-services-manual/700-show-me-home), checked 2026-10-26. Confidence: High.

### Future planning supports
- **Missouri Family to Family** (UMKC Institute for Human Development): free statewide resource center; peer support; leads the Missouri **Charting the LifeCourse** chapter (planning tools, including respite planning tools made with ARCH). Phone **1-800-444-0821**; mofamilytofamily.org (DMH presentation Sept 2024 and DHSS resource guide). Serves adults 18+ with IDD and families (2024 DMH slide).
- Legal planning (guardianship, successor or standby guardian, supported decision-making, wills, special needs trusts, letters of intent): agents 04 and 06.
- Planning content a case manager should push: written care manual (routines, meds, behavior supports), list of backup caregivers, DD eligibility and waiver request on file, MO HealthNet active, HCBS assessment done, housing applications filed early, AAA respite for parents 55+.
- Sources: [DMH "DD and You" Family to Family presentation, Sept 2024 (PDF)](https://dmh.mo.gov/sites/dmh/files/media/pdf/2024/10/moddandyoufamilytofamily.pdf); [DHSS MCH resource guide](https://health.mo.gov/seniors/shcn/pdf/mch-resource-guide-birth.pdf); found 2026-10-26 via search (not opened in full). Confidence: Medium.

### Missouri HCBS Waiver Finder (state screening tool)
- DSS runs an online "Missouri HCBS Waiver Finder" that asks residence, MO HealthNet status, primary need (physical or aging, IDD, TBI, dementia, HIV, medical fragility), age, and follow-up questions, then lists likely waivers (MOCDD, Comprehensive, PfH, CSW, BIW, ADW, SFCW, ADCW, AIDS, MCAW, ILW). "For informational and screening purposes only."
- Relevance to our screener: link to it; do not duplicate. It does **not** cover state plan personal care, CDS without a waiver, AAA respite, transport, housing, or AT.
- Source: [DSS Missouri HCBS Waiver Finder](https://dss.mo.gov/mhd/waiver/mo-hcbs-waiver-finder), checked 2026-10-26. Confidence: High.


## 1b. Daily life needs map
(For each need: the programs that apply, the order to try them, and the first call. "Medicaid" means MO HealthNet. Income and Medicaid rules: agent 06. DD eligibility and DD waivers: agent 01.)

### Need 1: Help at home (bathing, dressing, meals, supervision)
- Order:
  1. Get MO HealthNet active (DSDS refuses referrals without it).
  2. DSDS HCBS assessment (18 points). State plan personal care: agency model or CDS. No wait list.
  3. If physical disability and can self-direct: CDS (parent can be paid if NOT legal guardian). If more hours needed and age 18-64: Independent Living Waiver (slot limits).
  4. If DMH DD eligible: DD waiver personal assistant on top of state plan (state plan must be used first). New DD waiver enrollment wait-listed since 15 Oct 2026.
  5. Special cases: MCAW (aged out of MCCY private duty nursing at 21), BIW (TBI, 21-65), SFCW (dementia, lives with caregiver), ADW (63+), ADCW (18-62, licensed adult day center).
- First call: **DSDS HCBS Call Center 866-835-3505** (or online referral). If no Medicaid yet: apply at mydss.mo.gov first (agent 06).

### Need 2: A break for the caregiver (respite)
- Order:
  1. If parent or caregiver is 55+ and person is 18-59: **AAA Family Caregiver Support respite** (no Medicaid needed).
  2. If DMH DD eligible: respite in a DD waiver plan (CSW or Comprehensive; not PfH per agent 01), or county SB40 board funds.
  3. If person is 63+: ADW respite (up to 49 hours a week).
  4. If dementia: Missouri Caregiver Program (Memory Care Home Solutions) and SFCW.
  5. Paid caregiver hours (CDS) can free the parent even if not called "respite."
- First call: **Missouri Senior Resource Line 1-800-235-5503** (routes to the AAA) for parents 55+; otherwise the DD support coordinator.

### Need 3: A place to live
- Order:
  1. Apply early to every voucher list in reach (HACS in Springfield; OACAC for the 10 surrounding counties outside Springfield; Jasper County PHA). Ask for the disability preference and Mainstream vouchers. Keep the mailing address current.
  2. If DMH DD eligible and needs 24-hour or residential support: Comprehensive Waiver request now (wait list; agent 01).
  3. If homeless, institutionalized, or at risk, age 18-61, extremely low income: Missouri 811 referral via DMH or DSS partner; 2-1-1 press 6.
  4. Adults with physical disabilities: own home or apartment with CDS hours; RCF or ALF with DSDS personal care as a group option.
  5. Leaving a nursing facility after 60+ days: Show-Me Home.
  6. Home modifications: waiver (DD or ILW $5,000 per 5 years), AAA supplemental, CIL ramps, Show Me Loans, USDA 504 (rural, verify).
- First call: **the local PHA** (Springfield: HACS 417-866-4329, secondary source) and the DD support coordinator.

### Need 4: Getting around
- Order:
  1. Medical trips with fee-for-service Medicaid: **MTM NEMT (866) 269-5927** (2 business days ahead in Greene; 3 in all other counties of our area). Managed care: number on plan card. Gas reimbursement if family drives.
  2. Springfield, within 3/4 mile of a route: Reduced Fare Card ($0.50 rides) and Transit Ambassador training; if the person cannot use the bus: **Access Express** ($2.00, PCA free).
  3. Rural counties: **OATS Transit** (ask about the AAA contract for disability 18-59 or age 60+).
  4. Waiver day services: transport comes from the waiver or provider, not NEMT.
  5. Work trips: Vocational Rehabilitation (agent 07).
- First call: Springfield: **CU Transit (417) 831-8782**. Rural: **OATS 417-887-9272 / 800-770-6287** (Southwest Region).

### Need 5: Equipment and technology
- Order:
  1. Medical equipment with Medicaid: doctor prescription to a MO HealthNet DME provider (pre-certification for some items; 186 incontinence items a month without pre-certification).
  2. Items Medicaid does not cover: waiver (ILW SME $5,000 per 5 years; DD waiver AT, agent 01), AAA supplemental (parents 55+).
  3. Try before buying: MoAT device loan (5 weeks, free).
  4. Used equipment: MoAT reuse partners (empower: abilities, Springfield; On My Own, Nevada), Swap 'n Shop, CIL loan closets.
  5. Phone or computer access: TAP-T or TAP-I (AGI under $60,000 for 1 or 2 people).
  6. Buy with credit: Show Me Loans.
- First call: **MoAT 1-816-655-6700** (non-medical AT); the prescribing doctor for DME.

### Need 6: Emergency: the caregiver is sick, hospitalized, or dies
- Order:
  1. Immediate danger: 911.
  2. Adult 18-59 with a disability (or 60+) who cannot meet essential needs: **APS hotline 1-800-392-0210** (7 a.m. to 8 p.m., 7 days).
  3. If DMH DD eligible: call the support coordinator or DD Regional Office the same day; ask for an emergency Priority of Need (PON 5) and crisis access; ask about out-of-home respite (agent 01). Springfield Regional Office 417-895-7400; Joplin Satellite 417-629-3020.
  4. If on DSDS services: call 866-835-3505 for a care plan change (more hours; new worker if the paid CDS attendant was the parent).
  5. If a nursing facility stay happens: plan exit with Show-Me Home after 60 days.
  6. Legal authority (who can sign): successor or emergency guardianship, powers of attorney: agent 04.
- First call: **APS 1-800-392-0210** if no one else can step in; **DD support coordinator** if the person is DD eligible.
- Best prevention: a written future plan made while the parent is well (Missouri Family to Family 1-800-444-0821; Charting the LifeCourse).

## 2. Order and dependencies
1. **MO HealthNet comes before any DSDS HCBS.** DSDS "will not accept" a referral if the person is not Medicaid eligible on the referral date (exception: HCB Medicaid routed through FSD). Spenddown must be met first.
2. **DSDS assessment (18 points) comes before** state plan personal care, CDS, ILW, ADW, ADCW, SFCW, BIW.
3. **State plan personal care comes before DD waiver personal assistant** (state plan must be used first for a comparable service).
4. **One waiver at a time.** A person cannot be in a DSDS waiver and a DD waiver at once. Choosing a DD waiver ends the DSDS waiver.
5. **Guardianship decision affects CDS pay.** A legal guardian cannot be the paid CDS attendant. Decide on guardianship versus supported decision-making (agents 04 and 07) knowing this.
6. **DD eligibility before DD respite, DD residential, DD emergency PON.** Get it early; there is no DD crisis door without it (agent 01).
7. **MCCY private duty nursing before MCAW.** Only people who had MCCY private duty nursing before 21 can enter MCAW.
8. **Access Express needs a completed application with professional verification**, then up to 21 days.
9. **NEMT needs a MO HealthNet-covered destination**, fee-for-service status, and 2 or 3 business days notice.
10. **Missouri 811 needs a referral from a DMH or DSS partner**; a family cannot apply directly.
11. **Show-Me Home needs 60 consecutive days in a skilled nursing facility** and Medicaid on the day of discharge.
12. **AAA respite for parents needs the parent to be 55+** and the adult child 18-59.

## 3. Gaps and failure points

1. **The DD waiver wait list (15 Oct 2026) shifts load onto DSDS state plan services, which cannot replace waiver services.** State plan personal care is capped at 60% of the cost maximum ($3,355.99 a month in 2026; 412 agency units or 641 CDS units). It has no day services, no residential option, and no respite for people under 63. Evidence: cost maximum table and waiver cards above; agent 01 for the wait list memo.
2. **No paid respite route for most adults with IDD under 63 who are not on a DD waiver.** DSDS respite is ADW only (63+). Missouri has no Lifespan Respite program and no State Respite Coalition (ARCH map, no Missouri marker). The only general route is AAA Family Caregiver Support, which needs the parent to be 55+ and has limited funds.
3. **Workforce: authorized hours are not delivered hours.** Missouri DD agencies had 46.5% DSP turnover in 2024 (above the 37.1% NCI average), and 28.0% turned away or stopped taking new referrals because of staffing. Average DSP wage $18.07 against a $20.87 living wage (NCI 2024). Rural counties have fewer agencies, longer drives, and no data at county level.
4. **Provider supply frozen at the margin.** New CDS vendor and DME provider enrollment is under a moratorium from 1 August 2026 (6 months, may extend to 1 year). In rural counties with few vendors, a vendor exit cannot be replaced by a new local vendor during the moratorium. (Effect on access is my inference; not yet measured.)
5. **Guardianship and paid family care conflict.** A legal guardian cannot be the paid CDS attendant. Parents are often told to get guardianship at 18 (agents 04, 07) and lose their CDS pay as a result. Only SFCW (dementia) allows a guardian to be paid.
6. **The DSDS level of care tool may under-count supervision needs of people with IDD** who can do physical tasks with prompts. Not proven with data (Open question); field concern. CDS also requires the person to "self-direct," and the legal basis (19 CSR 15-8) centers on physical disability, so many adults with IDD cannot use CDS or ILW (ILW counts cognitive impairment only with onset at or after 22).
7. **Transport cliffs for rural families.** Access Express stops at 3/4 mile from a CU route. OATS rural routes run some towns only 1 to 4 days a month (Wright and Vernon County schedules). NEMT excludes trips to waiver services (day habilitation, adult day care) and covers attendants only for children under 21. Rural counties need 3 business days notice for NEMT.
8. **Housing: tiny targeted supply.** Springfield Housing Authority holds 23 Mainstream vouchers; Jasper County PHA holds 100; OACAC none (HUD 2023). Missouri 811 is a few dozen units statewide and needs an agency referral. DMH RAP is closed through January 2027 and serves behavioral health clients only. DD residential is wait-listed.
9. **Rural service deserts.** Laclede County has no Center for Independent Living on MoAT's map. Our 23 counties are split across 2 DSDS regions, 4 AAAs, 5 CILs, several OATS regions, and many PHAs, so families get passed between offices.
10. **Information is fragmented and some official pages are stale.** DSS pages dated 2014 and 2015 still name "LogistiCare" as NEMT broker, while the 2026 NEMT Manual names MTM Health. The DHSS site moved in 2025-2026 and old URLs return 404. CU's fare notice has a confusing effective date. The state's own Waiver Finder covers waivers only.
11. **Emergency placement has no clear front door for adults who are not DD eligible or not on a waiver.** APS can investigate and arrange services, but it is not a placement agency. The default fallback is often a nursing facility, then Show-Me Home after 60 days.
12. **Age cliffs at 21.** MCCY private duty nursing ends (MCAW only if PDN was in place), DME coverage narrows (several items covered only to age 20), and NEMT attendants stop. Transition planning must start by 19 or 20 (agent 07).
